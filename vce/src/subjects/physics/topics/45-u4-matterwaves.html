De Broglie's hypothesis

In 1924 Louis de Broglie reasoned that if waves (light) can behave like particles, then particles might behave like waves. He proposed that every moving particle has a wavelength given by the same relationship as for photons:

de Broglie wavelength
\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv} \quad (v \ll c) \]

Faster or heavier particles have shorter wavelengths.

Find the de Broglie wavelength of (a) an electron moving at \(2.0\times10^6\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) and (b) a 0.16 kg cricket ball bowled at 40 m s⁻¹. Comment on whether each would show diffraction.

(a) \(\lambda = \dfrac{h}{mv} = \dfrac{6.63\times10^{-34}}{(9.1\times10^{-31})(2.0\times10^6)} = 3.6\times10^{-10}\ \text{m}\).

This is similar to the spacing of atoms in a crystal (~\(10^{-10}\) m), so a crystal diffracts electrons strongly (\(\lambda/w \approx 1\)).

(b) \(\lambda = \dfrac{6.63\times10^{-34}}{0.16 \times 40} = 1.0\times10^{-34}\ \text{m}\).

Nothing has gaps anywhere near that small (a proton is about \(10^{-15}\) m), so \(\lambda/w\) is effectively zero and no wave behaviour can ever be observed.

(a) 0.36 nm, diffracts through crystals (b) \(10^{-34}\) m, no observable diffraction. All matter has a wavelength; it only matters for tiny masses.

Evidence: electron diffraction

In 1927, Davisson and Germer fired electrons at a nickel crystal and G. P. Thomson fired electrons through thin metal foil. Both saw diffraction patterns: electrons came off only in particular directions, forming spots or rings exactly like the patterns X-rays make with the same crystals. The measured wavelengths matched \(\lambda = h/p\).

Diffraction and interference are wave properties. Electrons (which clearly behave as particles in other experiments: they have mass and charge, and hit screens at single points) producing diffraction patterns is direct evidence that matter has wave-like properties. This is wave–particle duality for matter.

Accelerated electrons

Electrons in diffraction experiments and electron microscopes are accelerated through a voltage \(V\), gaining \(E_k = eV\). To find their wavelength, get the momentum from the kinetic energy:

\[ E_k = \frac{p^2}{2m} \Rightarrow p = \sqrt{2mE_k} = \sqrt{2meV} \qquad \lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}} \]

Electrons are accelerated from rest through 150 V. Find their de Broglie wavelength.

Kinetic energy: \(E_k = eV = (1.60\times10^{-19})(150) = 2.4\times10^{-17}\ \text{J}\).

Momentum: \(p = \sqrt{2mE_k} = \sqrt{2(9.1\times10^{-31})(2.4\times10^{-17})} = 6.6\times10^{-24}\ \text{kg m s}^{-1}\).

Wavelength: \(\lambda = h/p = 6.63\times10^{-34}/6.6\times10^{-24} = 1.0\times10^{-10}\ \text{m}\).

0.10 nm, about one atomic spacing, which is why a few hundred volts is ideal for electron diffraction by crystals. Increasing the voltage shortens the wavelength (\(\lambda \propto 1/\sqrt{V}\)), so the rings get smaller.

Comparing electron and X-ray diffraction

If an electron beam and an X-ray beam produce identical diffraction patterns from the same crystal, they have the same wavelength, and so (from \(\lambda = h/p\)) the same momentum. Their energies, however, are very different:

\[ \text{photon: } E = pc \qquad\qquad \text{electron: } E_k = \frac{p^2}{2m} \]

X-rays of wavelength 0.10 nm and an electron beam produce identical diffraction patterns from a metal foil. Find (a) the momentum of each, (b) the energy of each X-ray photon and (c) the kinetic energy of each electron. Give energies in eV.

(a) Same pattern → same λ → same momentum: \(p = h/\lambda = 6.63\times10^{-34}/1.0\times10^{-10} = 6.63\times10^{-24}\ \text{kg m s}^{-1}\) for both.

(b) Photon: \(E = pc = (6.63\times10^{-24})(3.0\times10^8) = 1.99\times10^{-15}\ \text{J} = 1.24\times10^4\ \text{eV}\) (12.4 keV).

(c) Electron: \(E_k = \dfrac{p^2}{2m} = \dfrac{(6.63\times10^{-24})^2}{2(9.1\times10^{-31})} = 2.4\times10^{-17}\ \text{J} = 151\ \text{eV}\).

Same momentum (\(6.6\times10^{-24}\ \text{kg m s}^{-1}\)); the photon has about 12 keV but the electron only about 150 eV. This is why electron microscopes can image delicate samples: the same resolving wavelength with far less energy.

An electron and a photon with the same energy have different momenta and wavelengths, so different patterns. The diffraction pattern depends on wavelength, which depends on momentum. Also, never use \(E = hc/\lambda\) for an electron: that formula is only for photons.

Practice

An electron beam and an X-ray beam produce identical diffraction patterns when directed at the same crystal. The electrons and X-ray photons must have the same:

  1. momentum
  2. energy
  3. speed
  4. frequency

Identical patterns mean equal wavelengths. \(\lambda = h/p\) for both, so the momenta are equal. Their energies (and speeds) differ.

The accelerating voltage in an electron diffraction experiment is increased. The diffraction rings on the screen:

  1. get larger, because the electrons have more energy
  2. stay the same size
  3. get smaller, because the electrons' wavelength decreases
  4. disappear, because the electrons behave as particles

Higher voltage → more momentum → shorter wavelength → smaller \(\lambda/w\) → less spreading, so the rings shrink.

An electron and a proton move at the same speed. Compared with the electron's de Broglie wavelength, the proton's is:

  1. the same
  2. 1835 times longer
  3. √1835 times shorter
  4. 1835 times shorter

\(\lambda = h/(mv)\). Same \(v\), 1835 times the mass → 1835 times the momentum → wavelength 1835 times shorter.

The main reason we never observe diffraction of a thrown tennis ball through a doorway is that:

  1. tennis balls are particles and have no wavelength
  2. its de Broglie wavelength is far too small compared with the doorway
  3. it moves too slowly
  4. it has no charge

All moving matter has a wavelength. For a tennis ball it's about \(10^{-34}\) m, so \(\lambda/w\) is effectively zero.

What speed must an electron have for its de Broglie wavelength to equal 500 nm (green light)?

\(v = \dfrac{h}{m\lambda} = \dfrac{6.63\times10^{-34}}{(9.1\times10^{-31})(5.0\times10^{-7})} = 1.5\times10^3\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).

Electrons in an electron microscope are accelerated through 20 kV. Find their de Broglie wavelength (ignore relativity) and explain why this microscope can resolve much finer detail than a light microscope.

\(p = \sqrt{2meV} = \sqrt{2(9.1\times10^{-31})(1.60\times10^{-19})(2.0\times10^4)} = 7.6\times10^{-23}\ \text{kg m s}^{-1}\).

\(\lambda = h/p = 8.7\times10^{-12}\ \text{m}\) (0.0087 nm).

This is about 60 000 times shorter than visible light, so \(\lambda/w\) is tiny even for very small structures: diffraction blurring is negligible and much finer detail is resolved.

Explain how electron diffraction experiments provide evidence for the wave nature of matter.

When a beam of electrons passes through a thin crystal or foil, it produces a pattern of rings (or spots), just like the pattern produced by X-rays. [1]

Diffraction is a wave phenomenon that occurs when the wavelength is comparable to the spacing of the atoms (\(\lambda/w \approx 1\)). [1]

The wavelength calculated from the ring pattern matches the de Broglie wavelength \(\lambda = h/p\), showing electrons behave as waves. [1]

Summary

  • \(\lambda = h/p = h/mv\); for accelerated electrons \(p = \sqrt{2meV}\).
  • Electron diffraction (rings like X-rays) is evidence for matter waves.
  • Same pattern ⇔ same wavelength ⇔ same momentum. Photon \(E = pc\); electron \(E_k = p^2/2m\).
