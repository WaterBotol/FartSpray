What a transformer does

A transformer changes the size of an AC voltage. It has two coils, the primary (input) and the secondary (output), wound on a shared iron core. There is no electrical connection between the coils: energy is transferred entirely through the changing magnetic field.

How it works (the induction chain)

  1. An alternating current in the primary coil produces an alternating (changing) magnetic field in the iron core.
  2. The core carries this changing flux through the secondary coil.
  3. The changing flux through the secondary induces an alternating emf in it (Faraday's law: \(\varepsilon = -N\Delta\Phi/\Delta t\)).
  4. Each turn of either coil experiences the same flux change, so the voltage per turn is the same in both coils. More turns on the secondary → more voltage.

“Explain how a transformer works” needs all the links: AC in primary → changing magnetic field/flux in the core → changing flux through the secondary → induced emf in the secondary (Faraday's law). Missing the word changing is the most common way to lose the mark.

The ideal transformer equations

Ideal transformer (subscript 1 = primary, 2 = secondary)
\[ \frac{N_1}{N_2} = \frac{V_1}{V_2} = \frac{I_2}{I_1} \]

An ideal transformer loses no energy, so \(P_{\text{in}} = P_{\text{out}}\): \(V_1I_1 = V_2I_2\). Note the current ratio is upside down compared with the voltage ratio.

  • Step-up (\(N_2 > N_1\)): voltage goes up, current goes down by the same factor.
  • Step-down (\(N_2 < N_1\)): voltage goes down, current goes up.
  • Power is never “stepped up”. A transformer trades voltage for current.

A phone charger contains a transformer that steps 230 V rms mains down to 5.0 V rms. Its primary has 2300 turns. (a) How many turns are on the secondary? (b) If the phone draws 2.0 A, what current flows in the primary? Assume the transformer is ideal.

A transformer's voltage ratio equals its turns ratio. An ideal transformer loses no energy, so power in equals power out: stepping the voltage down steps the current up by the same factor.

(a) Turns ratio equals voltage ratio:

\[ \frac{N_1}{N_2} = \frac{V_1}{V_2} \Rightarrow N_2 = 2300 \times \frac{5.0}{230} = 50\ \text{turns} \]

(b) Power in = power out: \(P = V_2I_2 = 5.0 \times 2.0 = 10\ \text{W}\).

\[ I_1 = \frac{P}{V_1} = \frac{10}{230} = 0.043\ \text{A} \]

(a) 50 turns (b) 0.043 A (43 mA). Check with the current ratio: \(I_1 = I_2 \times N_2/N_1 = 2.0 \times 50/2300 = 0.043\ \text{A}\) ✓.

A power station generator produces 22 kV. A transformer steps this up to 500 kV for transmission. If the generator supplies 1000 A, what current flows in the transmission line?

Because power is conserved in an ideal transformer, raising the voltage lowers the current by the same factor. That's why power is transmitted at very high voltage.

Voltage ratio: \(V_2/V_1 = 500/22 = 22.7\).

Current goes down by the same factor: \(I_2 = 1000 \times \dfrac{22}{500} = 44\ \text{A}\).

44 A. The power (22 MW) is unchanged; the much smaller current is the whole point (see Transmission).

Why transformers need AC

A steady DC current in the primary makes a constant magnetic field, so the flux through the secondary doesn't change and no emf is induced. The only output is a brief pulse at the moment DC is switched on or off (when the current, and flux, changes). A 12 V battery connected to a transformer does not produce a stepped-up DC voltage.

Real transformers

Real transformers are very efficient (often over 95%) but not perfect:

  • Resistance of the coils heats the wire (\(I^2R\)).
  • Eddy currents induced in the iron core heat it. Making the core from thin insulated laminations blocks the eddy-current paths.
  • Flux leakage: some flux doesn't pass through both coils.

Unless told otherwise, treat VCE transformers as ideal.

Practice

A 12 V DC battery is connected to the primary of a transformer with a turns ratio of 1:10 (step-up). After the connection is made, the steady secondary voltage is:

  1. 120 V DC
  2. 1.2 V DC
  3. 120 V AC
  4. 0 V

DC produces a constant flux, so there's no change in flux through the secondary and no induced emf (apart from a momentary spike at switch-on).

An ideal transformer has 500 primary turns and 100 secondary turns. The primary voltage is 240 V rms. The secondary voltage is:

  1. 1200 V
  2. 48 V
  3. 240 V
  4. 24 V

\(V_2 = V_1 \times N_2/N_1 = 240 \times 100/500 = 48\ \text{V}\).

In an ideal step-up transformer, compared with the primary, the secondary has:

  1. higher voltage and higher power
  2. higher voltage and higher current
  3. higher voltage and lower current, with the same power
  4. the same voltage and lower current

Power is conserved: if voltage goes up by some factor, current goes down by the same factor.

Transformer cores are laminated (made from thin insulated sheets) in order to:

  1. reduce energy losses from eddy currents
  2. increase the number of turns
  3. allow DC to pass through
  4. increase the output voltage

The insulation between sheets breaks up the loops in which eddy currents would flow, reducing heating of the core.

A model train set needs 16 V rms from 230 V rms mains, drawing 1.5 A. For an ideal transformer, find the turns ratio \(N_1:N_2\) and the primary current.

\(N_1/N_2 = 230/16 = 14.4\), so about 14.4 : 1.

\(P = 16 \times 1.5 = 24\ \text{W}\); \(I_1 = 24/230 = 0.10\ \text{A}\).

A transformer's primary is connected to 230 V rms. Its secondary supplies 11.5 V rms to a 4.6 Ω resistor. The transformer is 92% efficient. Find the primary current.

Output current: \(I_2 = 11.5/4.6 = 2.5\ \text{A}\); output power \(= 11.5 \times 2.5 = 28.75\ \text{W}\).

Input power \(= 28.75/0.92 = 31.25\ \text{W}\).

\(I_1 = 31.25/230 = 0.136\ \text{A}\) (compared with 0.125 A if it were ideal).

Explain how an alternating voltage in the primary coil of a transformer produces an alternating voltage in the secondary coil, even though the coils are not electrically connected.

The alternating current in the primary produces a continuously changing magnetic field (flux) in the iron core. [1]

The core channels this changing flux through the secondary coil. [1]

By Faraday's law, a changing flux through the secondary induces an emf in it (\(\varepsilon = -N\Delta\Phi/\Delta t\)); as the flux alternates, so does the induced voltage. [1]

Summary

  • \(N_1/N_2 = V_1/V_2 = I_2/I_1\); ideal means \(P_{\text{in}} = P_{\text{out}}\).
  • Transformers need a changing flux, so AC only.
  • Step-up raises voltage and lowers current; power is unchanged.
  • Losses: coil resistance, eddy currents (reduced by laminations), flux leakage.
