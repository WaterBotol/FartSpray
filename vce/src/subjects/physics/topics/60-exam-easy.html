Section A – Multiple-choice questions

Answer all questions. Choose the response that is correct or best answers the question. A correct answer scores 1; an incorrect answer scores 0. No marks are deducted for incorrect answers.

A 1200 kg car accelerates uniformly from rest to \(20\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) in 8.0 s. The net force on the car is closest to

  1. 1500 N
  2. 2400 N
  3. 3000 N
  4. 9600 N

\(a = \frac{20}{8.0} = 2.5\ \text{m s}^{-2}\), so \(F = ma = 1200 \times 2.5 = 3000\ \text{N}\).

Well handled overall. A (1500 N) comes from halving the acceleration as though using an average velocity.

A book rests on a table. According to Newton’s third law, the partner of the gravitational force of the Earth on the book is the

  1. normal force of the table on the book
  2. force of the book on the table
  3. gravitational force of the book on the Earth
  4. weight of the table

Third-law pairs act on different objects and are the same type of force. Earth pulls the book gravitationally, so the book pulls Earth gravitationally.

The normal force (A) balances the weight, but it’s not its third-law partner: it’s a different type of force acting on the same object. This is the most common error on this idea.

A ball on a string moves in a horizontal circle of radius 0.50 m at a constant speed of \(3.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}\). The magnitude of its acceleration is

  1. \(0\ \text{m s}^{-2}\)
  2. \(6.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}\)
  3. \(18\ \text{m s}^{-2}\)
  4. \(36\ \text{m s}^{-2}\)

\(a = \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{9.0}{0.50} = 18\ \text{m s}^{-2}\), directed towards the centre.

A (zero) reflects the misconception that constant speed means no acceleration. The direction of the velocity changes, so there is a centripetal acceleration.

A ball is thrown horizontally from the top of a cliff. Ignoring air resistance, during its flight the horizontal component of its velocity

  1. increases
  2. decreases
  3. stays constant
  4. is zero at the highest point

No horizontal force acts, so the horizontal velocity doesn’t change. Only the vertical component changes (at \(g\) downwards).

A 2.0 kg trolley moving at \(3.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) collides with a stationary 1.0 kg trolley and they stick together. Their speed after the collision is

  1. \(1.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}\)
  2. \(1.5\ \text{m s}^{-1}\)
  3. \(2.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}\)
  4. \(3.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}\)

Momentum is conserved: \(2.0 \times 3.0 = 3.0\,v\), so \(v = 2.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).

B (1.5) comes from dividing the speed by the number of trolleys rather than conserving momentum.

A spring with spring constant \(200\ \text{N m}^{-1}\) is compressed by 0.10 m. The elastic potential energy stored is

  1. 1.0 J
  2. 2.0 J
  3. 10 J
  4. 20 J

\(E = \tfrac12 kx^2 = \tfrac12 \times 200 \times 0.10^2 = 1.0\ \text{J}\).

B (2.0 J) omits the \(\tfrac12\); D uses \(kx\) (a force) as though it were an energy.

At the surface of the Earth the gravitational field strength is \(g\). At a distance of two Earth radii from the Earth’s centre, the field strength is

  1. \(\frac{g}{2}\)
  2. \(\frac{g}{4}\)
  3. \(2g\)
  4. \(4g\)

\(g \propto \frac{1}{r^2}\). Doubling \(r\) from the centre divides the field strength by 4.

Remember that \(r\) is measured from the centre, so “two Earth radii from the centre” is one Earth radius above the surface.

A satellite is in a stable circular orbit around the Earth. Which one of the following is correct?

  1. No forces act on the satellite.
  2. The gravitational force on the satellite is the net force and points towards the Earth’s centre.
  3. The satellite moves with constant velocity.
  4. The satellite’s acceleration is zero.

Gravity is the only force and supplies the centripetal force. The speed is constant, but the velocity (direction) keeps changing, so there is an acceleration.

Two parallel plates 0.020 m apart have a potential difference of 100 V between them. The strength of the uniform electric field between the plates is

  1. \(2.0\ \text{V m}^{-1}\)
  2. \(50\ \text{V m}^{-1}\)
  3. \(2000\ \text{V m}^{-1}\)
  4. \(5000\ \text{V m}^{-1}\)

\(E = \frac{V}{d} = \frac{100}{0.020} = 5000\ \text{V m}^{-1}\).

A comes from multiplying instead of dividing. Always check the units: V divided by m.

A straight wire 0.20 m long carries a current of 3.0 A at right angles to a uniform magnetic field of 0.50 T. The magnitude of the force on the wire is

  1. 0.030 N
  2. 0.30 N
  3. 3.0 N
  4. 30 N

\(F = IlB = 3.0 \times 0.20 \times 0.50 = 0.30\ \text{N}\).

In a DC motor, the function of the split-ring commutator is to

  1. convert the DC supply into AC for the coil
  2. reverse the direction of the current in the coil every half turn
  3. increase the strength of the magnetic field
  4. keep the current in the coil flowing in one direction at all times

Reversing the current every half turn keeps the torque in the same rotational direction, so the coil keeps turning.

D is the reverse of what happens: the external supply is constant, but the current in the coil reverses each half turn.

A loop of area \(0.020\ \text{m}^2\) is placed perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of 0.40 T. The magnetic flux through the loop is

  1. \(8.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{Wb}\)
  2. \(2.0 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{Wb}\)
  3. \(5.0 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{Wb}\)
  4. \(20\ \text{Wb}\)

\(\Phi = BA = 0.40 \times 0.020 = 8.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{Wb}\).

The north pole of a bar magnet is pushed towards one end of a coil connected in a closed circuit. The end of the coil nearest the magnet becomes

  1. a north pole
  2. a south pole
  3. neither pole, because no current flows
  4. a north pole only if the magnet is held still

By Lenz’s law the induced current opposes the change (flux increasing), so the near end becomes a north pole, repelling the approaching north pole.

B was a popular wrong answer: “opposites attract” thinking. The coil opposes the motion, so it must repel.

A mains supply has a peak voltage of 340 V. Its RMS voltage is closest to

  1. 170 V
  2. 240 V
  3. 340 V
  4. 480 V

\(V_{\text{RMS}} = \frac{V_p}{\sqrt2} = \frac{340}{1.41} \approx 240\ \text{V}\).

An ideal transformer has 400 turns on its primary coil and 20 turns on its secondary coil. The primary is connected to 240 V AC. The secondary voltage is

  1. 6.0 V
  2. 12 V
  3. 24 V
  4. 4800 V

\(\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}\): \(V_s = 240 \times \frac{20}{400} = 12\ \text{V}\).

D inverts the turns ratio. Check that a step-down transformer (fewer secondary turns) gives a smaller voltage.

Electricity is transmitted over long distances at very high voltages mainly because

  1. high voltages travel faster along the wires
  2. for the same power the current is smaller, so less power is lost as heat
  3. high voltage increases the resistance of the lines
  4. transformers only work at high voltages

\(P = VI\): for the same power, higher \(V\) means lower \(I\), and the power lost is \(I^2R\).

In a Young’s double-slit experiment, a bright fringe appears where the path difference from the two slits is

  1. half a wavelength
  2. a whole number of wavelengths
  3. an odd number of half-wavelengths
  4. exactly zero only

Constructive interference: path difference \(= n\lambda\), \(n = 0, 1, 2, \dots\). Zero is just the central bright band.

Light with a frequency above the threshold frequency shines on a metal. If the intensity of the light is increased, the

  1. maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons increases
  2. number of photoelectrons emitted each second increases
  3. threshold frequency decreases
  4. stopping voltage increases

More intense light means more photons per second, so more electrons are ejected per second. Each photon still has the same energy, so the maximum kinetic energy (and the stopping voltage) doesn’t change.

A reflects the wave-model prediction, which the photoelectric effect disproves. Expect this distinction to be tested every year.

An electron in an atom drops from an energy level of −1.5 eV to one of −3.4 eV. The energy of the emitted photon is

  1. 1.5 eV
  2. 1.9 eV
  3. 3.4 eV
  4. 4.9 eV

\(E = -1.5 - (-3.4) = 1.9\ \text{eV}\).

D adds the magnitudes. The photon carries the difference between the levels.

Einstein’s second postulate of special relativity states that

  1. the laws of physics are the same in all frames of reference
  2. the speed of light in a vacuum is the same for all observers in inertial frames of reference
  3. moving clocks run slow
  4. mass and energy are equivalent

The first postulate is about the laws of physics in inertial frames; the second is the constancy of the speed of light. Time dilation and \(E = mc^2\) are consequences, not postulates.

Answer all questions in the spaces provided. Show working for questions worth more than 1 mark, and give answers with units and appropriate significant figures.

A 5.0 kg box slides down a smooth (frictionless) ramp inclined at \(30^\circ\) to the horizontal.

Name the two forces acting on the box and state the direction of each.

  • Weight (gravitational force) acting vertically downwards
  • Normal force acting perpendicular to the ramp surface, away from it

Weight, \(mg\), vertically down; normal force, perpendicular to the surface of the ramp.

Many answers listed a “force down the slope”. That is a component of the weight, not a separate force, so it doesn’t earn a mark.

Calculate the acceleration of the box down the ramp.

  • \(a = g\sin\theta\) (or \(F = mg\sin\theta\) then \(a = F/m\))
  • \(a = 4.9\ \text{m s}^{-2}\)

\(a = g\sin 30^\circ = 9.8 \times 0.5 = 4.9\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) down the ramp.

Calculate the magnitude of the normal force on the box.

  • \(N = mg\cos\theta\)
  • \(N = 42\ \text{N}\) (42.4 N)

\(N = mg\cos 30^\circ = 5.0 \times 9.8 \times 0.866 = 42\ \text{N}\).

Using \(\sin\) instead of \(\cos\) was the main error. Check your answer is less than the weight (49 N).

Two blocks on a smooth horizontal surface are joined by a light string. A horizontal force of 15 N pulls the 3.0 kg block, which pulls the 2.0 kg block behind it.

Calculate the acceleration of the blocks.

  • Uses the total mass: \(a = \frac{F}{m_1 + m_2}\)
  • \(a = 3.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}\)

\(a = \frac{15}{3.0 + 2.0} = 3.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}\).

Calculate the tension in the string.

  • The string provides the net force on the 2.0 kg block: \(T = m_2 a\)
  • \(T = 6.0\ \text{N}\)

The string is the only horizontal force on the 2.0 kg block: \(T = 2.0 \times 3.0 = 6.0\ \text{N}\).

Some answers gave 15 N. The string only has to accelerate the rear block, so the tension is less than the applied force.

A ball is kicked from level ground at \(20\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) at \(30^\circ\) above the horizontal. Ignore air resistance.

Calculate the initial horizontal and vertical components of the velocity.

  • \(v_x = 20\cos 30^\circ = 17.3\ \text{m s}^{-1}\)
  • \(v_y = 20\sin 30^\circ = 10.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}\)

\(v_x = 17.3\ \text{m s}^{-1}\), \(v_y = 10.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).

Calculate the time taken to reach maximum height.

  • \(t = \frac{v_y}{g} = 1.0\ \text{s}\) (1.02 s)

At the top \(v_y = 0\): \(t = \frac{10.0}{9.8} = 1.02\ \text{s}\).

Calculate the maximum height reached.

  • \(0 = v_y^2 - 2gh\) or equivalent
  • \(h = 5.1\ \text{m}\)

\(h = \frac{v_y^2}{2g} = \frac{100}{19.6} = 5.1\ \text{m}\).

Calculate the horizontal distance travelled before the ball lands.

  • Total flight time is twice the time to the top (2.04 s)
  • Range \(= v_x t = 35\ \text{m}\)

\(t = 2 \times 1.02 = 2.04\ \text{s}\), so range \(= 17.3 \times 2.04 = 35\ \text{m}\).

Using the time to the top only (giving about 18 m) was the most common error.

A 1000 kg car travels around a flat, horizontal curve of radius 50 m at a constant speed of \(15\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).

Calculate the magnitude of the net force on the car.

  • \(F = \frac{mv^2}{r}\)
  • \(F = 4.5 \times 10^3\ \text{N}\)

\(F = \frac{1000 \times 15^2}{50} = 4500\ \text{N}\) towards the centre of the curve.

Identify the force that provides this net force.

  • Friction between the tyres and the road (sideways static friction)

Sideways (static) friction from the road on the tyres.

The maximum sideways friction the road can provide is 6000 N. Calculate the maximum speed at which the car can take the curve.

  • \(6000 = \frac{1000 v^2}{50}\)
  • \(v = 17\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) (17.3)

\(v = \sqrt{\frac{6000 \times 50}{1000}} = \sqrt{300} = 17\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).

A 0.15 kg cricket ball travelling at \(30\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) is hit straight back along its path at \(40\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).

Calculate the magnitude of the change in momentum of the ball.

  • Treats the velocities as opposite in sign
  • \(\Delta p = 10.5\ \text{kg m s}^{-1}\)

\(\Delta p = 0.15 \times (40 - (-30)) = 10.5\ \text{kg m s}^{-1}\).

Answers of 1.5 kg m s⁻¹ ignored the reversal of direction. Always take one direction as positive.

State the impulse given to the ball.

  • \(10.5\ \text{N s}\) (same as the change in momentum), away from the bat

Impulse = change in momentum = \(10.5\ \text{N s}\).

The ball is in contact with the bat for 5.0 ms. Calculate the average force on the ball.

  • \(F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}\) with \(\Delta t = 5.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{s}\)
  • \(F = 2.1 \times 10^3\ \text{N}\)

\(F = \frac{10.5}{0.0050} = 2100\ \text{N}\).

Explain how the padding in a cricket helmet reduces the force on a player’s head when hit by a ball.

  • The change in momentum (impulse) of the ball is fixed
  • Padding increases the collision time, so the average force \(F = \Delta p / \Delta t\) is smaller

The ball’s change in momentum is the same either way. The padding compresses and increases the time over which it stops, and since \(F = \Delta p/\Delta t\), the average force is smaller.

Full marks required both the fixed change in momentum and the increased time. “The padding absorbs the force” earned nothing.

A 0.20 kg toy car is launched along a smooth track by a spring of spring constant \(400\ \text{N m}^{-1}\) compressed by 5.0 cm.

Calculate the elastic potential energy stored in the spring.

In practice the car reaches a lower height. Give one reason.

  • Some energy is transformed into thermal energy (and sound) by friction or air resistance

Friction and air resistance transform some of the energy into thermal energy, so less is available as gravitational potential energy.

A space station orbits the Earth in a circular orbit 400 km above the surface. \(R_E = 6.37 \times 10^6\ \text{m}\), \(M_E = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\ \text{kg}\).

Calculate the gravitational field strength at the height of the station.

  • \(T = 5.5 \times 10^3\ \text{s} \approx 92\ \text{min}\)

\(T = \frac{2\pi \times 6.77 \times 10^6}{7.67 \times 10^3} = 5.5 \times 10^3\ \text{s} \approx 92\ \text{minutes}\).

Astronauts on the station feel weightless, although gravity still acts on them. Explain why.

  • The astronauts and the station are both in free fall, accelerating towards Earth at the same rate (the local \(g\))
  • So there is no normal (contact) force on the astronauts, and apparent weight is the normal force

The station and the astronauts fall around the Earth together with the same acceleration \(g\). The floor doesn’t push on them, and since we feel our weight through the normal force, they feel weightless.

Answers that said “there is no gravity in space” scored zero; part a shows the field is still 8.7 N kg⁻¹.

State the direction of the electric force on the electron compared with the direction of the electric field.

Opposite to the field direction, because the electron is negatively charged.

Explain why a commutator is needed for the coil to keep rotating in the same direction.

A coil of 200 turns, each of area \(0.010\ \text{m}^2\), sits perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of 0.50 T. The field is reduced steadily to zero in 0.25 s.

  • So its magnetic field is in the same direction as the original field (trying to maintain the flux)

  • A second, different valid way

A small power station supplies 100 kW at 2000 V (RMS) to a town through transmission lines with a total resistance of 4.0 Ω.

Calculate the current in the transmission lines.

\(I = \frac{100\,000}{20\,000} = 5.0\ \text{A}\), so \(P_{\text{loss}} = 25 \times 4.0 = 100\ \text{W}\): 100 times smaller.

Explain why AC rather than DC is used in this system.

A transformer works by the changing current in the primary producing a changing flux, which induces an EMF in the secondary. DC gives a constant flux and no induced EMF, so only AC can be stepped up for transmission.

Explain why the existence of a threshold frequency supports the particle (photon) model of light rather than the wave model.

  • Wave model: energy depends on intensity, so any frequency should eject electrons if the light is bright enough (or after a delay)
  • Photon model: each electron absorbs one photon of energy \(hf\); below \(f_0\) no single photon has enough energy, however intense the light

In the wave model, energy arrives continuously and depends on intensity, so bright enough light of any frequency should eventually free electrons. Experiment shows no emission below \(f_0\) however bright the light. In the photon model each electron absorbs one photon, so emission depends on \(hf\) exceeding the work function.
