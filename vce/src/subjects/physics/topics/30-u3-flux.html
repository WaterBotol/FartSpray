Magnetic flux

Magnetic flux \(\Phi_B\) measures how much magnetic field passes through an area. Picture it as the number of field lines threading a loop.

Magnetic flux
\[ \Phi_B = B_\perp A \]

\(B_\perp\) = the component of the magnetic field perpendicular to the area (T); \(A\) = area (m²). Unit: weber (Wb) = T m². (\(B\) is sometimes called flux density: flux per square metre.)

  • Plane of the loop perpendicular to \(B\) (field goes straight through): maximum flux, \(\Phi = BA\).
  • Loop tilted: only the perpendicular component counts, so the flux is less.
  • Plane of the loop parallel to \(B\) (field skims along it): zero flux.

Read carefully whether the question says the plane of the coil or the normal / axis of the coil is perpendicular to the field. Plane perpendicular to \(B\) → maximum flux. Axis (normal) perpendicular to \(B\) → the plane is parallel, so zero flux.

A rectangular loop 20 cm × 10 cm sits with its plane perpendicular to a uniform 0.50 T field. Find the flux through it. What is the flux after it rotates 90°?

Area: \(A = 0.20 \times 0.10 = 0.020\ \text{m}^2\). Flux: \(\Phi = BA = 0.50 \times 0.020 = 0.010\ \text{Wb}\).

After 90° the plane is parallel to the field, so \(\Phi = 0\).

\(1.0\times10^{-2}\ \text{Wb}\), then 0 Wb. Convert cm to m before multiplying: 20 cm × 10 cm = 200 cm² = 0.020 m².

Electromagnetic induction

An emf (a voltage) is induced in a coil whenever the magnetic flux through it changes. If the coil is part of a complete circuit, the emf drives an induced current. A steady flux, however large, induces nothing.

The flux \(\Phi = B_\perp A\) can change in three ways:

  1. Change \(B\): move a magnet towards or away from the coil, or switch an electromagnet on or off.
  2. Change \(A\): move the coil into or out of a field, or stretch/squash the loop.
  3. Change the angle: rotate the coil in the field (this is how generators work).

Faraday's law

Faraday's law of induction
\[ \varepsilon = -N\frac{\Delta\Phi_B}{\Delta t} \]

\(\varepsilon\) = average induced emf (V), \(N\) = number of turns in the coil, \(\Delta\Phi_B/\Delta t\) = rate of change of flux through one turn (Wb s⁻¹). The minus sign expresses Lenz's law (the direction opposes the change; see the next page). For magnitudes, drop it.

To get a bigger induced emf: more turns, a bigger change in flux (stronger field, larger area) or a faster change (shorter time).

A 200-turn coil of area \(4.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2\) is perpendicular to a 0.60 T magnetic field. The field is switched off, falling to zero in 0.050 s. Find the average emf induced in the coil.

Change in flux through one turn:

\[ \Delta\Phi = \Phi_f - \Phi_i = 0 - (0.60)(4.0\times10^{-3}) = -2.4\times10^{-3}\ \text{Wb} \]

Faraday's law:

\[ |\varepsilon| = N\frac{|\Delta\Phi|}{\Delta t} = 200 \times \frac{2.4\times10^{-3}}{0.050} = 9.6\ \text{V} \]

9.6 V. Forgetting \(N\) (giving 0.048 V) is the most common error.

A 50-turn coil of area 0.010 m² is rotated from a position where its plane is perpendicular to a 0.40 T field to a position where its plane is parallel to the field, in 0.020 s. Find the average emf.

Flux change: from \(BA = 0.40 \times 0.010 = 4.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{Wb}\) to 0.

\[ |\varepsilon| = 50 \times \frac{4.0\times10^{-3}}{0.020} = 10\ \text{V} \]

10 V (average over the quarter turn). This “quarter turn” calculation is how VCE calculates the average emf of a generator.

A single square loop of side 10 cm moves at a constant 2.0 m s⁻¹ into a region of uniform 0.50 T field perpendicular to the loop. Find the emf while it is entering. What is the emf once the loop is completely inside the field?

Time to enter completely: \(\Delta t = 0.10/2.0 = 0.050\ \text{s}\).

Flux change: from 0 to \(BA = 0.50 \times 0.010 = 5.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{Wb}\). \(\varepsilon = 5.0\times10^{-3}/0.050 = 0.10\ \text{V}\).

Fully inside: the flux is constant (still \(5.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{Wb}\)) even though the loop is moving, so \(\varepsilon = 0\).

0.10 V while entering; 0 V while completely inside; 0.10 V (opposite direction) while leaving.

From flux–time graphs to emf–time graphs

The emf at any moment is \(-N\) times the gradient of the flux–time graph. Straight sections give constant emf; flat sections give zero; steeper sections give bigger emf; a change in the sign of the gradient flips the emf's sign.

The emf depends on how fast the flux is changing, not on how big it is. Between 2 s and 3 s the flux is at its maximum but the emf is zero. In a rotating generator coil, the emf is largest when the flux is zero (and changing fastest).

Practice

A strong magnet is held stationary inside a coil connected to a meter. The meter reads:

  1. a large steady current, because the flux is large
  2. zero, because the flux is not changing
  3. a current that slowly decreases
  4. an alternating current

Induction requires a change in flux. A stationary magnet gives a constant flux, so no emf.

The flux through a single loop changes from 0.020 Wb to 0.080 Wb in 0.30 s. The average induced emf is:

  1. 0.018 V
  2. 0.27 V
  3. 0.060 V
  4. 0.20 V

\(\varepsilon = \Delta\Phi/\Delta t = 0.060/0.30 = 0.20\ \text{V}\).

A flat coil lies with its plane parallel to a uniform magnetic field. The magnetic flux through the coil is:

  1. zero
  2. maximum
  3. half the maximum
  4. \(BA\) multiplied by the number of turns

With the plane parallel to \(B\), no field lines pass through the coil, so \(\Phi = 0\).

Which change would not increase the average emf induced when a magnet is pushed into a coil?

  1. using a coil with more turns
  2. pushing the magnet in faster
  3. using a coil made of thicker wire with the same number of turns
  4. using a stronger magnet

\(\varepsilon = N\Delta\Phi/\Delta t\) doesn't depend on wire thickness. (Thicker wire has less resistance, so the current would be larger, but the emf is the same.)

A 300-turn coil of area 25 cm² sits in a magnetic field perpendicular to its plane. The field increases uniformly from 0.10 T to 0.50 T in 0.20 s. Find the average emf.

\(A = 25\ \text{cm}^2 = 2.5\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2\).

\(\Delta\Phi = \Delta B \times A = 0.40 \times 2.5\times10^{-3} = 1.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{Wb}\).

\(\varepsilon = 300 \times 1.0\times10^{-3}/0.20 = 1.5\ \text{V}\).

A 40-turn coil with area 0.050 m² sits with its plane perpendicular to a 0.30 T field. It is flipped over (rotated 180°) in 0.10 s. Find the average emf.

Initial flux \(+BA = +0.015\ \text{Wb}\). After a 180° flip the same field lines pass through it from the other side: \(-0.015\ \text{Wb}\).

\(|\Delta\Phi| = 0.030\ \text{Wb}\) (not zero, and not 0.015!).

\(\varepsilon = 40 \times 0.030/0.10 = 12\ \text{V}\).

Sketch the emf–time graph for a coil whose flux–time graph is a straight line rising from 0 to \(\Phi_{\max}\) in time \(t_1\), then remains constant. Explain the shape.

A constant (non-zero) emf from 0 to \(t_1\), of size \(N\Phi_{\max}/t_1\) (negative by Faraday's sign convention), then zero after \(t_1\). [1]

From 0 to \(t_1\) the flux changes at a constant rate (constant gradient), so the induced emf is constant. [1]

After \(t_1\) the flux is constant, so its rate of change (and the emf) is zero, even though the flux itself is at its maximum. [1]

Summary

  • \(\Phi = B_\perp A\): maximum when the plane is ⊥ B, zero when ∥ B. Unit: Wb.
  • Changing flux induces an emf: \(\varepsilon = -N\Delta\Phi/\Delta t\).
  • emf = \(-N\) × gradient of the Φ–t graph. Max flux can mean zero emf.
  • A 180° flip changes the flux by \(2BA\).
