How electromagnetic waves are made

An electromagnetic (EM) wave is produced whenever a charged particle accelerates (speeds up, slows down or changes direction).

  1. An accelerating charge creates a changing electric field around it.
  2. A changing electric field produces a changing magnetic field.
  3. That changing magnetic field in turn produces a changing electric field, and so on.
  4. The two fields regenerate each other and travel outward as a self-sustaining wave. No medium is needed, so EM waves cross the vacuum of space.

Examples: electrons oscillating up and down a radio antenna (radio waves); electrons being deflected in a synchrotron (synchrotron radiation); electrons changing energy levels in atoms (visible light, see Spectra).

A charge moving at constant velocity does not radiate. The dot point says accelerating charges. A current in a straight DC wire (steady drift) produces a steady magnetic field but no EM wave.

The structure of an EM wave

The wave equation
\[ c = f\lambda \qquad (\text{in general } v = f\lambda) \]

\(c = 3.0\times10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) (speed of light in a vacuum), \(f\) = frequency (Hz), \(\lambda\) = wavelength (m). Visible light: 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red).

Radio waves, visible light and gamma rays all travel at exactly \(c\) in a vacuum. They differ only in frequency and wavelength (and so in photon energy). Higher frequency ↔ shorter wavelength, always with \(f\lambda = c\).

The electromagnetic spectrum

Find (a) the wavelength of a 100 MHz FM radio signal, (b) the frequency of 550 nm green light and (c) the wavelength of 2.45 GHz microwave-oven radiation.

All electromagnetic waves travel at \(c\) in a vacuum, so frequency and wavelength are inversely proportional: higher frequency means shorter wavelength.

(a) \(\lambda = c/f = 3.0\times10^8/1.00\times10^8 = 3.0\ \text{m}\).

(b) \(f = c/\lambda = 3.0\times10^8/5.5\times10^{-7} = 5.5\times10^{14}\ \text{Hz}\).

(c) \(\lambda = 3.0\times10^8/2.45\times10^9 = 0.12\ \text{m}\) (12 cm).

(a) 3.0 m (b) \(5.5\times10^{14}\ \text{Hz}\) (c) 12 cm. Prefixes: MHz = 10⁶, GHz = 10⁹, nm = 10⁻⁹.

Polarisation: evidence that light is transverse

Not a separate dot point in the 2024–2027 study design, but it's the classic evidence for the word “transverse” in the first one.

In ordinary (unpolarised) light, the electric field oscillates in all directions perpendicular to the direction of travel. A polarising filter only lets through the component of the electric field oscillating along one direction, so the light that emerges is plane polarised.

  • Place a second filter (an analyser) after the first. Rotate it and the brightness changes; when its axis is at 90° to the first filter, almost no light gets through.
  • This only makes sense for a transverse wave. A longitudinal wave (like sound) oscillates along the direction of travel, so it has no “direction across the beam” to filter. Sound cannot be polarised.
  • Applications: polarised sunglasses cut glare (light reflected from water and roads is partly polarised horizontally), LCD screens, 3D cinema glasses, stress analysis in plastics.

If asked what polarisation shows, say: “light can be polarised, which is only possible for transverse waves, so it supports the model of light as a transverse (electromagnetic) wave.” It does not by itself prove light is a wave rather than a particle; interference and diffraction are the stronger wave evidence.

In the 1860s James Clerk Maxwell combined the laws of electricity and magnetism and found they predicted waves of changing electric and magnetic fields travelling at \(1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0} \approx 3.0\times10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}\). That matched the measured speed of light, so he concluded light is an electromagnetic wave. Hertz confirmed it in 1887 by generating radio waves from sparks.

Practice

Which of the following will produce electromagnetic radiation?

  1. an electron moving at constant velocity
  2. a stationary charged sphere
  3. an electron oscillating back and forth in an antenna
  4. a steady direct current in a straight wire

Only accelerating charges radiate. An oscillating electron is constantly accelerating.

Compared with visible light in a vacuum, X-rays have:

  1. a higher speed and shorter wavelength
  2. the same speed and a shorter wavelength
  3. the same speed and a longer wavelength
  4. a lower speed and higher frequency

All EM waves travel at \(c\) in a vacuum. X-rays have higher frequency, so shorter wavelength.

In an electromagnetic wave, the electric and magnetic fields are:

  1. parallel to each other and to the direction of travel
  2. perpendicular to each other and parallel to the direction of travel
  3. parallel to each other and perpendicular to the direction of travel
  4. perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel

Both fields are transverse (perpendicular to travel) and perpendicular to each other.

Sound waves cannot be polarised. This is because sound:

  1. is a longitudinal wave
  2. travels too slowly
  3. needs a medium
  4. has too long a wavelength

Polarisation selects one transverse direction of oscillation. Longitudinal waves oscillate along the direction of travel, so there is nothing to select.

Describe how an electromagnetic wave is produced and how it travels through a vacuum.

An accelerating charge (e.g. an electron oscillating in an antenna) produces a changing electric field. [1]

The changing electric field induces a changing magnetic field, which in turn induces a changing electric field; the fields are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel. [1]

Because the fields continually regenerate each other, the wave is self-propagating and needs no medium; in a vacuum it travels at \(c = 3.0\times10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}\). [1]

Summary

  • Accelerating charges → changing E field ↔ changing B field → EM wave.
  • EM waves are transverse, need no medium and all travel at \(c\) in a vacuum; \(c = f\lambda\).
  • Polarisation is possible only for transverse waves, supporting the EM wave model of light.
