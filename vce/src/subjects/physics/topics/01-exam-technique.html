Command words

The verb at the start of a question tells you what kind of answer earns the marks.

WordWhat the examiner wants
Identify / state / nameA short, precise answer. No explanation needed.
DescribeWhat happens, in detail: features, pattern, sequence. Not necessarily why.
ExplainWhy or how it happens: a chain of cause and effect using physics principles. Usually one mark per correct link.
CompareSimilarities and differences, with both things mentioned in each point (“X is … whereas Y is …”).
JustifySupport your answer with evidence or reasoning (often a calculation).
EvaluateJudge strengths and weaknesses and reach a conclusion.
Calculate / determineA numerical answer with working and units.
Show thatProve a given value: every step shown, final answer to one more significant figure than given.
SketchA graph with the right shape, labelled axes, key values and features (intercepts, asymptotes). No grid needed.

Setting out a calculation

Calculate the speed of a satellite orbiting Earth at a radius of \(8.0\times10^6\ \text{m}\).

For a satellite in a circular orbit, gravity is the only force, so it supplies the centripetal force: \(\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r}\). Cancelling \(m\) gives the orbital speed, which depends only on the central mass and the radius.

Write the equation in symbols first: \(v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}\).

Substitute every value with its power of ten: \(v = \sqrt{\dfrac{(6.67\times10^{-11})(5.98\times10^{24})}{8.0\times10^6}}\).

Answer with units and sensible significant figures: \(v = 7.1\times10^3\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).

Formula → substitution → answer with unit. Even if your final number is wrong, a correct formula and substitution usually earn method marks. A bare number earns nothing if it's wrong.

  • Significant figures: VCAA answers are usually expected to 2 or 3 significant figures. Match the data given. Keep full calculator values between steps and round only at the end.
  • Units: always include them. Convert to SI (m, kg, s, A) before substituting.
  • Consequential marks: if you get part (a) wrong and use that answer correctly in part (b), you can still earn the marks for (b). So always attempt later parts, using your earlier answer (or a sensible estimate).
  • Vectors: give a direction for forces, velocities, momenta, fields and accelerations.

Writing explanations

  1. State the physics principle or law (Newton's second law, Faraday's law, conservation of energy…).
  2. Apply it to the specific situation, naming objects and forces (“the normal force from the track on the cart”).
  3. Link to the outcome asked about, ideally with an equation showing the relationship.

A 3-mark “explain” question almost always wants three distinct, linked points.

Avoid vague words: “it”, “the force”, “the energy”, “gravity pushes”, “absorbs the force”, “centrifugal force”. Use the correct names: normal force, tension, weight, net force, induced emf, magnetic flux.

Graphs

  • Label both axes with quantity and unit. Use even scales that fill the grid.
  • Plot accurately; draw one smooth line of best fit (never dot to dot).
  • For gradients, use two widely separated points on the line and show the working.
  • Sketches: correct shape, key points labelled (e.g. the emf is zero when the flux is maximum), consistent period/amplitude.

Multiple choice

  • Cover the options, work out your own answer, then look.
  • Eliminate options with wrong units, wrong direction or an impossible size (faster than light, efficiency over 100%).
  • Watch for the classic distractors on every topic page: the answer you'd get by forgetting to square, forgetting \(N\), using the wrong frame, or using peak instead of RMS.
  • No penalty for wrong answers, so never leave one blank.

Reading time and timing

  • Use reading time to find the questions you're confident about and to plan the order. Read the long, multi-part questions carefully.
  • Budget a little over one minute per mark. Don't let a stuck 2-mark question eat ten minutes: flag it and come back.
  • Leave five minutes at the end to check units, significant figures and that every part is answered.
