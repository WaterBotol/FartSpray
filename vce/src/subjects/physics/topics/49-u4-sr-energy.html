Mass is a form of energy

Special relativity shows that mass and energy are equivalent: an object at rest has energy simply because it has mass.

Rest energy
\[ E_{\text{rest}} = mc^2 \]

\(m\) is the object's (rest) mass in kg. Because \(c^2 = 9.0\times10^{16}\ \text{m}^2\,\text{s}^{-2}\), a tiny mass is equivalent to an enormous energy: 1 g ↔ \(9\times10^{13}\) J, about the energy of a 20-kilotonne explosion.

Total and kinetic energy
\[ E_{\text{total}} = \gamma mc^2 = E_k + E_{\text{rest}} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad E_k = (\gamma - 1)mc^2 \]

\(E_k = (\gamma - 1)mc^2\) is the correct formula at all speeds. At low speeds it gives the same answer as \(\tfrac12 mv^2\) (to a very good approximation). As \(v \to c\), \(\gamma \to \infty\), so the kinetic energy needed grows without limit. No object with mass can reach the speed of light, because it would need infinite energy.

Calculate the rest energy of an electron in joules and in MeV.

\[ E = mc^2 = (9.1\times10^{-31})(3.0\times10^8)^2 = 8.2\times10^{-14}\ \text{J} \]
\[ \frac{8.19\times10^{-14}}{1.60\times10^{-19}} = 5.1\times10^5\ \text{eV} = 0.51\ \text{MeV} \]

\(8.2\times10^{-14}\ \text{J}\) = 0.51 MeV. Worth remembering: once an electron's kinetic energy is comparable to 0.5 MeV, it's relativistic.

A proton moves at \(0.60c\). Calculate its kinetic energy using relativity and compare with the classical value.

\(\gamma\): \(1/\sqrt{1 - 0.36} = 1.25\). Rest energy: \(mc^2 = (1.67\times10^{-27})(9.0\times10^{16}) = 1.50\times10^{-10}\ \text{J}\).

Relativistic: \(E_k = (\gamma - 1)mc^2 = 0.25 \times 1.50\times10^{-10} = 3.8\times10^{-11}\ \text{J}\).

Classical: \(\tfrac12 mv^2 = \tfrac12(1.67\times10^{-27})(1.8\times10^8)^2 = 2.7\times10^{-11}\ \text{J}\).

\(3.8\times10^{-11}\ \text{J}\) relativistic vs \(2.7\times10^{-11}\ \text{J}\) classical. At \(0.6c\) the classical formula underestimates by about 30%.

An electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 1.0 MV. (a) What is its kinetic energy? (b) What speed does the classical formula predict? (c) What is its actual speed?

(a) \(E_k = eV = 1.0\ \text{MeV} = 1.6\times10^{-13}\ \text{J}\).

(b) Classical: \(v = \sqrt{2E_k/m} = \sqrt{2(1.6\times10^{-13})/9.1\times10^{-31}} = 5.9\times10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}\), about \(2c\). Impossible.

(c) Relativistic: \(\gamma = 1 + \dfrac{E_k}{mc^2} = 1 + \dfrac{1.6\times10^{-13}}{8.19\times10^{-14}} = 2.95\).

Speed from \(\gamma\): \(\dfrac{v}{c} = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{\gamma^2}} = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{2.95^2}} = 0.94\).

(a) 1.0 MeV (b) \(5.9\times10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}\), faster than light, so wrong (c) \(0.94c = 2.8\times10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}\). Always check whether \(E_k\) is small compared with \(mc^2\) before using \(\tfrac12 mv^2\).

Older books say mass increases with speed. VCAA uses the (rest) mass \(m\) throughout and puts the speed dependence in \(\gamma\): write \(E_{\text{total}} = \gamma mc^2\), not “the mass becomes \(\gamma m\)”. Also, \(E_k = \tfrac12\gamma mv^2\) is wrong; the correct form is \((\gamma - 1)mc^2\).

Mass–energy conversion in nature and technology

Nuclear fusion in the Sun

In the Sun's core, hydrogen nuclei fuse (through several steps) into helium. The mass of the helium nucleus plus other products is less than the mass of the four protons that made it. The missing mass (the mass defect) appears as energy: kinetic energy of the products and gamma-ray photons, which eventually leave the Sun as electromagnetic radiation (mostly visible and infrared light).

The Sun radiates energy at \(3.85\times10^{26}\ \text{W}\). At what rate is it losing mass?

\[ \frac{\Delta m}{\Delta t} = \frac{P}{c^2} = \frac{3.85\times10^{26}}{(3.0\times10^8)^2} = 4.3\times10^9\ \text{kg s}^{-1} \]

About 4 million tonnes per second. The Sun is so massive (\(2\times10^{30}\) kg) that this barely matters over billions of years.

The overall fusion of four protons into helium releases 26.7 MeV. What mass is converted to energy in each reaction?

\(E = 26.7\times10^6 \times 1.60\times10^{-19} = 4.27\times10^{-12}\ \text{J}\).

\(\Delta m = E/c^2 = 4.27\times10^{-12}/9.0\times10^{16} = 4.7\times10^{-29}\ \text{kg}\).

\(4.7\times10^{-29}\ \text{kg}\), about 0.7% of the mass of the four protons.

Positron–electron annihilation

A positron is the antiparticle of the electron: the same mass, opposite charge. When a positron meets an electron they annihilate: their entire mass is converted into energy, carried away by gamma-ray photons.

Annihilation (particles almost at rest)
\[ e^+ + e^- \rightarrow 2\gamma \qquad E = 2m_ec^2 = 2 \times 0.511\ \text{MeV} = 1.022\ \text{MeV} \]

Two photons, each of 0.511 MeV, travel in opposite directions so that total momentum stays zero.

An electron and a positron, both essentially at rest, annihilate to produce two identical photons. Find the energy of each photon in joules and MeV, and its wavelength. (\(m_e = 9.1\times10^{-31}\ \text{kg}\))

Total energy: \(2m_ec^2 = 2(9.1\times10^{-31})(3.0\times10^8)^2 = 1.64\times10^{-13}\ \text{J}\). Each photon: \(8.2\times10^{-14}\ \text{J} = 0.51\ \text{MeV}\).

Wavelength: \(\lambda = \frac{hc}{E} = \frac{(6.63\times10^{-34})(3.0\times10^8)}{8.2\times10^{-14}} = 2.4\times10^{-12}\ \text{m}\).

0.51 MeV (\(8.2\times10^{-14}\) J) each, wavelength \(2.4\times10^{-12}\) m: gamma rays. PET (positron emission tomography) scanners detect these back-to-back pairs to locate where positrons were emitted in the body.

Nuclear transformations in particle accelerators

  • In particle accelerators, electric fields do work on particles, raising \(\gamma\) enormously. At the Australian Synchrotron, 3 GeV electrons have \(\gamma \approx 5900\).
  • When high-energy particles collide, kinetic energy can be converted into the mass of new particles (\(E = mc^2\) in reverse). The Large Hadron Collider produces particles such as the Higgs boson, whose mass (about 125 GeV/\(c^2\)) came from the collision energy.
  • Accelerators (e.g. hospital cyclotrons) also bombard nuclei to transform them into new ones, such as medical isotopes. In every nuclear transformation, a decrease in total mass is released as energy, and an increase in mass needs energy supplied. The details of particular nuclear processes are not required.

Fission

  • Nuclear fission (splitting uranium in a reactor) also converts a small fraction of mass into kinetic energy of the fragments, which becomes heat to drive turbines.

Say: “In fusion, the total mass of the products is less than the total mass of the reactants. The mass difference is converted to energy according to \(E = \Delta mc^2\), released as kinetic energy and electromagnetic radiation, so the Sun's mass decreases as it radiates.”

Practice

Which expression correctly gives the kinetic energy of an object moving at speed \(v\) close to \(c\)?

  1. \(\tfrac12 mv^2\)
  2. \(\tfrac12\gamma mv^2\)
  3. \((\gamma - 1)mc^2\)
  4. \(\gamma mc^2\)

Kinetic energy is total energy minus rest energy: \(\gamma mc^2 - mc^2 = (\gamma - 1)mc^2\). Option D is the total energy.

Increasing amounts of energy are supplied to an electron in an accelerator. Its speed:

  1. increases without limit
  2. approaches but never reaches \(c\)
  3. reaches \(c\) and then stays constant
  4. decreases once it passes \(0.9c\)

As \(v \to c\), \(\gamma \to \infty\), so each extra joule increases the speed less and less. Reaching \(c\) would need infinite energy.

The Sun's mass decreases over time mainly because:

  1. mass is converted to energy in nuclear fusion, which is radiated away
  2. it burns hydrogen chemically
  3. the solar wind carries away most of its mass
  4. gravity compresses it

Fusion products have less mass than the reactants; that mass becomes energy that leaves as radiation (\(\Delta m = E/c^2\)).

An object's total energy is three times its rest energy. Its kinetic energy is:

  1. \(mc^2\)
  2. \(3mc^2\)
  3. \(\tfrac32 mc^2\)
  4. \(2mc^2\)

\(E_{\text{total}} = 3mc^2 = E_k + mc^2 \Rightarrow E_k = 2mc^2\) (and \(\gamma = 3\)).

How much energy would be needed to accelerate a 1.0 kg object from rest to \(0.90c\)?

\(\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - 0.81} = 2.29\).

\(E_k = (\gamma - 1)mc^2 = 1.29 \times 1.0 \times 9.0\times10^{16} = 1.2\times10^{17}\ \text{J}\), about the energy released by 28 megatonnes of TNT, just to get one kilogram moving that fast.

A proton has a kinetic energy equal to its rest energy. Find its speed.

\(E_k = (\gamma - 1)mc^2 = mc^2 \Rightarrow \gamma = 2\).

\(v/c = \sqrt{1 - 1/\gamma^2} = \sqrt{1 - 0.25} = 0.866\).

\(v = 0.87c = 2.6\times10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}\). (True for any particle, not just protons.)

Explain why a spacecraft can never be accelerated to the speed of light, using the relativistic expression for kinetic energy.

The kinetic energy of an object is \(E_k = (\gamma - 1)mc^2\), where \(\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}\). [1]

As \(v\) approaches \(c\), \(1 - v^2/c^2\) approaches zero, so \(\gamma\) and therefore \(E_k\) increase without limit. [1]

Reaching \(v = c\) would require infinite energy, which is impossible, so any object with mass must travel slower than light. [1]

An electron and a positron at rest annihilate. Why are two photons produced, rather than one?

  1. because there are two particles, each becoming one photon
  2. because a single photon would carry too much energy
  3. because the total momentum was zero, so the photons must travel in opposite directions to keep it zero
  4. because photons always come in pairs

A single photon always carries momentum \(p = E/c\), so it can't have zero total momentum. Two equal photons moving in opposite directions can.

Summary

  • \(E_{\text{rest}} = mc^2\); \(E_{\text{total}} = \gamma mc^2\); \(E_k = (\gamma - 1)mc^2\).
  • \(\tfrac12 mv^2\) only works when \(v \ll c\). Check before using it.
  • Reaching \(c\) would need infinite energy.
  • Fusion in the Sun converts about 4 × 10⁹ kg of mass to radiated energy every second.
  • Electron–positron annihilation turns all the mass into two 0.511 MeV gamma photons; colliders turn kinetic energy into new mass.
