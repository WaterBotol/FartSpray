Exothermic vs endothermic

The enthalpy change \(\Delta H = H_{\text{products}} - H_{\text{reactants}}\) is the heat energy absorbed or released at constant pressure.

ExothermicEndothermic
Energy flowReleased to the surroundings (they get hotter)Absorbed from the surroundings (they get colder)
\(\Delta H\)NegativePositive
BondsEnergy released making bonds > energy absorbed breaking bondsEnergy absorbed breaking bonds > energy released making bonds
ExamplesCombustion, respiration, neutralisationPhotosynthesis, dissolving NH₄NO₃, thermal decomposition, electrolysis of water

Breaking bonds always absorbs energy; making bonds always releases energy. Whether a reaction is exothermic depends on which of these is bigger overall.

Thermochemical equations

A thermochemical equation
\[ \ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2H2O(l)} \quad \Delta H = -890\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1} \]

“Per mole” means per mole of reaction as written: 890 kJ is released when 1 mol CH₄ reacts with 2 mol O₂. States matter: forming \(\ce{H2O(g)}\) releases less energy than forming \(\ce{H2O(l)}\).

Given \(\ce{2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)}\), \(\Delta H = -572\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\), write the thermochemical equation for the decomposition of 1 mol of liquid water.

\(\Delta H\) belongs to the equation exactly as written. Reversing an equation reverses the sign of \(\Delta H\); multiplying the coefficients multiplies \(\Delta H\) by the same factor.

Reverse (sign flips): \(\ce{2H2O(l) -> 2H2(g) + O2(g)}\), \(\Delta H = +572\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\).

Halve: \(\ce{H2O(l) -> H2(g) + 1/2O2(g)}\), \(\Delta H = +286\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\).

Using the methane equation above, calculate the energy released when 10.0 g of methane burns completely.

The \(\Delta H\) value is the energy per mole of reaction as written. Find the moles of fuel, then scale the energy by that amount.

\(n(\ce{CH4}) = \dfrac{10.0}{16.0} = 0.625\text{ mol}\).

\(E = 0.625 \times 890 = \) 556 kJ released.

Given \(\ce{C(s) + O2(g) -> CO2(g)}\), \(\Delta H = -394\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\), and \(\ce{2CO(g) + O2(g) -> 2CO2(g)}\), \(\Delta H = -566\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\), find \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{2C(s) + O2(g) -> 2CO(g)}\).

Hess's law: the total enthalpy change is the same however you get from reactants to products. Rearrange the given equations (reverse, scale) so they add to the target equation, and do the same to their \(\Delta H\) values.

Double the first: \(\ce{2C + 2O2 -> 2CO2}\), \(\Delta H = -788\).

Reverse the second: \(\ce{2CO2 -> 2CO + O2}\), \(\Delta H = +566\).

Add (the 2CO₂ and one O₂ cancel): \(\ce{2C + O2 -> 2CO}\), \(\Delta H = -222\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\). Incomplete combustion releases far less energy than complete combustion (−788 kJ for 2 mol C).

Propane burns according to \(\ce{C3H8(g) + 5O2(g)}\) \(\ce{-> 3CO2(g) + 4H2O(l)}\), \(\Delta H = -2220\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\). What mass of propane is needed to heat water requiring 5.00 MJ, if only 60.0% of the energy is transferred to the water?

If only part of the released energy is useful, the fuel must release more than you need: divide the required energy by the efficiency. Then convert energy to moles using \(\Delta H\), and moles to mass.

Energy that must be released: \(\dfrac{5.00 \times 10^3\text{ kJ}}{0.600} = 8.33 \times 10^3\text{ kJ}\).

\(n(\ce{C3H8}) = \dfrac{8333}{2220} = 3.754\text{ mol}\).

\(m = 3.754 \times 44.0 = \) 165 g. Divide by the efficiency (you need more fuel), don't multiply.

  • Sign conventions: “energy released = 556 kJ” (positive number) but \(\Delta H = -890\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\) (negative).
  • “Per mole” is per mole of equation, not per mole of each substance. For \(\ce{2C8H18 + 25O2 -> ...}\), \(\Delta H = -10\,920\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\) is for 2 mol octane.
  • Catalysts lower \(E_a\) but don't change \(\Delta H\).
  • Efficiency: fuel needed = energy required ÷ efficiency.

  1. the reactants and the products
  2. the products and the peak

\(E_a\) is measured from the reactants to the transition state. B is the activation energy of the reverse reaction.

Which statement about an endothermic reaction is correct?

  1. It releases energy because bonds are broken
  2. Its products have less enthalpy than its reactants
  3. It can't happen at room temperature
  4. The energy absorbed breaking bonds exceeds the energy released forming bonds

Breaking bonds absorbs energy. Many endothermic processes happen spontaneously at room temperature (e.g. dissolving NH₄NO₃).

A reaction has \(E_a = 120\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\) and \(\Delta H = -45\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\). The activation energy of the reverse reaction is

  1. 165 kJ mol⁻¹
  2. 75 kJ mol⁻¹
  3. 120 kJ mol⁻¹
  4. 45 kJ mol⁻¹

\(E_a(\text{rev}) = E_a(\text{fwd}) - \Delta H = 120 - (-45) = 165\). Draw it: the products sit 45 kJ below the reactants, so they're further from the peak.

\(\ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g)}\) \(\ce{-> CO2(g) + 2H2O(g)}\) has \(\Delta H = -802\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\), whereas forming liquid water gives −890. The difference is mainly because

  1. gaseous water has stronger bonds
  2. energy is released when water vapour condenses
  3. the reaction with liquid water is faster
  4. a catalyst is used in one reaction

Condensation is exothermic, so forming liquid water releases an extra \(2 \times 44 = 88\) kJ.

\(\ce{C2H5OH(l) + 3O2(g)}\) \(\ce{-> 2CO2(g) + 3H2O(l)}\), \(\Delta H = -1360\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\). Calculate the volume of CO₂ at SLC produced when enough ethanol burns to release 500 kJ.

\(n(\text{reaction}) = \frac{500}{1360} = 0.3676\text{ mol}\), so \(n(\ce{CO2}) = 2 \times 0.3676 = 0.7353\text{ mol}\). [2]

\(V = 0.7353 \times 24.8 = 18.2\text{ L}\). [1]

Given \(\ce{S(s) + O2(g) -> SO2(g)}\), \(\Delta H = -297\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\), and \(\ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g) -> 2SO3(g)}\), \(\Delta H = -198\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\), find \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{2S(s) + 3O2(g) -> 2SO3(g)}\).

Double the first: \(\ce{2S + 2O2 -> 2SO2}\), \(\Delta H = -594\). [1]

Add the second: \(\ce{2S + 3O2 -> 2SO3}\). [1]

\(\Delta H = -594 + (-198) = -792\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\). [1]

A student writes: “\(\ce{2C8H18 + 25O2}\) \(\ce{-> 16CO2 + 18H2O}\), \(\Delta H = -5460\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\).” What's wrong?

\(-5460\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\) is for 1 mol of octane. As written (2 mol octane), \(\Delta H = -10\,920\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\). States are also missing.

Summary

  • Exothermic: \(\Delta H < 0\); endothermic: \(\Delta H > 0\).
  • Reverse → change sign; scale → scale \(\Delta H\); add equations → add \(\Delta H\).
  • \(E_a\): reactants to peak. \(E_a(\text{rev}) = E_a(\text{fwd}) - \Delta H\).
  • Energy = \(n \times |\Delta H|\), with \(n\) in moles of the equation as written.
