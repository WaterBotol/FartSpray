What is a fuel cell?

A fuel cell is a galvanic cell that runs continuously as long as fuel and oxidant (usually O₂ from air) are supplied. The fuel is oxidised at the anode and O₂ is reduced at the cathode, but the two never mix, so the energy is released as electricity rather than heat.

  • Porous electrodes give a large surface area where gas, electrolyte and electrode meet.
  • Catalysts (e.g. platinum or nickel) coat the electrodes to speed up the half-reactions.
  • Electrolyte carries ions between the electrodes: a proton exchange membrane (PEM, carries H⁺), KOH solution (carries OH⁻) or a molten carbonate or solid oxide at high temperature.

Half-equations

CellAnode (oxidation, −)Cathode (reduction, +)
H₂, acidic (PEM)\(\ce{H2 -> 2H+ + 2e-}\)\(\ce{O2 + 4H+ + 4e- -> 2H2O}\)
H₂, alkaline\(\ce{H2 + 2OH- -> 2H2O + 2e-}\)\(\ce{O2 + 2H2O + 4e- -> 4OH-}\)
CH₄, acidic\(\ce{CH4 + 2H2O -> CO2 + 8H+ + 8e-}\)\(\ce{O2 + 4H+ + 4e- -> 2H2O}\)
C₂H₅OH, acidic\(\ce{C2H5OH + 3H2O -> 2CO2 + 12H+ + 12e-}\)\(\ce{O2 + 4H+ + 4e- -> 2H2O}\)

The overall equation is the same as for combustion (e.g. \(\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}\)), but no flame is involved.

In an acidic (PEM) cell, H⁺ travels to the cathode and water forms at the cathode. In an alkaline cell, OH⁻ travels to the anode and water forms at the anode. Exam questions love this detail.

Combine the half-equations for an acidic methane fuel cell into the overall equation.

Anode gives 8 e⁻; cathode uses 4 e⁻, so double the cathode: \(\ce{2O2 + 8H+ + 8e- -> 4H2O}\).

Add and cancel (8 H⁺, 8 e⁻, 2 H₂O): \(\ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2H2O(l)}\).

A hydrogen fuel cell delivers a current of 50.0 A at 0.700 V for 1.00 hour. (a) What mass of H₂ is consumed? (b) What percentage of the energy available from burning this hydrogen (\(\Delta H = -286\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\)) is delivered as electricity? (\(F = 96\,500\ \text{C mol}^{-1}\))

\(Q = It = 50.0 \times 3600 = 1.80 \times 10^5\text{ C}\), so \(n(\ce{e-}) = \frac{Q}{F} = 1.865\text{ mol}\).

\(\ce{H2 -> 2H+ + 2e-}\): \(n(\ce{H2}) = 0.9326\text{ mol}\), \(m = 1.87\text{ g}\).

Electrical energy \(= VIt = 0.700 \times 50.0 \times 3600 = 126\text{ kJ}\). Available: \(0.9326 \times 286 = 267\text{ kJ}\).

(a) 1.87 g H₂; (b) \(\frac{126}{267} \times 100 = 47.2\%\). A typical car engine is about 25–30% efficient.

Green chemistry and hydrogen

Green chemistry principles relevant to energy include: using renewable feedstocks, designing for energy efficiency, using catalysts, preventing waste and choosing safer chemicals. Hydrogen's “colour” describes how it's made:

  • Green hydrogen: electrolysis of water powered by renewable electricity (no CO₂ emitted).
  • Grey hydrogen: steam reforming of natural gas: \(\ce{CH4 + H2O -> CO + 3H2}\), with CO₂ released.
  • Blue hydrogen: grey hydrogen with the CO₂ captured and stored.
  • Fuel cells are galvanic cells: anode negative, cathode positive.
  • The fuel is always at the anode (it's the reductant); O₂ is always at the cathode.
  • Fuel cells don't store energy; they convert it while fuel is supplied.
  • Balance acidic vs alkaline correctly: no H⁺ in an alkaline cell, and no OH⁻ in an acidic one.

Practice

In an alkaline hydrogen fuel cell, the half-equation at the anode is

  1. \(\ce{H2 + 2OH- -> 2H2O + 2e-}\)
  2. \(\ce{H2 -> 2H+ + 2e-}\)
  3. \(\ce{O2 + 2H2O + 4e- -> 4OH-}\)
  4. \(\ce{O2 + 4H+ + 4e- -> 2H2O}\)

Fuel oxidised at the anode, in an alkaline electrolyte (OH⁻, no H⁺).

Why are fuel cell electrodes porous?

  1. To let electrons pass through the electrolyte
  2. To reduce the mass of the cell
  3. To give a large surface area where gas, electrolyte and catalyst meet
  4. To stop the gases reaching the electrolyte

The reaction happens at the three-phase boundary; more surface means a higher rate (more current).

In a PEM (acidic) hydrogen fuel cell, water is produced

  1. at the anode
  2. at the cathode
  3. at both electrodes
  4. in the membrane

H⁺ crosses the membrane to the cathode, where \(\ce{O2 + 4H+ + 4e- -> 2H2O}\).

Fuel cells are more efficient than petrol engines mainly because

  1. hydrogen has more energy per gram than petrol
  2. they use platinum
  3. they produce only water
  4. they convert chemical energy directly into electricity without a heat-engine step

Heat engines lose much of the energy as heat. (A is true but unrelated to efficiency.)

How many moles of electrons are released per mole of ethanol in an acidic ethanol fuel cell (ethanol → CO₂)?

  1. 4
  2. 6
  3. 12
  4. 2

Each C goes from −2 to +4: 6 e⁻ per C, two carbons: 12. (Oxidation only to ethanoic acid gives 4.)

Which statement comparing a fuel cell with a primary galvanic cell is correct?

  1. Both convert chemical energy to electrical energy, but only the fuel cell has reactants supplied continuously
  2. Only the primary cell has an anode and a cathode
  3. The fuel cell's anode is positive
  4. The fuel cell stores energy for later use

Both are galvanic (anode −). Fuel cells convert energy as reactants flow; they don't store it.

A city is considering replacing diesel buses with hydrogen fuel cell buses. Evaluate this proposal with reference to efficiency, emissions at the point of use, and the source of the hydrogen.

Efficiency: fuel cells convert chemical energy directly into electricity (about 40–60%), compared with about 30–40% for diesel engines, so less energy is wasted as heat. [1]

Emissions: the only product at the bus is water, so there's no CO₂, CO, NOₓ or particulates in city streets (better air quality). [1]

Source: if the hydrogen comes from steam reforming of natural gas, CO₂ is still emitted at the production site; the benefit is greatest with green hydrogen from renewable-powered electrolysis. [1]

Other issues: hydrogen storage and refuelling infrastructure, and the cost and scarcity of platinum catalysts. A conclusion should weigh these. [1]

A student labels the O₂ electrode of a fuel cell as the negative anode “because oxygen is where the electrons go”. Correct them.

Electrons go to the O₂ electrode, so reduction occurs there: it's the cathode, and in a galvanic (or fuel) cell the cathode is positive. The fuel electrode is the negative anode.

Summary

  • Fuel at the anode (−), O₂ at the cathode (+); porous catalytic electrodes; the electrolyte carries ions.
  • Acidic: water forms at the cathode. Alkaline: water forms at the anode.
  • Direct chemical → electrical conversion gives higher efficiency than combustion.
  • “Green” only if the fuel (e.g. H₂) is produced renewably.
