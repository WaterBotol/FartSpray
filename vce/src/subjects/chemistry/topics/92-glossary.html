Definitions written the way examiners like them. Click the link after each for the full explanation.

A–E

Activation energy (\(E_a\))
The minimum energy colliding particles need to react; from the reactants to the peak of an energy profile. Rates
Active site
The region of an enzyme, formed by its tertiary structure, where the substrate binds. Enzymes
Aliquot
An accurately measured volume of solution delivered by a pipette. Titrations
Anode
The electrode where oxidation occurs (negative in galvanic cells, positive in electrolytic cells). Galvanic cells
Atom economy
The percentage of the total reactant mass that ends up in the desired product. Pathways
Base peak
The most intense peak in a mass spectrum. IR & MS
Biofuel
A fuel made from recently living biomass (biogas, bioethanol, biodiesel). Fuels
Calibration factor
The energy needed to raise the temperature of a calorimeter by 1 °C, found electrically: \(\frac{VIt}{\Delta T}\). Calorimetry
Catalyst
A substance that increases the rate by providing a pathway with lower activation energy, without being consumed. Rates
Cathode
The electrode where reduction occurs (positive in galvanic cells, negative in electrolytic cells). Galvanic cells
Chemical shift (\(\delta\))
The position of an NMR signal (ppm) relative to TMS, which depends on the chemical environment. NMR
Chiral centre
A carbon atom bonded to four different groups, giving rise to enantiomers. Isomers
Competitive inhibitor
A molecule that binds an enzyme's active site, blocking the substrate; its effect is reduced by adding more substrate. Enzymes
Concordant titres
Titres that agree within 0.10 mL. Titrations
Condensation reaction
Two molecules join with the loss of a small molecule, usually water. Food molecules
Conjugate redox pair
An oxidant and the reductant it forms when reduced, e.g. Cu²⁺/Cu. Redox
Denaturation
Loss of a protein's secondary and tertiary structure (and function), without breaking peptide bonds. Food molecules
Dynamic equilibrium
The state of a closed reversible system where forward and reverse rates are equal and concentrations are constant. Equilibrium
Electrolysis
Using electrical energy to drive a non-spontaneous redox reaction. Electrolysis
Enantiomers
Non-superimposable mirror-image stereoisomers. Isomers
End point
The point in a titration where the indicator changes colour. Titrations
Endothermic
Absorbing energy from the surroundings; \(\Delta H > 0\). Thermochemistry
Equivalence point
The point in a titration where reactants have been added in the exact mole ratio of the equation. Titrations
Exothermic
Releasing energy to the surroundings; \(\Delta H < 0\). Thermochemistry

F–O

Faraday constant
The charge on one mole of electrons, 96 500 C mol⁻¹. Faraday's laws
Fermentation
Anaerobic conversion of glucose to ethanol and CO₂ by yeast enzymes. Food as fuel
Fingerprint region
The complex region of an IR spectrum below about 1500 cm⁻¹, unique to each compound. IR & MS
Fossil fuel
A non-renewable fuel formed from ancient organic matter over millions of years (coal, oil, natural gas). Fuels
Fuel cell
A galvanic cell that converts chemical energy to electrical energy continuously as fuel and oxidant are supplied. Fuel cells
Functional group
An atom or group of atoms that gives an organic compound its characteristic reactions. Naming
Galvanic cell
A cell in which a spontaneous redox reaction produces electrical energy. Galvanic cells
Green chemistry
Designing chemical products and processes to reduce hazards, waste and energy use. Pathways
Half-equation
An equation showing either the oxidation or the reduction part of a redox reaction, with electrons. Redox
Homologous series
A family of compounds with the same functional group, differing by CH₂. Naming
Hydrolysis
A reaction in which water breaks a bond, splitting a molecule. Organic reactions
Le Chatelier's principle
A system at equilibrium responds to a change so as to partially oppose it. Le Chatelier
Molecular ion
The ion formed when a whole molecule loses an electron; its \(m/z\) gives the molar mass. IR & MS
Non-competitive inhibitor
A molecule that binds an enzyme away from the active site and changes its shape; more substrate doesn't restore the rate. Enzymes
Oxidant (oxidising agent)
A species that gains electrons (is reduced), causing another species to be oxidised. Redox
Oxidation
Loss of electrons; increase in oxidation number. Redox

P–Z

Peptide bond
The amide link (–CO–NH–) joining amino acids in a protein. Food molecules
Photosynthesis
The conversion of light energy into chemical energy: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. Food as fuel
Primary standard
A very pure, stable substance used to make a solution of accurately known concentration. Titrations
Reaction quotient (\(Q\))
The equilibrium expression evaluated with current (non-equilibrium) concentrations. Equilibrium
Reductant (reducing agent)
A species that loses electrons (is oxidised). Redox
Renewable
Can be replaced by natural processes within a human timescale. Fuels
Retention time
The time a component takes to pass through an HPLC column. Chromatography
Salt bridge
Connects half-cells, allowing ion migration to maintain electrical neutrality. Galvanic cells
Secondary cell
A rechargeable cell (discharge galvanic, recharge electrolytic). Electrolysis
SLC
Standard laboratory conditions: 25 °C and 100 kPa. Gases
Solvent extraction
Separating a compound from a mixture by dissolving it in a solvent in which it is very soluble (often using a separating funnel). Medicinal chemistry
Specific heat capacity
Energy needed to raise 1 g of a substance by 1 °C (water: 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹). Calorimetry
Steam distillation
Extracting volatile oils from plant material by passing steam through it, below the oils' boiling points. Medicinal chemistry
Structural isomers
Compounds with the same molecular formula but different arrangement of atoms. Isomers
Titre
The volume of solution delivered from a burette in a titration. Titrations
TMS
Tetramethylsilane, the 0 ppm reference in NMR. NMR
Transesterification
Converting one ester into another, e.g. triglyceride + methanol → biodiesel + glycerol. Fuels
Triglyceride
A triester of glycerol with three fatty acids (a fat or oil). Food molecules
Zwitterion
A molecule with both positive and negative charges, such as an amino acid at neutral pH. Food molecules
