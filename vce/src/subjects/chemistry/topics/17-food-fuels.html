Photosynthesis: where the energy comes from

Green plants capture light energy and store it as chemical energy in glucose. This is the ultimate source of the energy in food and in biofuels (and, over millions of years, in fossil fuels).

Photosynthesis (endothermic)
\[ \ce{6CO2(g) + 6H2O(l) -> C6H12O6(aq) + 6O2(g)} \quad \Delta H = +2803\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1} \]

Light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll; glucose and oxygen are the products used by living things in respiration.

Photosynthesis and respiration are the reverse of each other overall, so the energy absorbed in photosynthesis is the energy later released in respiration (Hess's law).

Respiration: releasing the energy

Aerobic cellular respiration
\[ \ce{C6H12O6(aq) + 6O2(g) -> 6CO2(g) + 6H2O(l)} \quad \Delta H \approx -2.8 \times 10^3\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1} \]

The same overall reaction as combustion, but done in many enzyme-catalysed steps at body temperature, capturing energy efficiently (as ATP).

Without enough oxygen (anaerobic): in muscles glucose → lactic acid; in yeast, fermentation gives ethanol and CO₂.

Fuel sources for the body

Carbohydrates, proteins and lipids (fats and oils) are the body's fuels. They are first hydrolysed during digestion (see biomolecules): starch and sucrose to glucose, proteins to amino acids, fats to glycerol and fatty acids. These are then oxidised in cells.

  • Carbohydrates are the body's main, fast-release energy source (glucose); excess is stored as glycogen.
  • Fats and oils are the most concentrated energy store, used for longer-term storage.
  • Proteins are mainly used for growth and repair, and are used for energy mostly when other fuels run short.

Fats release more energy per gram because their carbon is less oxidised (they contain far fewer O atoms relative to C and H).

A muesli bar contains 8.0 g fat, 25 g carbohydrate and 4.0 g protein. Estimate its energy content.

\(8.0 \times 37 + 25 \times 16 + 4.0 \times 17 = 296 + 400 + 68\).

764 kJ. Fat is only 22% of the mass but gives 39% of the energy.

A 1.20 g sample of potato chips is burnt in a bomb calorimeter (CF = 8.50 kJ °C⁻¹); the temperature rises by 3.10 °C. Calculate the energy content in kJ g⁻¹, and the energy in a 45 g packet.

\(q = 8.50 \times 3.10 = 26.35\text{ kJ}\).

\(\frac{26.35}{1.20} = 22.0\text{ kJ g}^{-1}\).

22.0 kJ g⁻¹; a 45 g packet provides about 990 kJ. That's between the carbohydrate and fat values, as expected for a food containing both.

Bioethanol: fermentation and distillation

Bioethanol is made from plant material (sugar cane, corn, wheat, or waste biomass). Starch or cellulose is first hydrolysed to glucose, then yeast ferments the glucose in the absence of oxygen:

Fermentation (yeast enzymes, about 30–37 °C, no oxygen)
\[ \ce{C6H12O6(aq) -> 2C2H5OH(aq) + 2CO2(g)} \]
  • Fermentation stops at around 15% ethanol, because higher concentrations kill the yeast.
  • Fractional distillation then separates ethanol (boiling point 78 °C) from water (100 °C), concentrating it to fuel grade. Distillation needs a lot of heat energy, which affects how “green” the fuel is.
  • Sustainability: the CO₂ released when bioethanol burns was recently absorbed from the air by the crop, so bioethanol is renewable and can be close to carbon neutral. It isn't fully carbon neutral when fossil fuels are used for farming, fertiliser, heating the distillation and transport, and growing fuel crops can compete with food crops for land.

A fermentation vat contains 50.0 kg of glucose (\(M = 180.0\)). Calculate the maximum mass of ethanol (\(M = 46.0\)) that can be produced, and the volume of CO₂ released at SLC.

\(n(\ce{C6H12O6}) = \frac{50\,000}{180.0} = 277.8\ \text{mol}\).

\(n(\ce{C2H5OH}) = n(\ce{CO2}) = 2 \times 277.8 = 555.6\ \text{mol}\). Mass of ethanol \(= 555.6 \times 46.0 = 2.56\times10^4\ \text{g} = 25.6\ \text{kg}\).

25.6 kg of ethanol; \(V(\ce{CO2}) = 555.6 \times 24.8 = 1.38\times10^4\ \text{L}\) at SLC. Almost half the glucose's mass leaves as CO₂, which is why the atom economy of fermentation is only 51%.

  • Photosynthesis is endothermic; respiration and combustion are exothermic, with the same ΔH magnitude.
  • Fermentation is anaerobic: no O₂ appears in the equation.
  • Distillation separates; it doesn't make ethanol.
  • “Renewable” ≠ “carbon neutral”. Account for energy used in production.
  • Fibre (cellulose) is a carbohydrate that humans can't hydrolyse, so it provides little energy.

Practice

Which statement about photosynthesis is correct?

  1. It is exothermic and produces CO₂.
  2. It converts light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose.
  3. It is the same process as fermentation.
  4. It only occurs at night.

Photosynthesis absorbs light energy (endothermic) and produces glucose and O₂.

A food contains 10 g fat and 10 g protein. Its approximate energy content is

  1. 200 kJ
  2. 370 kJ
  3. 170 kJ
  4. 540 kJ

\(10 \times 37 + 10 \times 17 = 540\text{ kJ}\).

Which statement about aerobic respiration and the combustion of glucose is correct?

  1. Respiration releases more energy per mole of glucose
  2. Combustion is catalysed by enzymes
  3. They have the same overall equation and ΔH, but respiration happens in many controlled steps
  4. Respiration produces carbon monoxide

Same reactants and products, so the same ΔH (Hess's law); the pathway is different.

Bioethanol from sugar cane is described as renewable but not always carbon neutral, mainly because

  1. fossil fuels may be used in farming, fertiliser production, distillation and transport
  2. burning ethanol produces no CO₂
  3. fermentation absorbs CO₂
  4. ethanol is made from crude oil

The CO₂ from burning was recently absorbed by the crop, but fossil-fuel energy used in production adds extra CO₂.

Per gram, fats release about twice as much energy as carbohydrates when oxidised. The best explanation is that fats

  1. contain more oxygen atoms per carbon
  2. are digested more slowly
  3. contain nitrogen
  4. contain far fewer oxygen atoms relative to carbon and hydrogen, so their carbon is less oxidised to start with

Carbohydrates are already partly oxidised (lots of C–O and O–H); fats are mostly C–H and C–C, so more energy is released forming CO₂ and H₂O.

A 250 mL sports drink contains 15 g of sugar (carbohydrate) and no fat or protein. (a) Calculate its energy content. (b) How long would that energy last a person using 450 kJ per hour while walking?

(a) \(15 \times 16 = 240\text{ kJ}\). [1]

(b) \(\frac{240}{450} = 0.533\text{ h} \approx 32\text{ min}\). [2]

Outline how bioethanol is produced from wheat starch, including two equations, and give one advantage and one disadvantage of bioethanol compared with petrol.

Starch is hydrolysed to glucose: \(\ce{(C6H10O5)_n + nH2O -> nC6H12O6}\). [1] Yeast ferments glucose anaerobically, then the mixture is distilled to concentrate the ethanol: \(\ce{C6H12O6 -> 2C2H5OH + 2CO2}\). [1] Advantage: renewable; the CO₂ released on combustion was recently absorbed by the crop. [1] Disadvantage (any one): lower energy per gram and per litre than petrol; uses farmland that could grow food; energy-intensive distillation. [1]

Summary

  • Photosynthesis stores light energy as chemical energy in glucose; respiration releases it (same |ΔH|).
  • Energy content: carbohydrate 16, protein 17, fat 37 kJ g⁻¹.
  • Bioethanol: hydrolysis of starch → fermentation of glucose (anaerobic) → distillation.
