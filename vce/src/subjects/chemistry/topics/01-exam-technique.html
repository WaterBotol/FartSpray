Calculations

  • Write each step with its formula: \(n = \frac{m}{M}\), \(n = cV\), \(Q = It\), \(q = mc\Delta T\). Method marks are awarded even if the final number is wrong.
  • Carry unrounded values through multi-step calculations; round only the final answer.
  • Significant figures: give the answer to the same number of significant figures as the least precise data value (usually 3). A final answer with 7 sig figs can cost a mark.
  • Units on every final answer, and on \(K\) when asked.
  • Use data-book values (molar volume 24.8 L mol⁻¹ at SLC, \(c\) of water 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹, \(F = 96\,500\)).

Equations

  • States are expected in almost every equation: (s), (l), (g), (aq).
  • Half-equations must balance atoms and charge, with electrons on the correct side. Use H⁺ in acidic and OH⁻ in alkaline conditions, never both.
  • Overall redox equations have no electrons left over.
  • Equilibrium arrows (⇌) for reversible reactions such as esterification and gas equilibria.
  • Organic equations need semi-structural formulas when a specific isomer matters.

Explanations that score

Change → particle-level cause → consequence. Example: “Increasing the temperature increases the average kinetic energy of the particles, so a greater proportion of collisions have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy; the frequency of successful collisions increases, so the rate increases.”

  • Avoid vague words: “it reacts better”, “more energy”, “the equilibrium moves”. Say what moves, which way and why.
  • Le Chatelier questions: state the direction of the shift and the reason (fewer gas particles, endothermic direction, \(Q < K\)).

Experimental design and data

TermMeaning
Independent variableWhat you deliberately change
Dependent variableWhat you measure
Controlled variablesKept the same so the test is fair (valid)
AccuracyHow close a result is to the true value
PrecisionHow close repeated results are to each other
Repeatability / reproducibilitySame results when repeated by you / by others with different equipment
Systematic errorConsistently shifts results one way (e.g. heat loss, uncalibrated balance); not fixed by repeats
Random errorUnpredictable scatter; reduced by repeating and averaging
ValidityDoes the experiment actually test the aim (with controlled variables)?

Errors examiners' reports mention every year

  • Missing or wrong states in equations.
  • Half-equations with unbalanced charge, or with H⁺ in an alkaline cell.
  • Anode and cathode polarity swapped between galvanic and electrolytic cells.
  • Using moles instead of concentration in \(K\) (when volumes don't cancel).
  • Saying a catalyst increases yield, or that temperature changes \(K\) “because of Le Chatelier” without direction.
  • Forgetting the dilution factor or aliquot ratio in titrations.
  • Using minutes instead of seconds in \(Q = It\).
  • Naming esters the wrong way round.
  • Stating “heat loss” without saying how it affects the result.
