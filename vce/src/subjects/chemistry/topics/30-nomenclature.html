Functional groups and homologous series

A homologous series is a family with the same functional group and general formula, where each member differs by \(\ce{CH2}\). Chain-length prefixes: meth (1), eth (2), prop (3), but (4), pent (5), hex (6), hept (7), oct (8).

Alcohols are primary, secondary or tertiary depending on how many carbons are bonded to the C–OH carbon (1, 2 or 3). This decides how they oxidise (see organic reactions).

Naming rules

  1. Find the longest carbon chain containing the main functional group. That's the parent name.
  2. Number from the end that gives the main functional group the lowest locant; then give the lowest numbers to multiple bonds and substituents.
  3. Name substituents (methyl, ethyl, chloro…) as prefixes in alphabetical order, with di-, tri- for repeats (ignored when alphabetising).
  4. Use commas between numbers and hyphens between numbers and letters: 2,3-dimethylbutane.
Priority (highest first) for the suffix
carboxylic acid > ester > amide > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > amine > alkene/alkyne; halo and alkyl groups are always prefixes

Name \(\ce{CH3CH2CH(CH2CH3)CH3}\), which a student called “2-ethylbutane”.

IUPAC names are based on the longest continuous carbon chain, which may bend through what looks like a branch. Number from the end that gives the substituents the lowest numbers.

Trace the longest chain: going through the ethyl branch gives \(\ce{CH3CH2-CH(CH3)-CH2CH3}\): five carbons with a methyl on C3.

3-methylpentane. An ethyl group on C2 always means the longest chain was missed.

Name \(\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH2COOH}\).

When a molecule has two functional groups, the higher-priority group (here the carboxylic acid) becomes the suffix and takes carbon 1; the other group becomes a prefix.

Carboxylic acid has priority: it's the suffix, and its carbon is C1. Chain of 4 carbons: butanoic acid.

The OH is on C3, as a prefix: 3-hydroxybutanoic acid.

Name the ester \(\ce{CH3CH2COOCH3}\), and the alcohol and acid it's made from.

Esters are named alcohol part first (as an alkyl group) and acid part second (ending in -oate). The part containing the C=O came from the acid.

Split at the single-bonded O of the ester link: \(\ce{CH3CH2CO-}\) | \(\ce{-OCH3}\). The part with C=O came from the acid (propanoic acid); the other part came from the alcohol (methanol).

Methyl propanoate: alcohol part first as “alkyl”, acid part second as “-oate”. Made from methanol + propanoic acid.

  • Esters are named backwards from how they're usually written: \(\ce{CH3COOCH2CH3}\) is ethyl ethanoate, while \(\ce{HCOOCH2CH2CH3}\) is propyl methanoate.
  • The carbon in –COOH, –CHO or –CONH₂ is C1 and counts in the chain length.
  • Propan-2-ol, not 2-propanol (current IUPAC puts the locant just before the suffix).
  • Aldehydes and carboxylic acids need no locant (always C1). Ketones do from pentan-2-one onward.

Practice

The IUPAC name of \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2CHO}\) is

  1. butan-1-ol
  2. butanone
  3. butanal
  4. propanal

Four carbons including the CHO carbon; aldehyde suffix -al.

The ester formed from ethanol and butanoic acid is

  1. butyl ethanoate
  2. ethyl butanoate
  3. ethyl butanol
  4. butanoyl ethanoate

Alcohol part (ethyl) + acid part (butanoate).

The correct IUPAC name for \(\ce{CH3CH(CH3)CH(CH3)CH3}\) is

  1. 2-methyl-3-methylbutane
  2. 1,2,3-trimethylpropane
  3. hexane
  4. 2,3-dimethylbutane

Longest chain 4 C, methyls on C2 and C3: 2,3-dimethylbutane (six carbons in total, but not a straight chain, so it isn't hexane).

Which is a secondary alcohol?

  1. \(\ce{CH3CH(OH)CH2CH3}\)
  2. \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2CH2OH}\)
  3. \(\ce{(CH3)3COH}\)
  4. \(\ce{CH3OH}\)

In butan-2-ol the C–OH carbon is bonded to two other carbons. C is tertiary; B is primary.

\(\ce{CH2=CHCH2CH2OH}\) is named

  1. but-1-en-4-ol
  2. but-3-ene-1-ol
  3. but-3-en-1-ol
  4. 1-hydroxybut-3-ene

Alcohol has priority: number from the OH end (C1), double bond between C3 and C4: but-3-en-1-ol (the “e” of -ene is dropped before a vowel).

What is the molecular formula of hexanoic acid?

  1. C₆H₁₂O
  2. C₆H₁₂O₂
  3. C₆H₁₄O₂
  4. C₆H₁₀O₂

\(\ce{CH3(CH2)4COOH}\): 6 C, 12 H, 2 O. General formula \(\ce{C_nH_{2n}O2}\).

Name: (a) \(\ce{CH3CHBrCH2CH3}\); (b) \(\ce{CH3COCH2CH2CH3}\); (c) \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2NH2}\).

(a) 2-bromobutane. (b) pentan-2-one. (c) propan-1-amine (propanamine is also accepted).

Draw (as a semi-structural formula) and name the ester formed from propan-1-ol and ethanoic acid.

\(\ce{CH3COOCH2CH2CH3}\): propyl ethanoate.

A compound has the molecular formula C₄H₈O. Give the semi-structural formula and name of (a) an aldehyde, (b) a ketone and (c) an unsaturated alcohol with this formula.

(a) \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2CHO}\) butanal (or 2-methylpropanal). (b) \(\ce{CH3COCH2CH3}\) butanone. (c) e.g. \(\ce{CH2=CHCH2CH2OH}\) but-3-en-1-ol.

A student names \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2COOCH2CH3}\) as “butyl ethanoate”. Correct them.

The acid part contains the C=O: \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2CO-}\) (4 C, butanoate). The alcohol part is \(\ce{-CH2CH3}\) (ethyl). Correct name: ethyl butanoate.

Summary

  • Longest chain containing the main group; lowest locant for the main group.
  • Priority for the suffix: acid > ester > amide > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > amine.
  • Esters: alkyl (from the alcohol) + alkanoate (from the acid).
