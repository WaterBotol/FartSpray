What you have to do

In Unit 4 you design and carry out your own investigation linked to the sustainable production of energy and/or materials, then communicate it in a scientific poster. It draws on the key science skills and on the Unit 3 and 4 content (calorimetry, electrochemistry, rates and yield, organic synthesis and analysis). Check your school's task sheet for its exact requirements.

  • Logbook: a dated record of planning, risk assessment, raw data, observations and changes to the method, kept as you go. It authenticates your work.
  • Poster: VCAA provides a poster template with a word limit (around 600 words); check your school's version for the exact requirements.
  • The sustainability link should be real, not tacked on: your discussion should say what your results mean for producing energy or materials more efficiently, with less waste, or from renewable feedstocks.

Your teacher must approve the question and your risk assessment before you start. Some ideas (flames, electrolysis producing H₂, KOH) need extra safety controls.

Plan an investigation into how the chain length of primary alcohols (methanol to butan-1-ol) affects their molar heat of combustion. Include the hypothesis, variables, method outline, data analysis, one safety consideration, the main sources of error and the sustainability link.

Hypothesis: “If the carbon chain is longer, then the molar heat of combustion will be larger, increasing by a roughly constant amount per CH₂ unit, because each extra CH₂ produces one more CO₂ and H₂O.”

Variables: IV = alcohol (1–4 carbons). DV = temperature rise of the water, converted to kJ mol⁻¹. Controlled: 100.0 g of water in the same can, same distance from wick to can, same starting temperature, same temperature rise (e.g. 20 °C) for each fuel, draught shield.

Method outline: weigh the spirit burner, heat the water by 20 °C, reweigh; three trials per alcohol. Calculate \(q = mc\Delta T\), then \(n = m/M\) and ΔH = −q/n.

Analysis: plot ΔH (kJ mol⁻¹) against the number of carbons; expect a straight line. Compare each value with the data book (% difference) and compare the gradient with the theoretical increase per CH₂ (about 650 kJ mol⁻¹).

Safety: alcohols are flammable and methanol is toxic: no refilling near flames, fume cupboard or well-ventilated room, safety glasses, heat-proof mat.

Errors: heat loss to the surroundings and incomplete combustion (systematic; |ΔH| too small); evaporation of alcohol from the wick before reweighing (systematic); reading the thermometer (random, reduced by repeats and a finer-resolution probe).

Sustainability link: compare the energy released per gram of fuel and per gram of CO₂ produced, and discuss which alcohols can be made from renewable biomass (e.g. bioethanol, biobutanol). This turns a standard experiment into an answer about sustainable fuels.

Writing a strong discussion

  1. Describe the trend in the processed data, with values.
  2. Explain it with chemistry (bonding, equations, collision theory, equilibrium).
  3. Compare with accepted or theoretical values: % difference, % yield or current efficiency.
  4. Evaluate the data: precision (spread of repeats), accuracy, specific random and systematic errors and the direction of their effects, outliers.
  5. Validity and limitations: were relevant variables controlled? Is the range of data enough for a general conclusion?
  6. Improvements and extensions that target the errors you identified, plus a follow-up question.
  7. Sustainability: what do the results suggest about producing energy or materials more efficiently or with less environmental impact?

“Do more trials” only helps with random error. If the main problem was heat loss (systematic), say how to reduce it (insulation, lid, calibrated calorimeter) or account for it. Generic improvements earn little credit.

Practice

Which statement is most appropriate for the conclusion of a chemistry poster?

  1. “The experiment proved that longer alcohols release more energy.”
  2. “The results were affected by human error.”
  3. “Molar heat of combustion increased by about 540 kJ mol⁻¹ per CH₂ unit, supporting the hypothesis, although all values were 15–20% below data-book values.”
  4. “Butanol is the best fuel.”

Evidence-based, uses “supporting”, quantifies the result and acknowledges the limitation.

The main purpose of the logbook is to

  1. provide a dated record of planning, raw data and changes that authenticates the student's work
  2. replace the poster
  3. list references only
  4. record the final conclusion

The logbook is the ongoing evidence that the investigation is genuinely yours.

A student measures the current efficiency of a water electrolyser as 118%. The most likely explanation is

  1. the electrolyser produced extra energy
  2. random error in the timer
  3. the gas was collected at a lower temperature than SLC
  4. a systematic error, such as the ammeter under-reading or gas volumes read at a higher temperature than assumed

Efficiency can't exceed 100%, so a systematic error made the measured gas appear larger than theory (or the charge smaller). C would make the volume smaller, not larger.

A student investigates how the mass of KOH catalyst affects the percentage yield of biodiesel from 50.0 g of canola oil. Identify the independent, dependent and two controlled variables, and suggest one way the student could confirm the product is biodiesel.

IV: mass of KOH catalyst. DV: percentage yield of methyl esters (biodiesel). [1]

Controlled (any two): mass and type of oil, volume of methanol, reaction temperature, reaction time, stirring rate, method of separating the glycerol layer. [2]

Confirmation: e.g. IR spectrum showing an ester C=O near 1740 cm⁻¹ and C–O, with no broad O–H; or comparing its properties (viscosity, density) with a biodiesel standard. [1]

Summary

  • The investigation must link to the sustainable production of energy and/or materials.
  • Logbook authenticates; poster communicates (title, communication statement, introduction, methodology, results, discussion, conclusion, references).
  • Discussions compare with theory, evaluate specific errors, and connect back to sustainability.
