The method

Faraday's laws in three steps
\[ Q = It \quad\longrightarrow\quad n(\ce{e-}) = \frac{Q}{F} \quad\longrightarrow\quad n(\text{product}) \text{ via the half-equation} \]

\(Q\) in coulombs, \(I\) in amperes, \(t\) in seconds, \(F = 96\,500\ \text{C mol}^{-1}\) (the charge on one mole of electrons).

Faraday's first law: the amount of product is proportional to the charge passed. Second law: to make one mole of product you need \(n\) moles of electrons, where \(n\) comes from the half-equation (1 for Ag⁺, 2 for Cu²⁺, 3 for Al³⁺, 4 for O₂ from water).

A current of 2.00 A flows through CuSO₄(aq) for 30.0 minutes. What mass of copper is deposited?

\(Q = 2.00 \times 1800 = 3600\text{ C}\); \(n(\ce{e-}) = \frac{3600}{96\,500} = 0.03731\text{ mol}\).

\(\ce{Cu^2+ + 2e- -> Cu}\): \(n(\ce{Cu}) = 0.01865\text{ mol}\).

\(m = 0.01865 \times 63.5 = \) 1.18 g.

How long must a current of 1.50 A flow to deposit 5.00 g of silver?

\(n(\ce{Ag}) = \frac{5.00}{107.9} = 0.04634\text{ mol} = n(\ce{e-})\) (1 : 1).

\(Q = 0.04634 \times 96\,500 = 4472\text{ C}\).

\(t = \frac{4472}{1.50} = 2981\text{ s} = \) 49.7 min.

A current of 1.00 A for 1930 s deposits 0.180 g of a metal from a solution of its M³⁺ ions. Identify the metal.

\(n(\ce{e-}) = \frac{1930}{96\,500} = 0.0200\text{ mol}\).

\(\ce{M^3+ + 3e- -> M}\): \(n(\ce{M}) = \frac{0.0200}{3} = 6.67 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol}\).

\(M = \frac{0.180}{6.67 \times 10^{-3}} = 27.0\ \text{g mol}^{-1}\): aluminium. (In practice Al can't be deposited from water; this is a molten-electrolyte question.)

Two electrolytic cells are connected in series: one contains AgNO₃(aq) and the other CuSO₄(aq). A current of 0.500 A flows for 1.00 hour. Find the mass of metal deposited in each.

In series, the same charge flows through both: \(Q = 1800\text{ C}\), \(n(\ce{e-}) = 0.01865\text{ mol}\).

Ag: \(0.01865 \times 107.9 = 2.01\text{ g}\). Cu: \(\frac{0.01865}{2} \times 63.5 = 0.592\text{ g}\).

2.01 g Ag and 0.592 g Cu. Twice as many moles of silver form, because Ag⁺ needs one electron and Cu²⁺ needs two.

Dilute sulfuric acid is electrolysed at 2.00 A for 30.0 min. Calculate the volumes of H₂ and O₂ at SLC.

\(n(\ce{e-}) = 0.03731\text{ mol}\). Cathode \(\ce{2H+ + 2e- -> H2}\): \(n(\ce{H2}) = 0.01865\text{ mol}\).

Anode \(\ce{2H2O -> O2 + 4H+ + 4e-}\): \(n(\ce{O2}) = 0.009326\text{ mol}\).

H₂: 0.463 L; O₂: 0.231 L. The 2 : 1 ratio matches the formula of water.

  • Time in seconds. Minutes or hours give answers 60 or 3600 times too small.
  • Use the electron ratio from the half-equation, not from the formula of the salt.
  • Series cells share the charge, not the mass or the moles of product.
  • Current efficiency: actual product = theoretical × efficiency (side reactions such as H₂ production waste some charge).

Practice

A current of 3.00 A for 10.0 minutes delivers a charge of

  1. 1800 C
  2. 30.0 C
  3. 0.0187 C
  4. 300 C

\(Q = 3.00 \times 600 = 1800\text{ C}\).

The number of moles of electrons needed to deposit 1.0 mol of chromium from Cr³⁺ ions is

  1. 1.0
  2. 2.0
  3. 3.0
  4. 6.0

\(\ce{Cr^3+ + 3e- -> Cr}\).

The same charge passes through molten NaCl and molten MgCl₂ cells in series. The ratio of moles of Na to moles of Mg produced is

  1. 1 : 1
  2. 2 : 1
  3. 1 : 2
  4. 23 : 24

Na⁺ needs 1 e⁻ and Mg²⁺ needs 2, so the same electrons make twice as many Na atoms.

0.327 g of zinc is deposited by 965 C of charge. The charge on the zinc ion is

  1. +1
  2. +3
  3. +4
  4. +2

\(n(\ce{e-}) = 0.0100\); \(n(\ce{Zn}) = \frac{0.327}{65.4} = 0.00500\). Two electrons per atom: Zn²⁺.

A copper refinery runs at 5.00 A for 2.00 h and deposits 10.9 g of copper. The current efficiency is closest to

  1. 100%
  2. 46%
  3. 92%
  4. 109%

Theoretical: \(\frac{36\,000}{96\,500} \times \frac12 \times 63.5 = 11.8\text{ g}\). \(\frac{10.9}{11.8} \approx 92\%\).

What mass of nickel is deposited from Ni²⁺(aq) by a current of 4.00 A for 45.0 minutes?

\(Q = 4.00 \times 2700 = 10\,800\text{ C}\); \(n(\ce{e-}) = 0.1119\text{ mol}\); \(n(\ce{Ni}) = 0.05596\text{ mol}\); \(m = 0.05596 \times 58.7 = 3.28\text{ g}\).

A car part with a surface area of 250 cm² is chrome-plated to a thickness of 0.0100 mm (density of Cr = 7.19 g cm⁻³) from a Cr(VI) solution (6 electrons per Cr atom). How long does it take at 20.0 A, assuming 100% efficiency?

Volume \(= 250 \times 0.00100 = 0.250\text{ cm}^3\); mass \(= 1.80\text{ g}\); \(n(\ce{Cr}) = \frac{1.80}{52.0} = 0.0346\text{ mol}\). [1]

\(n(\ce{e-}) = 6 \times 0.0346 = 0.2074\text{ mol}\); \(Q = 2.00 \times 10^4\text{ C}\). [1]

\(t = \frac{20\,014}{20.0} \approx 1.00 \times 10^3\text{ s}\) (about 16.7 min). [1]

Green hydrogen is made by electrolysis of water. An electrolyser runs at 150 A for 8.00 hours. (a) Calculate the mass of H₂ produced. (b) If the cell voltage is 1.90 V, calculate the electrical energy used, and the percentage of it stored in the hydrogen (\(\Delta H_c(\ce{H2}) = -286\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\)).

(a) \(Q = 150 \times 28\,800 = 4.32 \times 10^6\text{ C}\); \(n(\ce{e-}) = 44.77\text{ mol}\); \(n(\ce{H2}) = 22.38\text{ mol}\); \(m = 44.8\text{ g}\). [2]

(b) \(E = VIt = 1.90 \times 150 \times 28\,800 = 8.21 \times 10^6\text{ J} = 8210\text{ kJ}\). Stored: \(22.38 \times 286 = 6401\text{ kJ}\). Efficiency \(\approx 78\%\). [2]

A student calculates the mass of copper deposited by 2.00 A in 30 minutes as \(\frac{2.00 \times 30}{96\,500} \times 63.5\). Identify two errors.

Time must be in seconds (1800 s, not 30). The moles of electrons must be halved because Cu²⁺ needs 2 electrons per Cu atom. Correct: \(\frac{2.00 \times 1800}{96\,500} \times \frac12 \times 63.5 = 1.18\text{ g}\).

Summary

  • \(Q = It\) (seconds!), \(n(\ce{e-}) = \frac{Q}{F}\), then use the half-equation's electron ratio.
  • Cells in series pass the same charge.
  • Work backwards to find a charge, molar mass, time or current.
