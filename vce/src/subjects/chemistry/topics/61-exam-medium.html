Water is added to an equilibrium mixture of \(\ce{Fe^3+(aq) + SCN-(aq) <=> FeSCN^2+(aq)}\) (deep red). When equilibrium is re-established,

  1. the equilibrium has shifted right and the colour is darker
  2. the equilibrium has shifted left and the colour is lighter
  3. there is no shift, but the colour is lighter
  4. the equilibrium has shifted left and the colour is darker

Argon is added to an equilibrium mixture of \(\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}\) in a sealed, rigid container. The equilibrium

  1. shifts right, because the total pressure increases
  2. shifts left, because the total pressure increases
  3. does not shift, because the concentrations of the reacting gases are unchanged
  4. does not shift, because argon lowers K

At constant volume, adding an inert gas doesn’t change any reacting concentration, so Q still equals K.

State the reagent and condition for converting propane to 1-chloropropane, and name the type of reaction.

  • Cl₂ with UV light
  • Substitution

Chlorine gas with UV light. This is a substitution reaction: one H is replaced by Cl, and HCl also forms.

Write an equation for the conversion of 1-chloropropane to propan-1-ol.

  • \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2Cl + OH- -> CH3CH2CH2OH + Cl-}\) (NaOH(aq))

\(\ce{CH3CH2CH2Cl + NaOH -> CH3CH2CH2OH + NaCl}\)

Name a reagent for converting propan-1-ol to propanoic acid and describe what would be observed.

  • Acidified potassium dichromate (or acidified permanganate)
  • Orange → green (or purple → colourless)

Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ changes from orange to green (Cr₂O₇²⁻ → Cr³⁺). Acidified KMnO₄ changes from purple to colourless.

Give the semi-structural formula of methyl propanoate, and name the catalyst for the final step.

  • CH₃CH₂COOCH₃
  • Concentrated sulfuric acid

CH₃CH₂COOCH₃, made using a concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst.

CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ (ethyl ethanoate) was a common wrong answer. The acid provides the C=O end.

Explain why the first step gives a low yield of 1-chloropropane.

  • A mixture forms: 2-chloropropane and multiply substituted products (dichloropropanes etc.)

Substitution is unselective. It produces 2-chloropropane and multiply chlorinated products as well.

The yields of the four steps are 60%, 85%, 70% and 80%. Calculate the mass of methyl propanoate (M = 88.0 g mol⁻¹) obtained from 44.0 g of propane.

  • Overall yield \(= 0.60 \times 0.85 \times 0.70 \times 0.80 = 0.286\) (28.6%)
  • \(1.00\ \text{mol} \times 0.286 \times 88.0 = 25.1\ \text{g}\)

44.0 g of propane is 1.00 mol. Overall yield 28.6%, so 0.286 mol of ester, or \(25.1\ \text{g}\).

Adding or averaging the yields instead of multiplying them was very common.

An unknown compound Y has the molecular formula C₄H₈O.

  • IR: strong, sharp absorption at 1715 cm⁻¹; no absorption between 2500 and 3550 cm⁻¹.
  • Mass spectrum: molecular ion at m/z = 72, with major peaks at m/z = 57 and 43.
  • ¹³C NMR: four signals, one of them at 209 ppm.
  • ¹H NMR: 1.0 ppm (3H, triplet); 2.1 ppm (3H, singlet); 2.4 ppm (2H, quartet).

Explain what the IR spectrum shows about the functional groups in Y.

  • 1715 cm⁻¹: C=O present
  • No broad O–H band, so Y is not an alcohol or carboxylic acid

The strong band at 1715 cm⁻¹ shows C=O. The absence of any broad band at 2500–3550 cm⁻¹ rules out O–H, so Y is not an alcohol or carboxylic acid.

Give the formula of the fragment responsible for the peak at m/z = 43.

  • [CH₃CO]⁺ (accept [C₃H₇]⁺ if justified)

[CH₃CO]⁺ (15 + 28 = 43). The peak at 57 is [CH₃CH₂CO]⁺.

What does the ¹³C NMR spectrum tell you about the carbon atoms in Y?

  • All four carbons are in different environments (one is a C=O, at 209 ppm)

All four carbons are in different chemical environments. The signal at 209 ppm is a ketone or aldehyde C=O.

Assign each ¹H NMR signal to a group of hydrogens in Y, explaining the splitting.

  • 2.1 ppm singlet: CH₃ attached to C=O, with no neighbouring H
  • 2.4 ppm quartet: CH₂ next to C=O, split by the 3 H of the adjacent CH₃
  • 1.0 ppm triplet: CH₃ split by the 2 H of the adjacent CH₂

2.1 ppm, singlet: CH₃–C=O with no neighbouring H. 2.4 ppm, quartet: –CH₂– next to C=O, with 3 neighbours. 1.0 ppm, triplet: –CH₃ with 2 neighbours.

Give the name of compound Y.

  • Butanone (CH₃COCH₂CH₃)

Butanone, CH₃COCH₂CH₃.

Butanal is an isomer of Y. State one feature of its ¹H NMR spectrum that would distinguish it from Y.

  • An aldehyde H signal at about 9–10 ppm

A signal at about 9.5–10 ppm from the aldehyde –CHO hydrogen.

An iron tablet is dissolved in dilute sulfuric acid, and the solution is made up to 250.0 mL. 25.00 mL aliquots are titrated with 0.00200 M KMnO₄. The average concordant titre is 15.60 mL.
\(\ce{MnO4-(aq) + 8H+(aq) + 5Fe^2+(aq) -> Mn^2+(aq) + 5Fe^3+(aq) + 4H2O(l)}\)
E°: MnO₄⁻/Mn²⁺ +1.51 V; Cl₂/Cl⁻ +1.36 V; Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ +0.77 V.

Calculate the amount of MnO₄⁻ in the average titre.

  • \(3.12 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{mol}\)

\(0.00200 \times 0.01560 = 3.12 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{mol}\).

Calculate the amount of Fe²⁺ in each aliquot.

  • \(1.56 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{mol}\) (× 5)

\(5 \times 3.12 \times 10^{-5} = 1.56 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{mol}\).

Calculate the mass of iron in the tablet, in mg.

  • \(n(\ce{Fe})\) in the tablet \(= 10 \times 1.56 \times 10^{-4} = 1.56 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}\)
  • \(m = 87.0\ \text{mg}\)

\(1.56 \times 10^{-3} \times 55.8 = 0.0870\ \text{g} = 87.0\ \text{mg}\).

Explain why no separate indicator is needed.

  • MnO₄⁻ is purple and Mn²⁺ is almost colourless, so the first permanent pink colour marks the end point

MnO₄⁻ is intensely purple, while the products are almost colourless. The first excess drop gives a permanent pale pink, which marks the end point.

Explain why the tablet is dissolved in sulfuric acid rather than hydrochloric acid.

  • MnO₄⁻ (+1.51 V) can oxidise Cl⁻ (+1.36 V), which would use up extra permanganate and give a titre that is too high

Permanganate is a strong enough oxidant to oxidise Cl⁻ ions. Some titrant would be used up this way, making the titre, and the calculated iron content, too high.

The label states 80 mg of iron per tablet. Suggest one procedural error that would make the student’s result too high.

  • e.g. the burette was rinsed with water (diluting the KMnO₄, so the titre is too large), or the end point was overshot

For example, rinsing the burette with water dilutes the KMnO₄, so more is needed and the titre is too large. Overshooting the end point has the same effect.

A student investigates how temperature affects the activity of the enzyme catalase, which decomposes hydrogen peroxide: \(\ce{2H2O2(aq) -> 2H2O(l) + O2(g)}\). They measure the volume of O₂ collected in 60 s at 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 °C. The volume increases from 20 °C to a maximum at 40 °C, then falls sharply.

Identify the independent and dependent variables.

  • Independent: temperature; dependent: volume of O₂ collected in 60 s (the rate)

Independent: temperature. Dependent: volume of O₂ collected in 60 s.

State two variables that should be controlled.

  • Any two of: concentration/volume of H₂O₂, amount/concentration of catalase, pH, collection time

For example, the volume and concentration of H₂O₂, and the amount of catalase (and the pH).

Explain the shape of the results.

  • Up to 40 °C: more kinetic energy, so more frequent successful collisions between substrate and active site
  • Above 40 °C: the enzyme is denatured (tertiary structure disrupted, active site changes shape), so activity falls

Below the optimum, higher temperature means more frequent and more energetic collisions between H₂O₂ and the active site. Above about 40 °C the tertiary structure is disrupted and the active site changes shape (denaturation), so activity drops sharply.

Suggest how the student could improve the precision of the results.

  • Repeat each temperature several times and check the results are close together (and average them)

Carry out repeat trials at each temperature and check that they agree closely. Precision is about how close repeated measurements are to each other.

Many said “use more temperatures”. That improves resolution around the optimum, not precision.

Identify one safety hazard in this investigation and a way of managing it.

  • e.g. H₂O₂ is an irritant/oxidiser: wear safety glasses and gloves; or hot water baths: handle with care

Hydrogen peroxide irritates the skin and eyes, so wear safety glasses and gloves.

Assessor’s report

General comments

This paper matched the demand of a typical VCAA Chemistry exam. Most Section B questions needed at least two linked steps: a stoichiometric ratio followed by a conversion, or a principle followed by its application. Students who set out calculations as a chain of labelled amounts (“\(n(\ce{MnO4-}) = \dots\)”, “\(n(\ce{Fe^2+}) = 5 \times \dots\)”) rarely lost marks, even when an arithmetic slip crept in.

Question-type patterns

  • Comparing fuels fairly. Question 1a asked for CO₂ per unit of energy. Match the basis of your comparison to the question: per gram, per mole or per MJ.
  • Equilibrium tables. The initial–change–equilibrium method with the stoichiometric ratio (Question 5b) is examined every year. Label each row and convert amounts to concentrations.
  • Two-part observation questions. Question 6a wanted the instant change and the subsequent shift. Say both.
  • Rechargeable cells. The same electrode is the cathode during discharge and the anode during recharge. “Positive to positive” gets you the connection.
  • Spectroscopy synthesis. Question 9 rewarded students who used every piece of data. The singlet at 2.1 ppm is the giveaway for CH₃ next to C=O.

Common errors

  • Adding or averaging percentage yields instead of multiplying them.
  • Using the 1 : 1 ratio when it should have been 1 : 2 (H₂ in 5b) or 1 : 5 (MnO₄⁻ : Fe²⁺).
  • Confusing precision with accuracy or with resolution in the investigation question.
  • Writing H⁺ in alkaline half-equations and OH⁻ in acidic ones.

Advice

Aim for 75% or more before the harder paper. If Section A went well but Section B didn’t, practise writing full, labelled calculation chains and two-part explanations (what happens + why).
