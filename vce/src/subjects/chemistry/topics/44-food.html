The big idea: condensation and hydrolysis

All three major food molecules are built from small units joined by condensation reactions (water released) and broken down in digestion by hydrolysis (water added, catalysed by enzymes).

Carbohydrates

  • Monosaccharides: glucose and fructose are isomers (C₆H₁₂O₆).
  • Disaccharides: two monosaccharides + a glycosidic link, losing H₂O: sucrose (glucose + fructose), lactose (glucose + galactose), maltose (glucose + glucose). Formula C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁.
  • Polysaccharides (condensation polymers of glucose): starch (plant energy store: amylose unbranched, amylopectin branched), glycogen (animal energy store, highly branched), cellulose (plant cell walls; humans lack the enzyme to hydrolyse its links, so it's dietary fibre).
Condensation and hydrolysis of a disaccharide
\[ \ce{C6H12O6 + C6H12O6 <=>[\text{condensation}][\text{hydrolysis}] C12H22O11 + H2O} \]

Proteins

2-amino acids have the general formula \(\ce{H2N-CH(R)-COOH}\). The R group (side chain) differs between amino acids and can be non-polar, polar, acidic or basic. At the pH of body fluids they exist as zwitterions: \(\ce{H3N+-CH(R)-COO-}\).

LevelWhat it isHeld together by
PrimarySequence of amino acidsCovalent peptide bonds
SecondaryLocal folding: α-helices and β-pleated sheetsHydrogen bonds between C=O and N–H of the backbone
TertiaryOverall 3D shape of one chainR-group interactions: hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges (covalent S–S), dispersion forces
QuaternarySeveral chains together (e.g. haemoglobin)The same interactions, between chains

Denaturation (heat, pH change, heavy metals) disrupts the secondary/tertiary structure, so the protein loses its shape and function, but the peptide bonds (primary structure) stay intact. Hydrolysis breaks peptide bonds into amino acids. Cooking an egg denatures its protein; digestion hydrolyses it.

Show the condensation of glycine (\(\ce{H2NCH2COOH}\)) with alanine (\(\ce{H2NCH(CH3)COOH}\)) to form a dipeptide where glycine's COOH reacts.

The COOH of glycine reacts with the NH₂ of alanine, releasing H₂O and forming –CO–NH–.

\(\ce{H2NCH2COOH + H2NCH(CH3)COOH -> H2NCH2CONHCH(CH3)COOH + H2O}\). The other order (Ala–Gly) gives a different dipeptide: sequence matters.

Fats and oils

A triglyceride is a triester of glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol) with three fatty acids (long-chain carboxylic acids).

  • Saturated fatty acids (no C=C, e.g. stearic acid C₁₇H₃₅COOH) pack closely: stronger dispersion forces, so they're solids at room temperature (fats).
  • Unsaturated (one C=C: mono; several: poly) have cis kinks that stop close packing, so they're liquids (oils). Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids are polyunsaturated, with the first C=C at the 3rd or 6th carbon from the CH₃ end.
  • Iodine number: the mass of I₂ (g) that reacts with 100 g of fat, since I₂ adds across each C=C. A higher iodine number means more unsaturated.

(a) How many C=C bonds are in linoleic acid, C₁₇H₃₁COOH? (b) What mass of iodine reacts with 10.0 g of triolein, C₅₇H₁₀₄O₆ (\(M = 884\)), which has 3 C=C per molecule?

(a) A saturated C₁₈ acid is C₁₇H₃₅COOH. Linoleic has 4 fewer H, so it has 2 C=C (each C=C removes 2 H).

(b) \(n(\text{triolein}) = \frac{10.0}{884} = 0.01131\text{ mol}\), so \(n(\ce{I2}) = 3 \times 0.01131 = 0.03394\text{ mol}\).

\(m(\ce{I2}) = 0.03394 \times 253.8 = 8.61\text{ g}\) (iodine number ≈ 86).

Many vitamins act as coenzymes (see enzymes).

  • Denaturation doesn't break peptide bonds.
  • Disulfide bridges are covalent but count as tertiary structure (they join R groups).
  • \(n\) monomers joined in a chain release \(n - 1\) water molecules.
  • Cellulose and starch are both glucose polymers; they differ in how the glucose units are linked.
  • Unsaturated ≠ unhealthy/healthy by definition. Answer in terms of structure and properties.

Olive oil is a liquid at room temperature, whereas butter fat is solid, mainly because olive oil's fatty acids

  1. are shorter
  2. contain more oxygen
  3. contain cis C=C bonds that prevent close packing

Kinks from cis double bonds reduce contact between chains, so the dispersion forces are weaker and the melting point lower.

Write the equation for the hydrolysis of sucrose and name the products.

\(\ce{C12H22O11 + H2O -> C6H12O6 + C6H12O6}\): glucose and fructose (catalysed by sucrase or acid).

Explain, with reference to bonding, why the tertiary structure of a protein depends on its primary structure.

The primary structure fixes which R groups are present and where they are along the chain. [1] Tertiary structure forms from interactions between R groups (hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds between charged R groups, disulfide bridges between cysteines, dispersion forces between non-polar R groups). [1] So the sequence determines which interactions are possible and therefore the 3D fold; changing one amino acid can change the shape. [1]

A triglyceride is made from glycerol and three molecules of oleic acid, C₁₇H₃₃COOH. (a) Name the type of reaction and the functional group formed. (b) How many molecules of water are released? (c) Calculate the molar mass of the triglyceride. (d) How many moles of H₂ are needed to fully hydrogenate 1 mol of it?

(a) Condensation (esterification); ester groups. [1]

(b) 3 water molecules. [1]

(c) Glycerol C₃H₈O₃ (92.0) + 3 × oleic acid C₁₈H₃₄O₂ (282.0) − 3 × H₂O (18.0) = 92.0 + 846.0 − 54.0 = 884 g mol⁻¹ (C₅₇H₁₀₄O₆). [1]

(d) Each oleic acid has 1 C=C (C₁₇H₃₃ vs saturated C₁₇H₃₅), so 3 mol H₂. [1]

A student says: “Cooking breaks proteins into amino acids, so cooked meat is already digested.” Correct this.

Cooking denatures proteins: it disrupts hydrogen bonds and other interactions (secondary/tertiary structure) but doesn't break the covalent peptide bonds. Hydrolysis into amino acids still needs protease enzymes in digestion. (Denatured proteins are usually easier for proteases to reach, though.)

Summary

  • Condensation builds (glycosidic, peptide, ester links); hydrolysis breaks.
  • Protein structure: primary (sequence) → secondary (backbone H-bonds) → tertiary (R-group interactions) → quaternary.
  • Saturated fats pack closely (solid); cis-unsaturated oils don't (liquid).
  • Water-soluble vitamins: polar, many OH, not stored. Fat-soluble: non-polar, stored.
