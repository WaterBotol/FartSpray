What makes something a fuel?

A fuel is a substance that releases useful energy when it reacts, usually by combustion with oxygen. Fuels are compared on energy content (per gram, per mole or per litre), emissions (CO₂, CO, soot, SO₂, NOₓ), renewability, cost and practicality (storage, transport, safety).

Fossil fuels vs biofuels

Fossil fuels (coal, crude oil, natural gas)Biofuels (biogas, bioethanol, biodiesel)
OriginRemains of organisms buried and transformed by heat and pressure over millions of yearsRecently living biomass (crops, waste, algae, manure)
Renewable?No: used far faster than they formYes: can be replaced within a human timescale
CarbonReleases carbon locked away for millions of years: a net increase in atmospheric CO₂CO₂ released was recently absorbed by photosynthesis, so it's close to carbon neutral (but not fully: farming, processing and transport use energy)
IssuesFinite; SO₂ from sulfur impurities (especially coal); mining impactsLand and water use; competition with food crops; lower energy per gram (ethanol) or higher viscosity (biodiesel)

How the biofuels are made

  • Biogas (mainly methane and CO₂): anaerobic digestion of organic waste by bacteria.
  • Bioethanol: yeast fermentation of glucose from sugar or starch crops, then distillation: \(\ce{C6H12O6(aq) -> 2C2H5OH(aq) + 2CO2(g)}\).
  • Biodiesel: transesterification of a triglyceride (fat or oil) with methanol, using a base catalyst (e.g. KOH). The products are methyl esters of the fatty acids (the biodiesel) and glycerol.

Biodiesel and petrodiesel are chemically different (esters vs hydrocarbons) but have similar chain lengths, so they have similar properties. Biodiesel's ester group makes it slightly more polar: it absorbs more water and is more viscous, especially in the cold.

Combustion

Complete vs incomplete combustion of octane
\[ \ce{2C8H18(l) + 25O2(g) -> 16CO2(g) + 18H2O(g)} \]\[ \ce{2C8H18(l) + 17O2(g) -> 16CO(g) + 18H2O(g)} \qquad \ce{2C8H18(l) + 9O2(g) -> 16C(s) + 18H2O(g)} \]

With limited oxygen, carbon ends up as toxic carbon monoxide or soot (C). Incomplete combustion releases less energy per mole of fuel.

Balance C first, then H, then O last. If you need an odd number of O atoms, use a fraction for O₂ and then double everything. Include states: water is usually (g) in engines and (l) in the data book's heats of combustion. Read which one the question wants.

Calculate the mass of CO₂ produced by the complete combustion of 1.00 kg of octane (\(M = 114.0\text{ g mol}^{-1}\)).

\(n(\ce{C8H18}) = \dfrac{1000}{114.0} = 8.772\text{ mol}\).

Mole ratio \(\ce{C8H18 : CO2} = 2 : 16 = 1 : 8\), so \(n(\ce{CO2}) = 70.18\text{ mol}\).

\(m(\ce{CO2}) = 70.18 \times 44.0 = 3088\text{ g} \approx\) 3.09 kg. The CO₂ weighs three times as much as the fuel, because each carbon atom picks up two oxygen atoms from the air.

Comparing fuels fairly

Per gram, per mole and per litre can rank fuels differently. Data-book values (heats of combustion, water as liquid):

FuelkJ mol⁻¹kJ g⁻¹g CO₂ per MJ
Hydrogen, H₂2861410
Methane, CH₄89055.649.4
Octane, C₈H₁₈546047.964.5
Ethanol, C₂H₅OH136029.664.7

Ethanol has density 0.789 g mL⁻¹ and octane 0.703 g mL⁻¹. Compare the energy released by complete combustion of 1.00 L of each.

Ethanol: \(789\text{ g} \times 29.6\text{ kJ g}^{-1} = 2.34 \times 10^4\text{ kJ} = 23.4\text{ MJ}\).

Octane: \(703\text{ g} \times 47.9\text{ kJ g}^{-1} = 3.37 \times 10^4\text{ kJ} = 33.7\text{ MJ}\).

Octane gives about 44% more energy per litre, so an ethanol-fuelled car needs a bigger tank for the same range. Ethanol already contains an oxygen atom (it's partly “burnt”), which is why its energy per gram is lower.

  • “Carbon neutral” is an idealisation. Growing, harvesting, fermenting and transporting biofuels usually uses fossil energy.
  • Renewable ≠ produces no CO₂. Biofuels still emit CO₂ when burnt.
  • Per gram vs per mole: octane releases far more per mole than methane (bigger molecule), but less per gram.
  • Biodiesel isn't a hydrocarbon. It's a mixture of esters.

Practice

Which fuel is renewable?

  1. Natural gas
  2. Coal seam gas
  3. Biogas from a landfill
  4. LPG from crude oil

Biogas comes from decomposing recent organic matter. The others are fossil fuels.

The balanced equation for the complete combustion of ethanol is

  1. \(\ce{C2H5OH + 2O2 -> 2CO + 3H2O}\)
  2. \(\ce{C2H5OH + 3O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O}\)
  3. \(\ce{C2H5OH + 3.5O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O}\)
  4. \(\ce{C2H5OH + O2 -> 2C + 3H2O}\)

Right side: 4 + 3 = 7 O atoms; ethanol supplies 1, so \(\ce{O2}\) supplies 6: that's 3 \(\ce{O2}\). Option C forgets the O in ethanol.

A car switched from petrol to E100 (pure bioethanol). Which statement is correct?

  1. It will emit no CO₂
  2. It will travel further on a full tank
  3. Its exhaust gases will contain no water
  4. Its CO₂ emissions per MJ of energy will be about the same

Ethanol: 64.7 g CO₂/MJ; octane: 64.5 g/MJ. Ethanol has less energy per litre, so the range drops. The claimed benefit is that the carbon was recently absorbed, not that less is emitted.

Biodiesel is produced by

  1. reacting a triglyceride with methanol in the presence of a base catalyst
  2. fermenting glucose with yeast
  3. anaerobic digestion of manure
  4. fractional distillation of crude oil

Transesterification: the fatty acids swap from glycerol to methanol, giving methyl esters and glycerol.

How many moles of O₂ are needed for the complete combustion of 1.0 mol of butane, C₄H₁₀?

  1. 4.0
  2. 5.0
  3. 6.5
  4. 13

\(\ce{2C4H10 + 13O2 -> 8CO2 + 10H2O}\): 6.5 mol O₂ per mol of butane. (13 is for 2 mol.)

A gas heater in a poorly ventilated room is dangerous mainly because

  1. complete combustion produces carbon monoxide
  2. incomplete combustion produces carbon monoxide, which is toxic and odourless
  3. methane is toxic
  4. water vapour displaces oxygen

With limited O₂, combustion is incomplete and CO forms. CO binds to haemoglobin far more strongly than O₂.

Write a balanced equation, with states, for the incomplete combustion of methane that produces carbon monoxide and liquid water.

\(\ce{2CH4(g) + 3O2(g) -> 2CO(g) + 4H2O(l)}\).

Explain why bioethanol is described as renewable and give two reasons why it might not be fully carbon neutral.

It's made from crops (biomass) that can be regrown in a short time. [1]

Not carbon neutral because fossil fuels are often used for farm machinery, fertiliser production, heating the fermentation and distillation, and transporting the product. [2] (Any two.)

Using the data table above, calculate the mass of CO₂ released when enough methane is burnt to release 1.00 GJ of energy.

\(n(\ce{CH4}) = \frac{1.00 \times 10^6\text{ kJ}}{890\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}} = 1124\text{ mol}\). [1]

\(n(\ce{CO2}) = 1124\text{ mol}\) (1 : 1). [1]

\(m = 1124 \times 44.0 = 4.94 \times 10^4\text{ g} = 49.4\text{ kg}\). [1]

A council is choosing between biogas (from its sewage plant) and natural gas for its buses. Both are mostly methane. Discuss the similarities and differences in terms of energy content, emissions and sustainability.

Similar energy content per gram/mole of methane, but biogas also contains CO₂ (and traces of H₂S), so its energy per litre or kilogram of gas is lower unless it's upgraded (purified). [1]

Combustion of the methane gives the same CO₂ per MJ for both. [1]

Biogas is renewable and its carbon was recently in the atmosphere, so there's little net increase in CO₂; natural gas is a finite fossil fuel adding “new” CO₂. [1]

Using biogas also stops methane (a potent greenhouse gas) from the sewage escaping into the air. [1]

A student says: “Hydrogen is the perfect fuel because it produces only water.” Give two reasons this claim is incomplete.

Most hydrogen is currently made from natural gas (steam reforming), which releases CO₂. Only “green” hydrogen from electrolysis powered by renewables avoids this.

Hydrogen has very low energy per litre as a gas, so it must be compressed or liquefied (energy cost, safety issues); it's also flammable over a wide range of concentrations.

Summary

  • Fossil: ancient, finite, net CO₂ increase. Biofuel: recent biomass, renewable, closer to carbon neutral.
  • Biogas: anaerobic digestion; bioethanol: fermentation; biodiesel: transesterification.
  • Incomplete combustion gives CO and soot and releases less energy.
  • Compare per gram, per mole, per litre and per MJ of CO₂. The rankings can change.
