Section B questions chain several topics together. Show every step with units and correct significant figures, write balanced equations with states, and answer explanation questions with a clear cause → effect chain. Try each question fully before revealing the solution.

Question 1: fuels, calorimetry and fuel cells (13 marks)

A farm produces biogas that is 60% methane and 40% CO₂ by volume.

a. Explain why biogas is classified as renewable. (1)

b. Calculate the energy released by complete combustion of 1.00 L of this biogas at SLC (\(\Delta H_c(\ce{CH4}) = -890\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\)). (2)

c. A student burns methane under a calorimeter with CF = 4.50 kJ °C⁻¹. Burning 0.320 g of methane raises the temperature by 3.50 °C. Calculate the experimental \(\Delta H_c\) and give one reason it differs from the data-book value. (4)

d. The farm considers using the methane in an acidic fuel cell. Write the anode and cathode half-equations. (2)

e. The fuel cell delivers 20.0 A at 0.600 V for 2.00 h. Calculate the mass of methane consumed and the cell's efficiency compared with combustion. (4)

a. It's made by anaerobic digestion of recent organic matter (manure, crop waste), which is replaced on a short timescale. [1]

b. \(n(\ce{CH4}) = \frac{0.600}{24.8} = 0.02419\text{ mol}\) [1]; energy \(= 0.02419 \times 890 = 21.5\text{ kJ}\). [1]

c. \(q = 4.50 \times 3.50 = 15.75\text{ kJ}\) [1]; \(n = \frac{0.320}{16.0} = 0.0200\text{ mol}\) [1]; \(\Delta H_c = -\frac{15.75}{0.0200} = -788\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\) [1]. Lower in magnitude than 890 because of heat lost to the surroundings (or incomplete combustion, or water formed as vapour rather than liquid). [1]

d. Anode: \(\ce{CH4(g) + 2H2O(l)}\) \(\ce{-> CO2(g) + 8H+(aq) + 8e-}\) [1]. Cathode: \(\ce{O2(g) + 4H+(aq) + 4e-}\) \(\ce{-> 2H2O(l)}\) [1].

e. \(Q = 20.0 \times 7200 = 1.44 \times 10^5\text{ C}\); \(n(\ce{e-}) = 1.492\text{ mol}\) [1]; \(n(\ce{CH4}) = \frac{1.492}{8} = 0.1865\text{ mol}\), \(m = 2.98\text{ g}\) [1]. Electrical energy \(= 0.600 \times 1.44 \times 10^5 = 86.4\text{ kJ}\); chemical energy available \(= 0.1865 \times 890 = 166\text{ kJ}\) [1]; efficiency \(\approx 52\%\). [1]

Question 2: equilibrium in industry (12 marks)

Methanol is made by \(\ce{CO(g) + 2H2(g) <=> CH3OH(g)}\), \(\Delta H = -91\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\).

a. 0.50 mol CO and 1.00 mol H₂ are placed in a 2.0 L vessel. At equilibrium there is 0.20 mol CH₃OH. Calculate \(K\), with units. (4)

b. Predict and explain the effect on the equilibrium yield of methanol of: (i) halving the volume; (ii) raising the temperature. (4)

c. The industrial process runs at about 250 °C with a copper-based catalyst. Explain why this temperature is chosen. (2)

d. At time \(t\), the temperature of an equilibrium mixture is increased. Describe how the concentration–time graph for CH₃OH changes. (2)

a. Equilibrium amounts: CO \(0.50 - 0.20 = 0.30\), H₂ \(1.00 - 0.40 = 0.60\), CH₃OH 0.20 mol [1]. Concentrations: 0.15, 0.30, 0.10 M [1]. \(K = \frac{0.10}{0.15 \times 0.30^2}\) [1] \(= 7.4\ \text{M}^{-2}\). [1]

b. (i) Halving the volume doubles all concentrations; \(Q\) becomes \(\frac{K}{4}\) (\(Q < K\)), so the system shifts forward to fewer gas particles (3 → 1). Yield increases. [2] (ii) The forward reaction is exothermic; raising the temperature favours the endothermic (reverse) direction and decreases \(K\). Yield decreases. [2]

c. A lower temperature would give a higher equilibrium yield but a rate that's too slow; 250 °C with a catalyst gives an acceptable rate while keeping the yield reasonable: a compromise. [2]

d. No vertical jump at \(t\); [CH₃OH] gradually decreases to a new, lower constant value (and [CO], [H₂] gradually increase). [2]

Question 3: organic synthesis and spectroscopy (13 marks)

Ethyl ethanoate is made from ethene by the pathway: ethene → A → B, then A + B → ethyl ethanoate.

a. Name A and B and give the reagents and conditions for each of the first two steps. (4)

b. Write the equation for the final step, naming the catalyst. (2)

c. 30.0 g of B reacts with 25.0 g of A, and 26.4 g of ester is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. (3)

d. Describe how the IR spectra of B and the ester would differ. (2)

e. The ester's ¹H NMR spectrum shows three signals. Give the chemical shift, integration and splitting for each. (2)

a. A = ethanol: ethene + steam, H₃PO₄ catalyst, about 300 °C [2]. B = ethanoic acid: ethanol + \(\ce{H+/Cr2O7^2-}\) (or \(\ce{H+/MnO4-}\)), heat. [2]

b. \(\ce{CH3COOH + CH3CH2OH}\) \(\ce{<=> CH3COOCH2CH3 + H2O}\) [1]; concentrated sulfuric acid. [1]

c. \(n(\text{acid}) = \frac{30.0}{60.0} = 0.500\); \(n(\text{ethanol}) = \frac{25.0}{46.0} = 0.543\), so the acid is limiting [1]. Theoretical ester = 0.500 mol; actual \(= \frac{26.4}{88.0} = 0.300\) mol [1]. Yield = 60.0%. [1]

d. B (acid) shows a very broad O–H band at 2500–3300 cm⁻¹; the ester has no O–H band. Both show C=O near 1700–1750. [2]

e. δ ≈ 2.0, singlet, 3H (CH₃CO); δ ≈ 4.1, quartet, 2H (OCH₂); δ ≈ 1.3, triplet, 3H (CH₃). [2]

Question 4: food and analysis (12 marks)

a. Explain why fats and oils release more energy per gram than carbohydrates when oxidised in the body. (2)

b. A 25.00 mL sample of orange juice needs 12.40 mL of 0.00500 M iodine solution for the end point (\(\ce{C6H8O6 + I2}\) \(\ce{-> C6H6O6 + 2H+ + 2I-}\), \(M(\ce{C6H8O6}) = 176.1\)). Calculate the vitamin C content in mg per 100 mL. (3)

c. State the indicator and describe the colour change at the end point. (1)

d. Describe a simple chemical test that would show whether an oil sample is unsaturated, including the expected observation. (2)

e. A food label lists per serve: 3.0 g fat, 22 g carbohydrate (of which 4 g is dietary fibre), 5.0 g protein. Estimate the energy per serve, explaining how you treat the fibre. (4)

a. Fats contain far fewer oxygen atoms relative to carbon and hydrogen, so their carbon is less oxidised to begin with. [1] More energy is released as their C–H and C–C bonds are converted to CO₂ and H₂O (about 37 kJ g⁻¹ vs 16 kJ g⁻¹). [1]

b. \(n(\ce{I2}) = 6.20 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol} = n(\text{vit C})\) [1]; mass \(= 10.9\text{ mg}\) in 25.00 mL [1]; 43.7 mg per 100 mL. [1]

c. Starch; colourless to the first permanent blue-black. [1]

d. Add bromine water to the oil and shake. [1] If the oil is unsaturated, the orange-brown colour disappears as Br₂ adds across the C=C bonds. [1]

e. Fibre (cellulose) can't be hydrolysed by humans, so count only \(22 - 4 = 18\) g of carbohydrate [1]. Energy \(= 3.0 \times 37 + 18 \times 16 + 5.0 \times 17\) [1] \(= 111 + 288 + 85 = 484\text{ kJ}\) [1]. (Some labels assign fibre about 8 kJ g⁻¹; stating the assumption earns the mark.) [1]
