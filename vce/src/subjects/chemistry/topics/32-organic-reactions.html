Addition: two molecules join into one; C=C becomes C–C (100% atom economy). Substitution: one atom or group swaps for another; two products. Condensation: two molecules join with loss of a small molecule (usually water). Hydrolysis is the reverse: water splits a bond.

Give the organic product when each alcohol is heated with excess acidified potassium dichromate: (a) butan-1-ol; (b) butan-2-ol; (c) 2-methylpropan-2-ol. Describe the colour change in each case.

Acidified dichromate oxidises alcohols by removing H from the carbon bearing the OH. Primary alcohols have two such H (aldehyde then acid), secondary have one (ketone), and tertiary have none (no reaction).

(a) Primary → aldehyde → carboxylic acid with excess oxidant: butanoic acid, \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2COOH}\). Orange → green.

(b) Secondary → ketone: butanone, \(\ce{CH3COCH2CH3}\). Orange → green.

(c) Tertiary: no reaction, and the solution stays orange. (To stop a primary alcohol at the aldehyde, distil the aldehyde off as it forms, since it has a lower boiling point.)

Write an equation, using semi-structural formulas, for the formation of methyl propanoate. Name the catalyst and the type of reaction.

Esterification is a condensation reaction between a carboxylic acid and an alcohol, catalysed by concentrated sulfuric acid, releasing water. It's reversible.

Acid part: propanoic acid \(\ce{CH3CH2COOH}\). Alcohol part: methanol \(\ce{CH3OH}\).

\(\ce{CH3CH2COOH + CH3OH}\) \(\ce{<=> CH3CH2COOCH3 + H2O}\); concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst; condensation (esterification). It's reversible, so excess of one reactant or removal of water improves the yield.

Describe what happens when a triglyceride reacts with methanol and a KOH catalyst.

Transesterification swaps the alcohol part of an ester. A triglyceride has three ester links, so one triglyceride reacts with three methanol molecules.

Each of the three ester links in the triglyceride swaps its glycerol part for a methanol part.

Triglyceride + 3 methanol → 3 fatty acid methyl esters (biodiesel) + glycerol. It's called transesterification: one ester becomes another.

  • Tertiary alcohols don't oxidise with dichromate or permanganate.
  • Hydration needs steam and a catalyst; alkenes don't react with liquid water at room temperature.
  • Substitution of alkanes needs UV light and gives a mixture of products (multiple substitution).
  • Esterification and ester hydrolysis are the same equilibrium run in opposite directions.
  • The product of adding HCl to propene can be 1-chloropropane or 2-chloropropane (both form).

The reaction of ethene with bromine is an example of

  1. substitution
  2. condensation
  3. addition
  4. oxidation of an alcohol

Br₂ adds across the double bond, giving 1,2-dibromoethane.

Propan-2-ol is heated with acidified permanganate. The organic product is

  1. propanone
  2. propanal
  3. propanoic acid
  4. propene

Secondary alcohol → ketone.

A student adds acidified dichromate to an unknown alcohol and heats it. The solution stays orange. The alcohol could be

  1. ethanol
  2. butan-2-ol
  3. methanol
  4. 2-methylbutan-2-ol

Only a tertiary alcohol fails to reduce dichromate. 2-methylbutan-2-ol has no H on the C–OH carbon.

The reagent and conditions to convert chloroethane into ethanol are

  1. H₂O, H₃PO₄, 300 °C
  2. NaOH(aq), heat
  3. H⁺/Cr₂O₇²⁻
  4. NH₃, heat

Substitution of Cl by OH using hydroxide ions. D gives ethanamine.

Which pair of reactants produces the ester \(\ce{HCOOCH2CH3}\)?

  1. ethanoic acid and methanol
  2. methanol and propanoic acid
  3. methanoic acid and ethanol
  4. ethanal and methanoic acid

Ethyl methanoate: HCOO– comes from methanoic acid, –CH₂CH₃ from ethanol. Note that A gives methyl ethanoate, an isomer.

Which reaction has 100% atom economy?

  1. \(\ce{C2H4 + H2O -> C2H5OH}\)
  2. \(\ce{C2H5Cl + OH- -> C2H5OH + Cl-}\)
  3. \(\ce{C6H12O6 -> 2C2H5OH + 2CO2}\)
  4. \(\ce{CH3COOH + C2H5OH}\) \(\ce{-> CH3COOC2H5 + H2O}\)

Addition: all reactant atoms end up in the one product.

Write equations for (a) the hydrogenation of propene, (b) the reaction of ethanoic acid with propan-1-ol, (c) the reaction of 1-chloropropane with ammonia.

(a) \(\ce{CH2=CHCH3 + H2 -> CH3CH2CH3}\) (Ni catalyst, heat).

(b) \(\ce{CH3COOH + CH3CH2CH2OH}\) \(\ce{<=> CH3COOCH2CH2CH3 + H2O}\) (propyl ethanoate).

(c) \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2Cl + NH3}\) \(\ce{-> CH3CH2CH2NH2 + HCl}\) (propan-1-amine).

Write the balanced overall equation for the oxidation of propan-1-ol to propanoic acid by acidified permanganate.

Oxidation: \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2OH + H2O}\) \(\ce{-> CH3CH2COOH + 4H+ + 4e-}\) (× 5). Reduction: \(\ce{MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e-}\) \(\ce{-> Mn^2+ + 4H2O}\) (× 4). [2]

Overall: \(\ce{5CH3CH2CH2OH + 4MnO4- + 12H+}\) \(\ce{-> 5CH3CH2COOH + 4Mn^2+ + 11H2O}\). [1]

Aspirin can be hydrolysed in the body at its ester group. Describe what hydrolysis means, and name the two types of product formed from an ester.

Hydrolysis: a reaction in which water breaks a bond, splitting the molecule into two, the reverse of a condensation. [1]

An ester hydrolyses to a carboxylic acid and an alcohol (in basic conditions, the carboxylate salt forms). [2]

A student claims that ethanol can be made from ethene by bubbling ethene through water at room temperature. Evaluate this.

Incorrect: hydration needs steam at high temperature (about 300 °C) and a catalyst (phosphoric acid), usually at high pressure. At room temperature with liquid water, no significant reaction occurs.

Summary

  • Alkenes: addition (H₂, X₂, HX, H₂O). Alkanes and haloalkanes: substitution.
  • 1° alcohol → aldehyde → acid; 2° → ketone; 3° → no reaction (dichromate stays orange).
  • Acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (conc. H₂SO₄); acid + amine → amide + water.
