Reading and building pathways

Pathway questions give a flow chart with some compounds, reagents or conditions missing. Work forwards from the starting material and backwards from the product (what could this have been made from?). A standard chain:

A typical four-step pathway
\[ \ce{CH2=CH2 ->[H2O, H3PO4][300 °C] CH3CH2OH ->[H+/Cr2O7^2-] CH3COOH ->[CH3CH2OH][conc. H2SO4] CH3COOCH2CH3} \]

ethene → ethanol → ethanoic acid → ethyl ethanoate

Starting from propene, design a pathway to make propyl propanoate. Give the reagents and conditions for each step.

Propyl propanoate = propanoic acid + propan-1-ol. Both come from propene, but we need the primary alcohol.

Step 1: propene + HCl → 1-chloropropane (some forms alongside 2-chloropropane; separate them). Step 2: 1-chloropropane + NaOH(aq), heat → propan-1-ol.

Step 3: some of the propan-1-ol + excess \(\ce{H+/MnO4-}\), heat → propanoic acid.

Step 4: propanoic acid + propan-1-ol, conc. H₂SO₄, heat (reflux) → propyl propanoate + water. (Hydrating propene directly gives mainly propan-2-ol, which would make the ketone and the wrong ester.)

Yield

Percentage yield
\[ \%\ \text{yield} = \frac{\text{actual mass (or mol)}}{\text{theoretical mass (or mol)}} \times 100 \qquad \text{overall yield} = y_1 \times y_2 \times \cdots \]

The theoretical yield comes from the limiting reactant. For multi-step syntheses, multiply the step yields (as decimals).

12.0 g of ethanoic acid (\(M = 60.0\)) is refluxed with excess ethanol and 11.0 g of ethyl ethanoate (\(M = 88.0\)) is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield.

\(n(\text{acid}) = 0.200\text{ mol}\) (limiting), so theoretical \(n(\text{ester}) = 0.200\text{ mol}\).

Actual \(n = \frac{11.0}{88.0} = 0.125\text{ mol}\).

Yield = \(\frac{0.125}{0.200} \times 100 = 62.5\%\). Esterification is an equilibrium, so it never reaches 100%; losses in purification (separating funnel, distillation) reduce it further.

A three-step synthesis has step yields of 80%, 75% and 90%. What is the overall yield?

\(0.80 \times 0.75 \times 0.90 = 0.54\).

54%, not the average (81.7%) and not the lowest (75%). Long pathways lose a lot, which is one reason chemists prefer short routes.

Atom economy

Atom economy
\[ \text{atom economy} = \frac{M(\text{desired product})}{\text{total } M \text{ of all reactants}} \times 100 \]

Uses the balanced equation (coefficients × molar masses). It measures how much of the reactants' mass could end up in the product, even at 100% yield.

Compare the atom economy of making ethanol by (a) hydration of ethene and (b) fermentation of glucose.

(a) \(\ce{C2H4 + H2O -> C2H5OH}\): all atoms end up in ethanol: 100%.

(b) \(\ce{C6H12O6 -> 2C2H5OH + 2CO2}\): \(\frac{2 \times 46.0}{180.0} \times 100 = 51.1\%\).

(a) 100%; (b) 51.1%. But (a) uses a non-renewable feedstock (ethene from crude oil) and high temperature, while (b) uses renewable sugar at low temperature. Green chemistry weighs several principles, not just atom economy.

Green chemistry in synthesis

  • Prevent waste and design for high atom economy (addition reactions are ideal).
  • Use renewable feedstocks (biomass instead of petrochemicals).
  • Use catalysts (including enzymes) rather than stoichiometric reagents.
  • Design for energy efficiency (lower temperatures and pressures).
  • Use safer solvents and reagents (e.g. water instead of toxic organic solvents; avoid heavy metals such as chromium).
  • Atom economy ≠ percentage yield. A reaction can have 100% atom economy and 30% yield, or 100% yield and 40% atom economy.
  • Include coefficients in atom economy (2 × 46.0, not 46.0).
  • Always base theoretical yield on the limiting reactant.
  • Yields above 100% mean the product is impure or still wet.

Practice

A reaction produced 4.5 g of product when the theoretical yield was 6.0 g. The percentage yield is

  1. 133%
  2. 75%
  3. 25%
  4. 45%

\(\frac{4.5}{6.0} \times 100 = 75\%\).

Which reagents convert ethanol into ethanoic acid?

  1. H₂, Ni
  2. NaOH(aq)
  3. conc. H₂SO₄ only
  4. \(\ce{H+/Cr2O7^2-}\), heat

Oxidation of a primary alcohol to a carboxylic acid.

The atom economy for \(\ce{CH3CH2Cl + NaOH -> CH3CH2OH + NaCl}\) is closest to (\(M\): chloroethane 64.5, NaOH 40.0, ethanol 46.0)

  1. 44.0%
  2. 100%
  3. 71.3%
  4. 56.0%

\(\frac{46.0}{64.5 + 40.0} \times 100 = 44.0\%\). Substitution always wastes the leaving group.

A four-step synthesis has 90% yield at every step. The overall yield is closest to

  1. 90%
  2. 60%
  3. 66%
  4. 36%

\(0.9^4 = 0.656\).

To make butanone from but-1-ene, the most sensible pathway is

  1. hydrogenation, then oxidation
  2. hydration (giving mainly butan-2-ol), then oxidation
  3. substitution with Cl₂/UV, then esterification
  4. oxidation, then hydration

Hydration of but-1-ene gives mostly butan-2-ol (secondary), which oxidises to butanone.

Give the reagents and conditions for each step: ethane → chloroethane → ethanol → ethanoic acid.

Ethane → chloroethane: Cl₂, UV light (substitution). Chloroethane → ethanol: NaOH(aq), heat (substitution). Ethanol → ethanoic acid: \(\ce{H+/Cr2O7^2-}\) or \(\ce{H+/MnO4-}\), heat (oxidation).

25.0 g of propan-1-ol (\(M = 60.0\)) is converted to propanoic acid (74.0) with 70% yield, and then all of the acid is reacted with excess methanol to give methyl propanoate (88.0) with 60% yield. Calculate the mass of ester obtained.

\(n(\text{propanol}) = 0.4167\text{ mol}\). [1]

Overall yield \(= 0.70 \times 0.60 = 0.42\). [1]

\(n(\text{ester}) = 0.4167 \times 0.42 = 0.1750\text{ mol}\). [1]

\(m = 0.1750 \times 88.0 = 15.4\text{ g}\). [1]

Ethyl ethanoate (a solvent) can be made by esterification, or by a newer process: \(\ce{2CH3CHO -> CH3COOCH2CH3}\) using a catalyst. Calculate the atom economy of both routes and discuss which is “greener”.

Esterification: \(\ce{CH3COOH + C2H5OH -> CH3COOC2H5 + H2O}\): \(\frac{88.0}{60.0 + 46.0} \times 100 = 83.0\%\). [1]

Aldehyde route: \(\frac{88.0}{2 \times 44.0} \times 100 = 100\%\). [1]

The second route produces no by-product and uses a catalyst (good green principles). [1] But also consider where the feedstocks come from (renewable ethanol vs petrochemical ethanal), energy use and yield before deciding. [1]

A student obtains a 108% yield of aspirin. Suggest two reasons.

The product wasn't fully dried (it still contains water or solvent). It contains impurities such as unreacted starting material or by-products. A yield over 100% is never real.

Summary

  • Plan pathways by working forwards from the reactant and backwards from the target.
  • Overall yield = product of the step yields.
  • Atom economy = \(\frac{M(\text{product})}{\sum M(\text{reactants})} \times 100\); addition reactions score 100%.
  • Green chemistry weighs atom economy, feedstock, energy, catalysts and safety together.
