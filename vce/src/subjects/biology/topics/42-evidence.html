Molecular evidence: comparative genomics

Closely related species share a recent common ancestor, so there has been less time for mutations to accumulate: their DNA (and protein) sequences are more similar. Comparing sequences gives a measure of relatedness.

  • Conserved genes (essential genes that change slowly, e.g. those for cytochrome c, ribosomal RNA, histones) allow comparison of even very distantly related organisms.
  • DNA comparisons are more sensitive than amino acid comparisons, because silent mutations change DNA without changing the protein.
  • Molecular clock: if mutations accumulate at a roughly constant rate, the number of differences estimates the time since two lineages diverged (calibrated with fossil dates).
  • Mitochondrial DNA (inherited maternally, mutates relatively fast) is used for recent divergences such as human migrations.

Phylogenetic trees

  • Nodes = common ancestors; branch tips = species (living or extinct).
  • Relatedness depends on how recent the shared ancestor is, not on how close the names are on the page. Branches can rotate around a node without changing the tree's meaning.
  • A clade is an ancestor and all its descendants.

Differences in a DNA sequence between four species: P–Q 3, P–R 12, P–S 15, Q–R 12, Q–S 15, R–S 14. Draw the most likely tree.

Mutations build up over time, so the fewer sequence differences two species have, the more recently they shared a common ancestor. Join the closest pair first, then add the others in order of increasing difference.

P and Q differ least (3): they share the most recent common ancestor.

R is next closest to the P/Q group (12 differences each); S is furthest from all.

(((P, Q), R), S): S branched off first, then R, then P and Q split most recently.

  • Analogous structures don't show close relatedness; they show convergent evolution.
  • Humans didn't evolve from chimpanzees. They share a common ancestor.
  • Reading a tree left to right isn't a ranking; neighbouring tips aren't necessarily the closest relatives.
  • Amino acid similarity can hide DNA differences (degenerate code), so DNA is the finer tool.

Explain why vestigial structures are evidence for evolution.

They're reduced structures with little or no current function that were functional in ancestors (e.g. whale pelvic bones). Their presence is best explained by descent from ancestors that used them, with the structure reducing over time.

Explain why comparing the DNA of a highly conserved gene is useful for determining relationships between very distantly related organisms (e.g. humans and yeast).

Conserved genes have essential functions, so most mutations in them are harmful and eliminated by selection; they change very slowly. [1] Distantly related species still share enough sequence to be compared meaningfully. [1] The number of differences then reflects time since divergence without being “saturated” by changes, as fast-changing sequences would be. [1]

Sharks and dolphins both have streamlined bodies and dorsal fins. (a) What type of structures are these? (b) Explain how they arose. (c) What evidence would show that dolphins are more closely related to cows than to sharks?

(a) Analogous. [1] (b) Convergent evolution: similar selection pressures in water favoured similar body shapes in unrelated lineages. [1] (c) Molecular evidence: dolphin DNA/protein sequences are more similar to cows' (and other mammals') than to sharks'. [1] Structural evidence: dolphins have mammalian homologous features, e.g. pentadactyl bones in flippers, lungs, mammary glands, vestigial pelvic bones. [1]

A student looks at a phylogenetic tree and says species D is more closely related to C than to A because D is drawn next to C. When is this reasoning correct, and when isn't it?

Relatedness depends only on the most recent common ancestor (node), not on position on the page. It's correct only if C and D share a node more recent than the one they share with A. Rotating branches can put any tip next to any other without changing relationships.

Summary

  • Fewer sequence differences = more recent common ancestor; conserved genes for distant relatives.
  • Homologous → divergent evolution; analogous → convergent; vestigial → ancestral function.
  • Trees: nodes are common ancestors; relatedness depends on the most recent shared node.
