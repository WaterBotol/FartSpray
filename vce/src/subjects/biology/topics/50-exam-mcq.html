The VCE Biology exam (2 h 30 min plus 15 min reading) begins with a multiple-choice section. Allow about 1.5 minutes per question. Biology MCQs often hinge on one word (“template”, “non-competitive”, “passive”, “population”): underline it.

1. The template strand of a gene reads 3′ TAC CGT 5′. The mRNA codons are

  1. 5′ UAC CGU 3′
  2. 5′ AUG GCA 3′
  3. 3′ AUG GCA 5′
  4. 5′ ATG GCA 3′

mRNA is complementary to the template and built 5′→3′: AUG GCA. D contains T (DNA).

2. In the trp operon, when tryptophan concentration is high,

  1. trpR is not transcribed
  2. RNA polymerase binds more strongly to the promoter
  3. tryptophan binds directly to the operator
  4. the repressor–tryptophan complex binds the operator

Tryptophan is the corepressor that activates the repressor protein.

3. Alternative splicing allows

  1. one gene to code for more than one polypeptide
  2. introns to be translated
  3. DNA to be copied faster
  4. prokaryotes to remove introns

Different combinations of exons produce different mature mRNAs.

4. A linear DNA molecule of 8000 bp has three recognition sites for a restriction enzyme. How many fragments are produced by a complete digest?

  1. 3
  2. 2
  3. 4
  4. 6

Linear: cuts + 1 = 4 fragments (a circular plasmid would give 3).

5. Which step of PCR involves primers binding to the template strands?

  1. denaturation at 95 °C
  2. annealing at about 55 °C
  3. extension at 72 °C
  4. ligation

Annealing: cooled so primers can hydrogen bond to complementary sequences.

6. Bacteria are transformed with a plasmid containing an ampicillin-resistance gene and a GFP gene. The human gene is inserted into the GFP gene. On ampicillin plates, colonies containing the recombinant plasmid will

  1. not grow
  2. glow green
  3. be killed by ampicillin
  4. grow but not glow

They have the plasmid (so they're ampicillin-resistant), but GFP is disrupted by the insert.

7. In the light-dependent stage of photosynthesis,

  1. water is split and oxygen is released
  2. CO₂ is fixed by Rubisco
  3. glucose is produced
  4. NADPH is unloaded to NADP⁺

Photolysis in the thylakoids releases O₂; NADP⁺ is loaded to NADPH.

8. C4 plants outperform C3 plants in hot conditions mainly because

  1. they don't use Rubisco
  2. they keep their stomata open all day
  3. they concentrate CO₂ around Rubisco in bundle sheath cells, reducing photorespiration
  4. they photosynthesise at night

Spatial separation: PEP carboxylase fixes CO₂ first, then it's released to Rubisco. D describes CAM's night-time fixation.

9. Adding more substrate increases the rate of an inhibited enzyme reaction to the uninhibited maximum. The inhibitor is

  1. non-competitive
  2. competitive
  3. irreversible
  4. a coenzyme

Only competitive inhibition can be overcome by excess substrate.

10. The final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration is

  1. NAD⁺
  2. pyruvate
  3. CO₂
  4. O₂

O₂ + electrons + H⁺ → water at the end of the ETC.

11. In yeast cells deprived of oxygen, ethanol is produced because

  1. converting pyruvate to ethanol regenerates NAD⁺, allowing glycolysis to continue
  2. ethanol contains more energy than glucose
  3. the Krebs cycle produces ethanol without oxygen
  4. ethanol is needed for the ETC

Fermentation's purpose is NAD⁺ regeneration; ATP comes only from glycolysis.

12. Histamine released from mast cells causes

  1. antibody production
  2. phagocytosis
  3. vasodilation and increased capillary permeability
  4. apoptosis of infected cells

These produce the redness, heat and swelling of inflammation.

13. Cytotoxic T cells destroy virus-infected cells by recognising

  1. free virus particles in the blood
  2. viral antigen presented on MHC class I
  3. antibodies on the infected cell
  4. viral antigen presented on MHC class II

All nucleated cells present internal antigens on MHC I. MHC II is used by antigen-presenting cells to activate T helper cells.

14. Receiving antivenom after a snakebite is an example of

  1. artificial passive immunity
  2. artificial active immunity
  3. natural passive immunity
  4. natural active immunity

Ready-made antibodies, given by medical intervention.

15. A disease has \(R_0 = 5\). The minimum proportion of the population that must be immune for herd immunity is

  1. 20%
  2. 50%
  3. 95%
  4. 80%

\(1 - \frac15 = 0.8\).

16. Which is the best description of antigenic shift?

  1. gradual accumulation of point mutations in viral antigen genes
  2. bacteria acquiring antibiotic-resistance plasmids
  3. reassortment of gene segments between two different virus strains infecting the same cell
  4. the loss of MHC markers from infected cells

A describes drift.

17. A small group of birds is blown to a remote island and founds a new population with allele frequencies different from the mainland. This is

  1. a bottleneck effect
  2. a founder effect
  3. natural selection
  4. gene flow

A new population started by a few individuals: founder effect (genetic drift).

18. In a population of 200 individuals, 32 are homozygous recessive (aa) and 96 are heterozygous (Aa). The frequency of allele a is

  1. 0.40
  2. 0.16
  3. 0.48
  4. 0.64

a alleles \(= 2(32) + 96 = 160\) out of 400 = 0.40.

19. The wings of bats and the wings of insects are best described as

  1. homologous, showing divergent evolution
  2. vestigial
  3. evidence of a recent common ancestor
  4. analogous, showing convergent evolution

Same function, completely different structure and origin.

20. Which skeletal feature provides the clearest evidence of habitual bipedalism?

  1. opposable thumbs
  2. a large cranial capacity
  3. a centrally positioned foramen magnum
  4. forward-facing eye sockets

A and D are primate traits; brain size increased much later than bipedalism.
