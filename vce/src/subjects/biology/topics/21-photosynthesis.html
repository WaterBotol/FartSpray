Overall equation and the chloroplast

Photosynthesis (as written by VCAA)
\[ 6\,\ce{CO2} + 12\,\ce{H2O} \xrightarrow[\text{chlorophyll}]{\text{light}} \ce{C6H12O6} + 6\,\ce{O2} + 6\,\ce{H2O} \]

Writing 12 H₂O shows that all the O₂ released comes from water (split in the light-dependent stage); the 6 H₂O on the right is newly formed.

Chloroplasts have an inner system of flattened membrane sacs called thylakoids (stacked in grana), which contain chlorophyll, surrounded by fluid called the stroma.

The two stages

Light-dependent stageLight-independent stage (Calvin cycle)
LocationThylakoid membranes (grana)Stroma
InputsLight energy, H₂O, NADP⁺, ADP + PiCO₂, NADPH, ATP
OutputsO₂ (released), NADPH, ATPGlucose (via 3-carbon sugars), NADP⁺, ADP + Pi
What happensChlorophyll absorbs light; water is split (photolysis) into O₂, H⁺ and electrons; energy is captured as ATP and NADPHCO₂ is fixed onto RuBP by the enzyme Rubisco; ATP and NADPH provide energy and hydrogen to make sugar; RuBP is regenerated

The stages are coupled by the coenzymes: the light-dependent stage loads NADP⁺ → NADPH and ADP → ATP; the Calvin cycle unloads them. Without light, ATP and NADPH run out within seconds and the Calvin cycle stops too, even though it doesn't use light directly.

Rubisco, photorespiration, and C3/C4/CAM plants

Rubisco can bind O₂ instead of CO₂. When stomata close on hot, dry days, O₂ builds up inside the leaf and CO₂ falls, and Rubisco increasingly fixes O₂: photorespiration. It wastes energy, releases CO₂ and reduces the efficiency of photosynthesis.

Limiting factors

The rate of photosynthesis is limited by whichever factor is in shortest supply: light intensity, CO₂ concentration, temperature (enzymes such as Rubisco) and water availability (lack of water closes stomata, reducing CO₂ intake).

A graph of photosynthesis rate against light intensity rises and then plateaus at 0.04% CO₂. At 0.1% CO₂ it plateaus at a higher rate. Explain both parts of the curve.

At low light intensity, rate increases with light: light is the limiting factor (not enough ATP and NADPH made).

At the plateau, more light has no effect: another factor is limiting. Raising CO₂ raises the plateau, so CO₂ was the limiting factor there.

Light limits on the rising part; CO₂ (or temperature) limits on the plateau. Glasshouse growers add CO₂ to increase crop yield.

Improving photosynthesis with CRISPR

Researchers are using CRISPR-Cas9 to edit genes that affect photosynthetic efficiency, for example altering genes that regulate Rubisco or photorespiration, stomatal density (water-use efficiency) or how quickly leaves adjust to changing light. The aim is higher crop yields and more drought-tolerant crops for a growing population and a changing climate. Most of this work is still at the research or field-trial stage.

  • The O₂ comes from water, not CO₂.
  • “Light-independent” doesn't mean it happens in the dark. It needs ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent stage.
  • NADPH, not NADH, in photosynthesis.
  • C4 plants still use Rubisco and the Calvin cycle; they just concentrate CO₂ around it.
  • Plants respire too, day and night, in their mitochondria.

State the location, inputs and outputs of the Calvin cycle.

Location: stroma of the chloroplast. Inputs: CO₂, ATP, NADPH. Outputs: glucose (sugars), ADP + Pi, NADP⁺.

Explain why maize (C4) grows better than wheat (C3) in hot, sunny conditions.

In the heat, stomata partly close and C3 plants suffer photorespiration, as Rubisco fixes O₂ instead of CO₂. [1] Maize fixes CO₂ first with PEP carboxylase, which doesn't bind O₂, then releases it at high concentration around Rubisco in the bundle sheath cells. [1] So Rubisco mostly fixes CO₂; photorespiration is minimal and photosynthesis stays efficient. [1]

A student measures O₂ bubbles from pondweed at different distances from a lamp. (a) Name the independent and dependent variables. (b) Why is a heat shield (water bath) placed between lamp and pondweed? (c) At the closest distances, bubble rate stops increasing. Suggest why.

(a) Independent: light intensity (distance from the lamp). Dependent: rate of O₂ production (bubbles per minute). [1]

(b) To keep the temperature constant (a controlled variable), so any change is due to light alone. [1]

(c) Another factor, such as CO₂ concentration or temperature, has become limiting, [1] or the light-dependent machinery is saturated. [1]

A student says: “Plants photosynthesise during the day and respire at night.” Correct this.

Plants respire all the time (day and night) in their mitochondria, to make ATP. During the day, photosynthesis usually exceeds respiration, so there's net O₂ release; at night only respiration occurs.

Summary

  • Light-dependent (thylakoids): H₂O, light, NADP⁺, ADP → O₂, NADPH, ATP.
  • Calvin cycle (stroma): CO₂, NADPH, ATP → glucose, NADP⁺, ADP; Rubisco fixes CO₂.
  • Photorespiration in hot/dry C3 plants; C4 (spatial) and CAM (temporal) avoid it.
  • Limiting factors: light, CO₂, temperature, water.
