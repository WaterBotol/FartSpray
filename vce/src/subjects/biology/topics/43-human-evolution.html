The Laetoli footprints (Tanzania, about 3.7 million years old) show fully upright walking long before large brains evolved.

Trends over time

  • Brain size increased: about 400–500 cm³ in australopithecines, about 600 cm³ in Homo habilis, about 900–1100 cm³ in H. erectus, about 1350 cm³ in modern humans.
  • Face flatter (less prognathism); smaller teeth and jaws; parabolic (curved) dental arcade; brow ridges reduced; a chin appears in H. sapiens.
  • Tools and culture became more complex (from stone tools in early Homo to fire, art and ornaments).

Out of Africa and into Australia

  • Homo sapiens evolved in Africa and dispersed across the world, with a major migration out of Africa about 70 000–60 000 years ago (earlier, smaller movements also occurred).
  • Evidence: fossils (oldest H. sapiens in Africa); mitochondrial DNA (maternally inherited) shows the greatest diversity in African populations, consistent with the longest time there; nuclear and ancient DNA.
  • Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples arrived in Australia at least 50 000 years ago; evidence from Madjedbebe (Northern Territory) suggests about 65 000 years. Reaching Australia required sea crossings. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultures are among the oldest continuous cultures on Earth.
  • Interbreeding: modern people outside Africa carry about 1–2% Neanderthal DNA; people in Melanesia and Aboriginal Australians also carry a few per cent Denisovan DNA. So H. sapiens replaced other hominins, but not without some interbreeding.

Changing classification

Hominin classification is revised as new evidence appears: Denisovans were identified in 2010 from DNA before any significant skeleton was found; H. floresiensis (2004) and H. naledi (2015) forced rethinks of which species coexisted. Ancient DNA sequencing, new dating methods and new fossils can all change where branches sit on the hominin tree.

  • Humans didn't evolve from chimpanzees; we share a common ancestor about 6–7 million years ago.
  • Bipedalism came before big brains (australopithecines walked upright with ape-sized brains).
  • Hominin evolution isn't a single ladder; several hominin species lived at the same time.
  • mtDNA traces the maternal line only (not recombined each generation).

Australopithecines were bipedal with small brains. Neanderthals are a sister group (with some interbreeding), not our direct ancestors.

  3. Neanderthals had larger brains

Shared DNA segments inherited from Neanderthals are direct evidence of interbreeding.

Describe three skeletal adaptations for bipedalism and explain how each helps.

Central foramen magnum: skull balanced on an upright spine. Bowl-shaped pelvis: supports internal organs and anchors walking muscles. Angled femur (carrying angle): keeps knees and feet under the body's centre of mass. (Also S-shaped spine for shock absorption; arched foot with an aligned big toe for push-off.)

Explain how the classification of hominins can change over time, using an example.

Classification is based on current evidence (fossils, dating, DNA). [1] New discoveries or techniques can add species or change relationships. [1] Example: Denisovans were recognised as a distinct group in 2010 from DNA sequenced from a finger bone, and later evidence showed they interbred with H. sapiens. [1]

Outline the evidence for the arrival of modern humans in Australia and the significance of these findings.

Archaeological evidence (stone tools, ochre, remains of hearths) at Madjedbebe in the Northern Territory, dated by luminescence methods to about 65 000 years ago. [1] Genetic evidence from Aboriginal Australians shows a long, deep history in Australia after dispersal from Africa. [1] Reaching Australia required sea crossings, showing early boat-building and navigation. [1] Significance: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have among the oldest continuous cultures in the world. [1]

A student says: “Evolution means humans are descended from monkeys.” Correct this.

Humans and living monkeys and apes share common ancestors; humans didn't descend from any living species. For example, humans and chimpanzees share a common ancestor that lived about 6–7 million years ago, and each lineage has evolved since then.

Summary

  • Primates → hominoids → hominins (bipedal).
  • Bipedal skeleton: central foramen magnum, S-spine, bowl pelvis, angled femur, arched foot.
  • Trends: bigger brain, flatter face, smaller teeth; bipedalism first.
  • H. sapiens: Africa ~300 000 years ago; out of Africa ~60–70 000; Australia ≥ 50 000 (possibly ~65 000); interbred with Neanderthals and Denisovans.
