Structural vs regulatory genes

Structural genesRegulatory genes
Code for proteins that make up the cell or do its work (enzymes, structural proteins, transport proteins)Code for proteins (or RNA) that control the expression of other genes, e.g. transcription factors and repressors
Example: genes for the enzymes that make tryptophanExample: trpR, which codes for the trp repressor

Regulation saves energy and resources: a cell makes a protein only when it's needed. In eukaryotes, transcription factors bind near promoters to switch genes on or off; in prokaryotes, related genes are often grouped into operons controlled together.

The trp operon

E. coli can make the amino acid tryptophan using five enzymes coded by the structural genes trpE, trpD, trpC, trpB, trpA. These share one promoter and one operator (the repressor's binding site). The regulatory gene trpR is elsewhere on the chromosome.

Repression (the main on/off switch)

Tryptophan levelWhat happensResult
LowThe repressor protein (made from trpR) is inactive: it can't bind the operator. RNA polymerase transcribes the structural genes.Enzymes made; the cell synthesises tryptophan
HighTryptophan acts as a corepressor: it binds the repressor, changing its shape so it binds the operator. RNA polymerase is blocked.Transcription stops; energy isn't wasted making tryptophan the cell already has

Attenuation (fine-tuning)

Even when the repressor isn't bound, transcription can be stopped early. The leader sequence (trpL) contains four regions (1–4) that can pair up to form hairpins, and region 1 contains two tryptophan codons. In bacteria, translation of the leader begins while it's still being transcribed:

  • High tryptophan: plenty of tryptophan-carrying tRNA, so the ribosome translates region 1 quickly and covers region 2. Regions 3 and 4 pair to form a terminator hairpin, and RNA polymerase detaches: transcription stops before the structural genes.
  • Low tryptophan: the ribosome stalls at the trp codons in region 1 (waiting for scarce trp-tRNA). Region 2 pairs with region 3 (an antiterminator), so the 3–4 terminator can't form, and transcription continues into the structural genes.

Attenuation only works in prokaryotes, because transcription and translation are coupled (no nucleus). Repression gives a coarse on/off response; attenuation adjusts transcription according to how much tryptophan-tRNA is available.

A mutation in trpR produces a repressor that can't bind tryptophan. Predict the effect on expression of the trp structural genes when tryptophan is abundant, and explain.

The trp operon is repressible. When tryptophan is plentiful, it acts as a corepressor: it binds the repressor protein, changing its shape so it binds the operator and blocks RNA polymerase. Without that binding, the operon stays on.

The repressor needs tryptophan (corepressor) bound to change shape and attach to the operator.

A repressor that can't bind tryptophan stays inactive, so the operator stays free and RNA polymerase can transcribe.

Repression fails, so the structural genes are expressed even when tryptophan is high (although attenuation can still reduce transcription somewhat). The cell wastes energy making tryptophan.

  • The repressor is made all the time (trpR is expressed constantly); it's activated by tryptophan.
  • trpR isn't part of the operon: it has its own promoter elsewhere.
  • Tryptophan is the corepressor, not the repressor.
  • Attenuation stops transcription (early termination), not translation of the structural genes.
  • The trp operon is repressible (normally on, switched off by its product); don't confuse it with inducible systems.

During attenuation, when tryptophan is scarce,

  1. the ribosome moves rapidly through the leader, allowing the terminator hairpin to form
  2. the repressor binds the attenuator
  3. the ribosome stalls at the trp codons, region 2 pairs with region 3, and transcription continues
  4. RNA polymerase can't bind the promoter

The 2–3 antiterminator prevents the 3–4 terminator from forming.

Attenuation of the trp operon wouldn't work in a eukaryotic cell mainly because

  1. eukaryotes don't need tryptophan
  2. eukaryotic transcription (nucleus) and translation (cytoplasm) are separated
  3. eukaryotes have no ribosomes
  4. eukaryotic mRNA has no codons for tryptophan

Attenuation depends on a ribosome translating the leader while RNA polymerase is still transcribing.

  2. transcribed only when tryptophan is low
  3. transcribed at exactly the normal high-tryptophan rate

Repression is lost (the operator can't be blocked), but attenuation acts on the leader transcript and still works.

Explain how the trp operon prevents E. coli wasting energy when tryptophan is available in its environment.

Tryptophan enters the cell and binds to the repressor protein (corepressor). [1] The activated repressor binds the operator, blocking RNA polymerase from transcribing the structural genes. [1] The enzymes for tryptophan synthesis aren't made, saving amino acids and ATP. [1]

Researchers change the two tryptophan codons in the leader sequence to codons for alanine. Predict the effect on attenuation when tryptophan is low but alanine is abundant.

The ribosome would no longer stall when tryptophan is low, because it doesn't need trp-tRNA to translate region 1. [1] It would move quickly, cover region 2, and allow the 3–4 terminator hairpin to form. [1] So transcription would terminate early even though the cell needs tryptophan: attenuation would respond to alanine, not tryptophan. [1]

Compare repression and attenuation as ways of regulating the trp operon.

Both reduce transcription of the structural genes when tryptophan is high. [1] Repression: tryptophan-activated repressor binds the operator and stops transcription from starting. [1] Attenuation: transcription starts but terminates early in the leader region when a terminator hairpin forms, which depends on ribosome speed (the availability of trp-tRNA). [1] Repression is an on/off switch; attenuation fine-tunes the level. [1]

A student claims the trpR gene is switched off when tryptophan is high. Explain why this is incorrect.

trpR is expressed continuously (constitutively), so repressor protein is always present. Tryptophan regulates the operon by binding to and activating existing repressor, not by changing trpR expression.

Summary

  • Regulatory genes (trpR) control structural genes (trpE–A).
  • High trp: trp + repressor → binds operator → no transcription.
  • Attenuation: ribosome speed in the leader decides between terminator (3–4) and antiterminator (2–3).
