Fossils

A fossil is any preserved evidence of past life: bones, shells, imprints, footprints, preserved tissue, amber inclusions. Fossilisation is rare. It usually needs rapid burial in sediment, low oxygen (slowing decay) and hard parts (bone, shell, wood), so the record is incomplete and biased towards organisms with hard parts living where sediment accumulates.

Dating fossils

Relative datingAbsolute dating
Gives order (older/younger), not an age in yearsGives an age in years
Stratigraphy: in undisturbed sedimentary rock, lower layers are older (law of superposition)Radiometric dating: measures the proportion of a radioactive isotope remaining, using its known half-life
Index fossils: species that were widespread but existed only briefly; finding one dates the layerCarbon-14 (half-life 5730 years): once-living material up to about 50 000 years. Potassium-40 → argon-40 (half-life 1.25 billion years): volcanic rock layers millions of years old

A bone contains 12.5% of the carbon-14 found in living tissue. How old is it?

100% → 50% → 25% → 12.5%: three half-lives.

\(3 \times 5730 = 17\,190\) years (about 17 000 years).

Transitional fossils show features intermediate between an ancestral group and its descendants. Examples: Tiktaalik (about 375 million years ago: fish with limb-like fins, a neck and lungs, between fish and tetrapods) and Archaeopteryx (feathers and wings, like birds, plus teeth, claws and a bony tail, like dinosaurs).

What is a species?

Under the biological species concept, a species is a group of organisms that can interbreed in nature to produce viable, fertile offspring. (Horse × donkey → mule, which is sterile, so horses and donkeys are separate species.)

Speciation

AllopatricSympatric
IsolationGeographical barrier (mountains, ocean, river, habitat loss) separates populationsNo geographical barrier; populations share an area
How it happensNo gene flow; different selection pressures, mutations and drift change each gene pool; eventually they can't interbreed even if reunitedReproductive isolation arises within one area, e.g. polyploidy in plants (a chromosome-number change makes offspring unable to breed with the parent population), or shifts in feeding or breeding time
ExampleGalápagos finches: birds from the mainland colonised different islands with different food sources; beak shapes diverged (adaptive radiation)Howea palms on Lord Howe Island: two palm species evolved on one tiny island. One population colonised alkaline (calcareous) soils and began flowering several weeks earlier than the population on volcanic soils; the difference in flowering time stopped gene flow and the populations diverged. (Polyploidy in plants is another route.)

Galápagos finches (allopatric): populations on different islands, separated by ocean, faced different food sources; beak shape and size diverged until they became separate species. Howea palms, Lord Howe Island (sympatric): with no geographical barrier on an island only about 11 km long, a shift in soil type and flowering time reproductively isolated two populations, which became Howea forsteriana (the kentia palm) and Howea belmoreana.

(1) A population is split by a geographical barrier. (2) No gene flow between the groups. (3) Different selection pressures (and random mutation and drift) act on each. (4) Allele frequencies diverge over many generations. (5) Reproductive isolation develops: they can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring even if the barrier is removed. Two species.

  • Relative dating gives order only, not age in years.
  • Carbon-14 can't date dinosaur fossils (millions of years): too little C-14 would remain. Date the volcanic layers around them instead.
  • Geographical isolation alone isn't speciation; reproductive isolation must develop.
  • Sterile hybrids mean different species.

Practice

Which condition makes fossilisation most likely?

  1. death in an oxygen-rich environment
  2. rapid burial in fine sediment
  3. soft body with no bones
  4. being eaten by scavengers

Rapid burial protects remains from decay and scavengers.

A good index fossil is a species that

  1. lived for a very long time in one small area
  2. is found only in volcanic rock
  3. had no hard parts
  4. was widespread but existed for a relatively short time

That combination pins down a narrow time range across many locations.

Charcoal from a campfire contains 25% of the C-14 of living wood. Its age is about

  1. 2865 years
  2. 5730 years
  3. 11 460 years
  4. 22 920 years

Two half-lives: \(2 \times 5730\).

Lions and tigers can produce offspring (ligers) in captivity, but male ligers are sterile and the species don't interbreed in the wild. This shows that lions and tigers are

  1. separate species
  2. the same species
  3. undergoing allopatric speciation now
  4. polyploid

No fertile offspring in nature: separate species.

Which is essential for allopatric speciation?

  1. polyploidy
  2. a geographical barrier preventing gene flow
  3. identical selection pressures on both populations
  4. a large amount of gene flow

“Allo” = other, “patric” = homeland: separated places.

Explain why the fossil record is incomplete.

Fossilisation requires rare conditions (rapid burial, low oxygen, hard parts), so most organisms decay without fossilising. Many fossils are also destroyed by erosion or geological activity, or haven't been discovered.

A population of beetles is divided when a river changes course. After thousands of years, beetles from the two sides can no longer produce fertile offspring. Explain how this happened.

The river acted as a geographical barrier, preventing gene flow between the two populations. [1] Each side experienced different selection pressures (and random mutations and genetic drift). [1] Over many generations, allele frequencies in the two gene pools diverged. [1] Reproductive isolation developed, so they became separate species (allopatric speciation). [1]

Explain why Tiktaalik is described as a transitional fossil and what it shows about evolution.

It has a mix of fish features (scales, fins, gills) and tetrapod features (limb-like fin bones, a neck, a flattened head, lungs). [1] It's intermediate between lobe-finned fish and early four-limbed animals. [1] It supports the hypothesis that tetrapods evolved from fish ancestors through gradual change. [1]

A student proposes using carbon-14 to date a 70-million-year-old dinosaur bone. Explain why this won't work and suggest an alternative.

After about 50 000 years (about 9 half-lives), too little C-14 remains to measure. Instead, date volcanic layers above and below the fossil using a long half-life isotope such as potassium-40 (to argon-40).

Two species of Howea palm evolved on Lord Howe Island, which is too small to contain a geographical barrier. The key isolating factor was

  1. a mountain range dividing the island
  2. migration of one population to the mainland
  3. a difference in flowering time linked to different soil types, which stopped gene flow
  4. hybrid sterility caused by polyploidy

Temporal (flowering-time) isolation without a geographical barrier: sympatric speciation.

Summary

  • Fossils need rapid burial, low oxygen and hard parts; the record is incomplete.
  • Relative dating (stratigraphy, index fossils) vs absolute dating (radiometric half-lives).
  • Species: interbreed to produce fertile offspring.
  • Allopatric: barrier → no gene flow → divergence → reproductive isolation. Sympatric: isolation without a barrier (e.g. Howea palms on Lord Howe Island: flowering-time differences; or polyploidy).
