Command words

WordWhat earns the marks
Identify / name / stateA precise term or fact; no explanation needed.
DescribeWhat happens, in sequence or with features. For graphs: the trend, with data values.
ExplainHow or why: a linked chain of cause and effect using biological concepts.
CompareSimilarities and differences, each point about both things (“whereas…”).
JustifyGive evidence (often from data in the question) for a claim.
Evaluate / discussStrengths and limitations, or benefits and risks, then a conclusion.
PredictState what will happen and why, using biological reasoning.

Writing explanations that score

  • Use exact terms: “template strand”, “non-competitive inhibitor”, “allele frequency”, “plasma cells”, “MHC class I”. Vague words (“stuff”, “the thing”, “it”) lose marks.
  • Link each step: “the repressor binds the operator, so RNA polymerase can't bind, so the structural genes aren't transcribed.”
  • Match the marks: a 3-mark “explain” needs three linked points.
  • Answer in the context given: if the question is about a specific plant, pathogen or population, refer to it by name and use its data.

Unfamiliar scenarios

Many Section B questions describe a situation you've never seen (a new enzyme, an unusual organism, a fictional island). They always test study-design concepts. Strategy: (1) identify which concept is being tested; (2) apply the standard explanation; (3) use the specific names and data from the stem.

Data and graphs

  • Describe trends with data: “the rate increased from 2 to 8 units between 10 and 30 °C, then fell to 0 at 50 °C.”
  • Distinguish correlation from causation.
  • Check axes and units, and whether a scale is logarithmic.
  • See key science skills for variables, controls, errors, accuracy and precision.

Errors examiners' reports mention every year

  • Confusing codon (mRNA), anticodon (tRNA) and triplet (DNA); saying tRNA carries codons.
  • Saying the Golgi makes proteins, or that ribosomes are made in the Golgi.
  • Writing NADH in photosynthesis, or NADPH in respiration.
  • Saying enzymes are “killed”, or that low temperature denatures enzymes.
  • Saying antibodies kill pathogens directly; confusing plasma cells and memory cells.
  • Mixing up MHC I (all nucleated cells, cytotoxic T cells) and MHC II (antigen-presenting cells, T helper cells).
  • Lamarckian wording: organisms “adapt because they need to”, or mutations “occur in response to” the environment.
  • Saying individuals evolve, rather than populations.
  • Calling a vaccine passive immunity.
