Explain the low activity (0–20 min) and steady activity (after ~60 min), including how repression and attenuation work together

  • 0–20 min: trp present, so enzymes are not needed and activity is low
  • Repression: trp binds the repressor, which binds the operator and blocks RNA polymerase
  • Attenuation: with high trp the ribosome moves quickly through the leader, the terminator loop forms and transcription stops early
  • After the shift (no trp): the repressor is inactive and there is no attenuation, so the structural genes are transcribed and enzymes made
  • Activity levels off once enzyme production reaches a steady rate

Refer to both time periods. Repression is the main on/off switch, and attenuation fine-tunes it. With trp absent, both are released and the enzymes rise to a plateau.

Contrast the adaptive response to an extracellular vs an intracellular pathogen

  • Extracellular: humoral immunity; intracellular: cell-mediated immunity
  • Extracellular: B cells become plasma cells that make antibodies
  • Intracellular: cytotoxic T cells recognise infected cells via MHC I
  • Outcome: antibodies neutralise or tag free pathogens, while cytotoxic T cells induce apoptosis of infected cells

Use paired contrasts. Humoral (B cells, antibodies) for pathogens outside cells, cell-mediated (Tc, apoptosis) for pathogens inside them. 41% scored zero.
