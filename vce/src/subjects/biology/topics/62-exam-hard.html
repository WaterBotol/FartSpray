A single-stranded viral genome contains 22% A, 30% U, 28% G and 20% C. This shows that

  1. it is DNA, because A does not equal T
  2. it is RNA, and base-pairing rules do not require A = U in a single strand

U means RNA. Chargaff’s rule (A = T/U, G = C) only applies to double-stranded molecules, where every base has a partner.

Valine’s hydrophobic R group replaces glutamic acid’s charged one on the protein surface. At low oxygen, this sticky patch binds to neighbouring haemoglobin molecules, forming long fibres that distort the cell into a sickle shape.
