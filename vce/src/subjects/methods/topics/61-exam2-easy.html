Mathematical Methods · Exam 2 practice (easier)

  • Reading time: 15 minutes. Writing time: 2 hours. CAS calculator and one bound reference allowed.
  • Section A: 20 multiple-choice questions (20 marks). Section B: 4 extended-response questions (60 marks).
  • Give exact answers where asked. Otherwise give decimals to the accuracy requested. Show the setup (the equation or integral you entered) even when your CAS does the work.

Section A – Multiple-choice questions

Answer all questions. Choose the response that is correct for the question. A correct answer scores 1; an incorrect answer scores 0. No marks are deducted for incorrect answers.

Section B – Extended-response questions

Answer all questions. In questions where more than one mark is available, appropriate working must be shown. Where an exact answer is required, a decimal approximation will not be accepted.

Let \(f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\), \(f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x\).

Find the \(x\)-intercepts of the graph of \(f\).

  • \(x = 0\) and \(x = 3\)

\(f(x) = x(x - 3)^2\), so the intercepts are \(x = 0\) and \(x = 3\) (a turning point).

Find the coordinates and nature of the stationary points.

  • \(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 9 = 3(x - 1)(x - 3)\)
  • Stationary points \((1, 4)\) and \((3, 0)\)
  • \((1, 4)\) local maximum, \((3, 0)\) local minimum

\(f'(x) = 0\) at \(x = 1\) and \(x = 3\). \((1, 4)\) is a local maximum and \((3, 0)\) is a local minimum.

Find the values of \(k\) for which \(f(x) = k\) has three solutions.

  • \(0 < k < 4\)

A horizontal line meets the graph three times strictly between the two turning points: \(0 < k < 4\).

Find the equation of the tangent to the graph of \(f\) at \(x = 4\).

  • \(f(4) = 4\) and \(f'(4) = 9\)
  • \(y = 9x - 32\)

\(y - 4 = 9(x - 4)\), so \(y = 9x - 32\).

Find the area enclosed by the graph of \(f\) and the \(x\)-axis.

  • \(\int_0^3 (x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x)\,dx\)
  • \(\frac{27}{4}\)

\(\int_0^3 f(x)\,dx = \frac{27}{4}\)

Find the average value of \(f\) over \([0, 3]\).

  • \(\frac94\)

\(\frac13 \times \frac{27}{4} = \frac94\)

Let \(g(x) = f(x - 2) + 1\). Describe the transformation from \(f\) to \(g\), and give the coordinates of the local maximum of \(g\).

  • Translation 2 units right and 1 unit up
  • \((3, 5)\)

A translation of 2 units in the positive \(x\) direction and 1 unit in the positive \(y\) direction. The local maximum moves from \((1, 4)\) to \((3, 5)\).

Find the value of \(a\) for which the tangent at \(x = a\) has gradient \(-3\).

  • \(a = 2\)

\(3a^2 - 12a + 9 = -3\) gives \(3(a - 2)^2 = 0\), so \(a = 2\).

Newton’s method is used to solve \(f(x) = 1\), starting at \(x_0 = 0\). Find \(x_1\) and \(x_2\), correct to three decimal places.

  • Uses \(h(x) = f(x) - 1\) with \(x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{h(x_n)}{h'(x_n)}\): \(x_1 = \frac19 \approx 0.111\)
  • \(x_2 \approx 0.121\)

\(h(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x - 1\), \(h'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 9\). \(x_1 = 0 - \frac{-1}{9} = 0.111\), and then \(x_2 = 0.121\).

Applying Newton’s method to \(f\) instead of \(f - 1\) was the key error.

The height of the tide at a jetty, in metres, is modelled by \(h(t) = 3 + 2\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right)\), where \(t\) is the number of hours after midnight, \(0 \le t \le 24\).

State the maximum and minimum heights.

  • 5 m and 1 m

Maximum 5 m, minimum 1 m.

State the period of the model.

  • 12 hours

\(\frac{2\pi}{\pi/6} = 12\) hours.

Find the first time after midnight when the height is 4 m.

  • \(\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right) = \frac12\)
  • \(t = 1\) (1:00 am)

\(\frac{\pi t}{6} = \frac{\pi}{6}\), so \(t = 1\): 1:00 am.

For how many hours in the 24-hour period is the height at least 4 m?

  • \(\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right) \ge \frac12\) for \(\frac{\pi t}{6} \in \left[\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{5\pi}{6}\right]\), i.e. \(t \in [1, 5]\)
  • 4 hours per cycle
  • Two cycles: 8 hours

In each 12-hour cycle, \(h \ge 4\) for \(1 \le t \le 5\), which is 4 hours. There are two cycles, so 8 hours in total.

Find the maximum rate at which the tide rises, and a time at which this occurs.

  • \(h'(t) = \frac{\pi}{3}\cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right)\), with a maximum of \(\frac{\pi}{3}\) m/h (≈ 1.05)
  • At \(t = 0\) (midnight), or \(t = 12, 24\)

\(h'(t) = \frac{\pi}{3}\cos\frac{\pi t}{6}\) has a maximum of \(\frac{\pi}{3} \approx 1.05\) m per hour, at midnight (and at noon).

Find the average height between \(t = 0\) and \(t = 3\). Give an exact answer.

  • \(\frac13\int_0^3 h(t)\,dt\)
  • \(3 + \frac{4}{\pi}\)

\(\frac13\left[3t - \frac{12}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi t}{6}\right]_0^3 = \frac13\left(9 + \frac{12}{\pi}\right) = 3 + \frac4\pi \approx 4.27\) m

A boat can only use the jetty when the height is at least 2 m. Find the times between midnight and noon when it cannot use the jetty.

  • Solve \(h(t) = 2\): \(\sin\frac{\pi t}{6} = -\frac12\), giving \(t = 7\) and \(t = 11\)
  • Between 7:00 am and 11:00 am

\(h(t) < 2\) when \(\sin\frac{\pi t}{6} < -\frac12\), i.e. \(7 < t < 11\): from 7:00 am to 11:00 am.

The model is changed to \(h(t) = 3 + 2\sin\left(\frac{\pi(t - c)}{6}\right)\) so that the first high tide occurs at 5:00 am. Find the smallest positive value of \(c\).

  • High tide when \(\frac{\pi(t - c)}{6} = \frac{\pi}{2}\), i.e. \(t - c = 3\)
  • \(c = 2\)

The original high tide is at \(t = 3\). Shifting it to \(t = 5\) needs \(c = 2\).

  • 0.0668

\(\Pr(X > 1350) = \Pr(Z > 1.5) = 0.0668\)

  • \(\Pr(X > a) = 0.9\), i.e. inverse normal with area 0.1 to the left
  • \(a = 1072\) hours

\(a = 1200 - 1.2816 \times 100 \approx 1072\) hours.

Assessor’s report

General comments

This paper followed the Exam 2 format at a gentler level. The four extended questions each built from routine parts (intercepts, derivatives, a standard probability) to one or two parts that needed thought (Newton’s method on \(f - 1\), hours above a threshold, a conditional binomial). Students who used their CAS efficiently and wrote the setup for each part scored well.

Where marks were lost

  • Not showing the setup. A bare decimal from the CAS earns the answer mark at best. Write the equation, integral or distribution you used.
  • Domain checks. In Questions 4d and 4f, solutions outside \(0 < x < 10\) must be rejected.
  • Reading what is asked. Question 2d asked for the total time over 24 hours, not one cycle. Question 3b asked for the lifetime exceeded by 90%, which is the 10th percentile.
  • Newton’s method. To solve \(f(x) = 1\), apply the method to \(f(x) - 1\).
  • Interpreting confidence intervals. “0.05 is inside the interval, so the claim is consistent with the data” is the expected wording. A CI never proves a claim.

Advice

Aim for 65/80 or more. If Section A was strong but Section B weak, practise writing the setup line for every CAS step. It is the habit that protects method marks.
