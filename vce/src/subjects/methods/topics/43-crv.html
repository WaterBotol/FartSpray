Probability density functions

A continuous random variable takes any value in an interval. Its probability density function \(f\) satisfies:

Conditions for a pdf
\[ f(x) \ge 0 \text{ for all } x \qquad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 1 \qquad P(a < X < b) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx \]

\(P(X = a) = 0\) for any single value, so \(<\) and \(\le\) give the same probability. (For discrete variables they don't!)

Summary statistics
\[ E(X) = \int x f(x)\,dx \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X) = \int x^2 f(x)\,dx - \mu^2 \qquad \int_{-\infty}^{m} f(x)\,dx = \frac12 \ (\text{median } m) \]

The \(p\)th percentile \(q\) satisfies \(\int_{-\infty}^{q} f(x)\,dx = \frac{p}{100}\). \(E(aX + b) = aE(X) + b\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(aX + b) = a^2\operatorname{Var}(X)\), exactly as for discrete variables.

\(f(x) = kx^2\) for \(0 \le x \le 3\), and 0 elsewhere. Find (a) \(k\), (b) \(P(X < 1)\), (c) \(E(X)\), (d) the median, (e) \(\operatorname{Var}(X)\).

For a continuous random variable, probability is area under the pdf. The total area must be 1. The mean uses \(\int x f(x)\,dx\), the median splits the area in half, and variance uses \(E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2\) as in the discrete case.

(a) \(\int_0^3 kx^2\,dx = 9k = 1 \Rightarrow k = \frac19\).

(b) \(\int_0^1\frac{x^2}{9}dx = \frac{1}{27}\).

(c) \(E(X) = \int_0^3\frac{x^3}{9}dx = \frac{81}{36} = \frac94\).

(d) \(\int_0^m\frac{x^2}{9}dx = \frac{m^3}{27} = \frac12 \Rightarrow m = \frac{3}{\sqrt[3]{2}} \approx 2.381\).

(e) \(E(X^2) = \int_0^3\frac{x^4}{9}dx = \frac{243}{45} = 5.4\); \(\operatorname{Var} = 5.4 - \left(\frac94\right)^2 = 0.3375\).

\(k = \frac19\); \(P(X < 1) = \frac1{27}\); \(E(X) = 2.25\); median \(\approx 2.381\); \(\operatorname{Var} = 0.3375\). The median is bigger than the mean here because the distribution is skewed left (bunched at high values).

\(f(x) = \begin{cases} x, & 0 \le x \le 1 \\ 2 - x, & 1 < x \le 2 \\ 0, & \text{elsewhere} \end{cases}\). Find \(P(X < 0.5)\), \(E(X)\) and \(P(X > 1.5 \mid X > 1)\).

A piecewise pdf is integrated one piece at a time, each over its own interval. A pdf symmetric about \(x = a\) has mean and median \(a\).

\(P(X < 0.5) = \int_0^{0.5}x\,dx = 0.125\).

The graph is symmetric about \(x = 1\), so \(E(X) = 1\) (and the median is 1).

\(P(X > 1.5) = \int_{1.5}^2(2 - x)\,dx = 0.125\) and \(P(X > 1) = 0.5\).

0.125; 1; \(\frac{0.125}{0.5} = 0.25\). For a piecewise pdf, integrate each piece over its own interval.

The time \(T\) (minutes) between customer arrivals has pdf \(f(t) = 0.5e^{-0.5t}\), \(t \ge 0\). Find \(P(T > 2)\) and the median time.

Probabilities for an unbounded pdf use an integral out to infinity, where \(e^{-kt} \to 0\). The median is the \(m\) where the area to its left is exactly half.

\(P(T > 2) = \int_2^\infty 0.5e^{-0.5t}dt = \left[-e^{-0.5t}\right]_2^\infty = e^{-1}\).

Median: \(\int_0^m 0.5e^{-0.5t}dt = 1 - e^{-0.5m} = \frac12 \Rightarrow m = 2\log_e 2\).

\(P(T > 2) = e^{-1} \approx 0.368\); median \(= 2\log_e 2 \approx 1.386\) min (less than the mean of 2 min: the long right tail pulls the mean up).

  • \(f(x)\) isn't a probability. A pdf value can be bigger than 1; only areas are probabilities.
  • The median is where the area is \(\frac12\), not where \(f\) is \(\frac12\), and not halfway along the domain.
  • For a pdf, “find \(k\)” needs both conditions: total area 1 and \(f \ge 0\) (sometimes this rules out a solution).
  • Conditional: \(P(X > a \mid X > b) = \frac{P(X > a)}{P(X > b)}\) only when \(a \ge b\) (so the first event is inside the second).

For a continuous random variable \(X\), \(P(X \le 3) = 0.7\). Then \(P(X < 3)\) is

  1. less than 0.7
  2. 0.3
  3. impossible to find
  4. 0.7

\(P(X = 3) = 0\) for a continuous variable, so the two are equal.

  2. \(\frac14\)
  3. \(\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\)
  4. \(\frac12\)

\(\frac12[-\cos x]_0^{\pi/3} = \frac12\left(1 - \frac12\right) = \frac14\).

\(X\) has mean 5 and standard deviation 2. \(Y = 10 - 3X\). The mean and standard deviation of \(Y\) are

  1. \(-5\) and \(-6\)
  2. \(-5\) and 36
  3. \(-5\) and 6
  4. 5 and 6

\(E(Y) = 10 - 15 = -5\); \(\operatorname{sd}(Y) = |-3| \times 2 = 6\). A standard deviation is never negative; 36 is the variance.

\(f(x) = 2x\) for \(0 \le x \le 1\). The median of \(X\) is

  1. \(\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\)
  2. \(\frac12\)
  3. \(\frac23\)
  4. \(\frac14\)

\(\int_0^m 2x\,dx = m^2 = \frac12 \Rightarrow m = \frac{1}{\sqrt2} \approx 0.707\). (\(\frac23\) is the mean; \(\frac12\) is halfway along the domain.)

\(f(x) = \frac{3}{8}x^2\) for \(0 \le x \le 2\). Find \(E(X)\) and the 90th percentile.

\(E(X) = \int_0^2\frac38x^3\,dx = \frac38 \cdot 4 = 1.5\).

\(\int_0^q\frac38x^2\,dx = \frac{q^3}{8} = 0.9 \Rightarrow q = \sqrt[3]{7.2} \approx 1.931\).

\(f(x) = \begin{cases} a, & 0 \le x < 1 \\ \frac{a}{x^2}, & 1 \le x \le 4 \\ 0, & \text{elsewhere}\end{cases}\) is a pdf. Find \(a\) and \(P(X > 2)\).

\(a + a\left[-\frac1x\right]_1^4 = a + a\left(1 - \frac14\right) = \frac74a = 1 \Rightarrow a = \frac47\). [2]

\(P(X > 2) = \frac47\left[-\frac1x\right]_2^4 = \frac47\left(\frac12 - \frac14\right) = \frac17\). [1]

The time \(T\) hours that a battery lasts has pdf \(f(t) = \frac{1}{50}e^{-t/50}\), \(t \ge 0\). (a) Find the probability a battery lasts more than 100 hours. (b) Find the probability that a battery lasts more than 150 hours given that it has already lasted 50 hours. (c) Comment on your answers.

(a) \(P(T > 100) = e^{-2} \approx 0.1353\). [1]

(b) \(\frac{P(T > 150)}{P(T > 50)} = \frac{e^{-3}}{e^{-1}} = e^{-2} \approx 0.1353\). [2]

(c) The same: the battery doesn't “remember” it's already been used (the memoryless property of this distribution). [1]

A student says \(f(x) = 3\) on \([0, \frac13]\) can't be a pdf because \(f(x) > 1\). Are they right?

No. \(f(x) \ge 0\) and the total area is \(3 \times \frac13 = 1\), so it's a valid pdf. Density values can exceed 1; probabilities (areas) can't.

Summary

  • pdf: \(f \ge 0\), total area 1; probabilities are areas.
  • Mean \(\int xf\), variance \(\int x^2f - \mu^2\), median: area to the left \(= \frac12\).
  • Piecewise: integrate piece by piece. Conditional: divide areas.
