Tangents and normals

Tangent and normal at \(x = a\)
\[ \text{tangent: } y - f(a) = f'(a)(x - a) \qquad \text{normal: } y - f(a) = -\frac{1}{f'(a)}(x - a) \]

The normal is perpendicular to the tangent: \(m_1 m_2 = -1\).

Find the equations of the tangent and the normal to \(y = x^3 - 2x\) at \(x = 1\).

Point: \(y(1) = 1 - 2 = -1\), so \((1, -1)\). Gradient: \(y' = 3x^2 - 2\), so \(y'(1) = 1\).

Tangent: \(y + 1 = 1(x - 1) \Rightarrow y = x - 2\). Normal gradient \(= -1\): \(y + 1 = -(x - 1) \Rightarrow y = -x\).

Tangent \(y = x - 2\); normal \(y = -x\).

Find the equations of the tangents to \(y = x^2\) that pass through the point \((1, -3)\).

Let the point of contact be \((a, a^2)\). The gradient there is \(2a\), so the tangent is \(y - a^2 = 2a(x - a)\), i.e. \(y = 2ax - a^2\).

It must pass through \((1, -3)\): \(-3 = 2a - a^2 \Rightarrow a^2 - 2a - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow (a - 3)(a + 1) = 0\).

\(a = 3\): \(y = 6x - 9\); \(a = -1\): \(y = -2x - 1\). The external point isn't on the curve, so you can't use it as the point of contact. Use a general point instead.

Rates of change

Average rate of change over \([a, b]\) = \(\dfrac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}\) (gradient of a secant). Instantaneous rate at \(x = a\) = \(f'(a)\) (gradient of the tangent). Read the question carefully: VCAA asks for both, often in the same question.

Stationary points and their nature

Stationary points occur where \(f'(x) = 0\). Classify with a sign diagram of \(f'\) or with the second derivative:

Sign of \(f'\) (left → right)\(f''(a)\)Nature
\(+\ \to\ -\)\(< 0\)local maximum
\(-\ \to\ +\)\(> 0\)local minimum
\(+\ \to\ +\) or \(-\ \to\ -\)\(= 0\) (test fails!)stationary point of inflection

\(f(x) = x^4\) has \(f'(0) = 0\) and \(f''(0) = 0\), but \((0, 0)\) is a local minimum. When the second derivative is 0, go back to the sign test.

For \(f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 5\), find the stationary points and their nature, the point of inflection, and the intervals where \(f\) is strictly decreasing.

\(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x - 9 = 3(x - 3)(x + 1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -1, 3\).

\(f(-1) = -1 - 3 + 9 + 5 = 10\); \(f(3) = 27 - 27 - 27 + 5 = -22\). \(f''(x) = 6x - 6\): \(f''(-1) = -12 < 0\) (max), \(f''(3) = 12 > 0\) (min).

Inflection: \(f''(x) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 1\), and \(f''\) changes sign there. \(f(1) = -6\).

Local max \((-1, 10)\), local min \((3, -22)\), inflection \((1, -6)\). Strictly decreasing on \([-1, 3]\). VCAA includes the endpoints of monotone intervals (the function is still strictly decreasing on the closed interval).

Concavity and points of inflection

\(f''(x) > 0\): concave up (gradient increasing). \(f''(x) < 0\): concave down. A point of inflection is where the concavity changes; it may or may not be stationary. At a non-stationary inflection point the gradient is at a local max or min, so it's where a quantity grows fastest or slowest (a favourite Exam 2 context).

For \(f(x) = xe^{-x}\), find the coordinates of the stationary point and the point of inflection.

\(f'(x) = e^{-x} - xe^{-x} = (1 - x)e^{-x} = 0 \Rightarrow x = 1\). \(f(1) = e^{-1}\); \(f'\) goes \(+ \to -\), so it's a maximum.

\(f''(x) = -e^{-x} - (1 - x)e^{-x} = (x - 2)e^{-x} = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2\), and it changes sign.

Local max \(\left(1, \frac1e\right)\); inflection \(\left(2, \frac{2}{e^2}\right)\).

Sketching the gradient function

  • Stationary points of \(f\) → \(x\)-intercepts of \(f'\).
  • \(f\) increasing → \(f'\) above the axis; \(f\) decreasing → \(f'\) below.
  • Inflection points of \(f\) → turning points of \(f'\).
  • Corners/cusps of \(f\) → open circles or gaps in \(f'\). Vertical asymptotes stay as asymptotes.

Linear approximation

Linear approximation
\[ f(x + h) \approx f(x) + h\,f'(x) \]

Use the tangent line to estimate nearby values. Example: \(\sqrt{16.2} \approx 4 + 0.2\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{16}} = 4.025\) (true value 4.0249…).

  • Stationary point ≠ turning point. Turning points are only the maxima and minima; stationary inflections also have \(f' = 0\).
  • Local ≠ absolute. The absolute maximum on a closed interval might be at an endpoint.
  • Normal gradient: negative reciprocal. If the tangent is horizontal, the normal is vertical (\(x = a\)).
  • A factor squared in \(f'\) (e.g. \(f'(x) = (x - 2)^2(x + 1)\)) gives no sign change at \(x = 2\): stationary inflection.

Practice

The equation of the tangent to \(y = e^{2x}\) at \(x = 0\) is

  1. \(y = 2x\)
  2. \(y = x + 1\)
  3. \(y = 2x + 1\)
  4. \(y = 2ex + 1\)

Point \((0, 1)\); gradient \(2e^0 = 2\). So \(y = 2x + 1\).

\(f'(x) = (x - 2)^2(x + 1)\). The graph of \(f\) has

  1. a local max at \(x = -1\) and a local min at \(x = 2\)
  2. a local min at \(x = -1\) and a stationary point of inflection at \(x = 2\)
  3. a local max at \(x = 2\) only
  4. local minima at \(x = -1\) and \(x = 2\)

Sign of \(f'\): negative for \(x < -1\), positive between \(-1\) and 2, positive after 2. So min at \(-1\); no sign change at 2 (squared factor): stationary inflection.

The average rate of change of \(f(x) = x^2 + 1\) over \([1, 3]\) is

  1. 2
  2. 6
  3. 8
  4. 4

\(\frac{f(3) - f(1)}{3 - 1} = \frac{10 - 2}{2} = 4\). (6 is the instantaneous rate at \(x = 3\).)

The function \(f(x) = x^3 - 12x\) is strictly decreasing on

  1. \([-2, 2]\)
  2. \((-\infty, -2]\)
  3. \([2, \infty)\)
  4. \([-2\sqrt3, 2\sqrt3]\)

\(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12 \le 0\) for \(-2 \le x \le 2\) (zero only at the endpoints).

For \(f(x) = x^4 - 4x^3\), which statement is true?

  1. \((0, 0)\) is a local maximum
  2. \((0, 0)\) is a local minimum
  3. \((0, 0)\) is a stationary point of inflection
  4. \(f\) has no stationary point at \(x = 0\)

\(f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x^2 = 4x^2(x - 3)\). Negative on both sides of 0 (squared factor), so there's no sign change: stationary inflection. The minimum is at \(x = 3\).

The normal to \(y = \sqrt{x}\) at \(x = 4\) crosses the \(x\)-axis at

  1. \(x = 3\)
  2. \(x = 4.5\)
  3. \(x = 8\)
  4. \(x = -4\)

Point \((4, 2)\); tangent gradient \(\frac{1}{2\sqrt4} = \frac14\), so normal gradient \(-4\). Normal: \(y - 2 = -4(x - 4)\). At \(y = 0\): \(x - 4 = \frac12\), so \(x = 4.5\). Option D is where the tangent (not the normal) crosses the axis.

Find the coordinates and nature of the stationary points of \(y = 2x^3 + 3x^2 - 12x\).

\(y' = 6x^2 + 6x - 12 = 6(x + 2)(x - 1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -2, 1\).

\(y(-2) = -16 + 12 + 24 = 20\): local max \((-2, 20)\). \(y(1) = 2 + 3 - 12 = -7\): local min \((1, -7)\). (Positive cubic, so max first.)

Find the equation of the tangent to \(y = \log_e x\) that passes through the origin.

Contact point \((a, \log_e a)\), gradient \(\frac1a\). Tangent: \(y - \log_e a = \frac1a(x - a)\). [1]

Through \((0, 0)\): \(-\log_e a = -1 \Rightarrow a = e\). [1]

Tangent: \(y = \frac{x}{e}\). [1]

The number of people infected in an outbreak is modelled by \(N(t) = \dfrac{1000}{1 + 99e^{-0.5t}}\), \(t\) in days. Using CAS: (a) find the rate of infection when \(t = 5\); (b) find when the infection rate is greatest and state that rate.

(a) \(N'(5) \approx 48.8\) people per day. [1]

(b) The rate is greatest at the point of inflection of \(N\): solve \(N''(t) = 0\), giving \(t = 2\log_e 99 \approx 9.19\) days. [2]

At that time \(N = 500\) and \(N' = 0.5 \times 500 \times \frac{500}{1000} = 125\) people per day. [1]

A student says: “\(f''(0) = 0\) for \(f(x) = x^4\), so \((0, 0)\) is a point of inflection.” Correct them.

\(f''(a) = 0\) is necessary but not sufficient. Concavity must change: \(f''(x) = 12x^2 \ge 0\) on both sides of 0, so there's no change. Sign test on \(f'(x) = 4x^3\): \(- \to +\). It's a local minimum.

Summary

  • Tangent: \(y - f(a) = f'(a)(x - a)\). For tangents through an external point, use a general contact point \((a, f(a))\).
  • Stationary points: solve \(f' = 0\); classify with a sign diagram (safest) or \(f''\).
  • Inflection: \(f''\) changes sign; the gradient is at its greatest or least there.
  • Monotone intervals are given as closed intervals in VCAA solutions.
