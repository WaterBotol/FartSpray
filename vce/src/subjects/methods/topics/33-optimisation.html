The method

  1. Draw a diagram and label the variable(s).
  2. Write the quantity to optimise as a function of one variable (use the constraint to eliminate the others).
  3. State the domain (lengths positive, can't cut more than you have…).
  4. Differentiate and solve \(= 0\); check the nature.
  5. Check the endpoints and answer the question actually asked (the maximum volume? the dimensions? the time?).

VCAA marking schemes award a mark for a correct expression in one variable and often for the domain, before any calculus. Show them.

Squares of side \(x\) cm are cut from each corner of a 24 cm × 24 cm sheet, and the sides are folded up to make an open box. Find the maximum volume.

Optimisation always goes: write the quantity as a function of one variable, state the domain, set the derivative to zero, then justify it's a max or min (sign test or second derivative) and check the endpoints.

\(V = x(24 - 2x)^2\), domain \(0 < x < 12\).

\(V'(x) = (24 - 2x)^2 + x\cdot 2(24 - 2x)(-2) = (24 - 2x)(24 - 6x)\).

\(V' = 0 \Rightarrow x = 12\) (outside the domain; zero volume) or \(x = 4\). Sign: \(V' > 0\) before 4, \(< 0\) after, so it's a maximum.

\(V_{\max} = 4(16)^2 = 1024\text{ cm}^3\) when \(x = 4\).

A farmer has 60 m of fencing to enclose a rectangular pen against a long straight wall (the wall forms one side). Find the maximum area.

The fixed amount of fencing is the constraint: use it to write one side in terms of the other, so the area depends on only one variable.

Let the two sides perpendicular to the wall be \(w\). The side parallel to the wall is \(60 - 2w\). \(A = w(60 - 2w)\), \(0 < w < 30\).

\(A'(w) = 60 - 4w = 0 \Rightarrow w = 15\). \(A'' = -4 < 0\): maximum.

\(A_{\max} = 15 \times 30 = 450\text{ m}^2\).

A closed cylindrical can must hold 500 cm³. Find the radius that minimises its surface area, correct to two decimal places, and the minimum area.

There are two variables (\(r\) and \(h\)) but a fixed volume links them. Use the volume constraint to eliminate \(h\), then minimise the surface area as a function of \(r\) alone.

Constraint: \(\pi r^2 h = 500 \Rightarrow h = \dfrac{500}{\pi r^2}\).

\(S = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh = 2\pi r^2 + \dfrac{1000}{r}\), \(r > 0\).

\(S'(r) = 4\pi r - \dfrac{1000}{r^2} = 0 \Rightarrow r^3 = \dfrac{250}{\pi}\).

\(r = \left(\frac{250}{\pi}\right)^{1/3} \approx 4.30\) cm, giving \(S_{\min} \approx 348.73\text{ cm}^2\). (At the optimum \(h = 2r\): the height equals the diameter.)

Find the minimum distance from the point \((0, 3)\) to the parabola \(y = x^2\).

Distance involves a square root, which is messy to differentiate. Because the square root is increasing, the \(x\) that minimises \(d^2\) also minimises \(d\), so minimise \(D = d^2\) instead.

A point on the curve is \((x, x^2)\). Minimise the square of the distance (easier, same minimising \(x\)): \(D = x^2 + (x^2 - 3)^2\).

\(D'(x) = 2x + 4x(x^2 - 3) = 2x(2x^2 - 5) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0\) or \(x^2 = \frac52\).

\(D(0) = 9\) (local max of \(D\)); \(D\) at \(x^2 = \frac52\): \(\frac52 + \frac14 = \frac{11}{4}\).

Minimum distance \(= \sqrt{\frac{11}{4}} = \frac{\sqrt{11}}{2}\), at \(\left(\pm\sqrt{\frac52}, \frac52\right)\).

A swimmer is 3 km offshore, directly opposite point \(P\) on a straight beach. She wants to reach a café on the beach 8 km from \(P\). She swims at 2 km/h and runs at 5 km/h. Where should she land to minimise the total time?

Total time is the sum of each leg's distance divided by its speed. The swim leg is a hypotenuse, so it needs Pythagoras. Because the domain is closed, the endpoints must also be checked.

Land \(x\) km from \(P\) towards the café. \(T(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{9 + x^2}}{2} + \dfrac{8 - x}{5}\), \(0 \le x \le 8\).

\(T'(x) = \dfrac{x}{2\sqrt{9 + x^2}} - \dfrac15 = 0 \Rightarrow 5x = 2\sqrt{9 + x^2} \Rightarrow 21x^2 = 36\).

\(x = \frac{6}{\sqrt{21}} \approx 1.31\) km, giving \(T \approx 2.975\) h. Endpoints: \(T(0) = 3.1\) h, \(T(8) \approx 4.27\) h.

Land about 1.31 km from \(P\); the minimum time is about 2.97 h (2 h 58.5 min).

Endpoints: absolute max and min

On a closed interval, the absolute maximum and minimum are among the stationary points and the endpoints. Evaluate all of them and compare.

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of \(f(x) = x^3 - 3x\) on \([-2, 3]\).

On a closed interval, the absolute max and min happen either at a stationary point or at an endpoint. Evaluate the function at all of them and pick the largest and smallest.

\(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow x = \pm1\). \(f(-1) = 2\), \(f(1) = -2\).

Endpoints: \(f(-2) = -8 + 6 = -2\), \(f(3) = 27 - 9 = 18\).

Absolute max 18 (at \(x = 3\)); absolute min \(-2\) (at \(x = -2\) and \(x = 1\)). The local max of 2 isn't the absolute max.

  • Forgetting the endpoints. Especially when a stationary point is outside the domain.
  • Answering the wrong question. “Find the maximum area” wants the area, not \(x\).
  • Two variables left in the function. Use the constraint to eliminate one before differentiating.
  • Rounding too early. Keep the exact \(x\) (or full calculator value) until the final answer.

Practice

Two positive numbers have a sum of 20. The maximum value of their product is

  1. 99
  2. 100
  3. 200
  4. 400

\(P = x(20 - x)\), \(P' = 20 - 2x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 10\). \(P = 100\).

The absolute maximum of \(f(x) = x^3 - 3x\) on \([-2, 2]\) is

  1. 2, at \(x = -1\) only
  2. 18
  3. \(-2\)
  4. 2, at \(x = -1\) and \(x = 2\)

\(f(-1) = 2\) and the endpoint \(f(2) = 8 - 6 = 2\). The maximum value 2 occurs twice.

A rectangle has perimeter 40 cm. Its maximum possible area is

  1. 100 cm²
  2. 400 cm²
  3. 80 cm²
  4. 200 cm²

\(A = x(20 - x)\), maximised at \(x = 10\): a 10 × 10 square, area 100 cm².

A rectangle has two vertices on the \(x\)-axis and two on the parabola \(y = 9 - x^2\) (above the axis). Its maximum area is

  1. \(6\sqrt3\)
  2. 18
  3. \(12\sqrt3\)
  4. 36

\(A = 2x(9 - x^2)\), \(0 < x < 3\). \(A' = 18 - 6x^2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = \sqrt3\). \(A = 2\sqrt3 \times 6 = 12\sqrt3\).

A company's profit is \(P(q) = -2q^3 + 30q^2 - 96q\) (thousands of dollars) for \(0 \le q \le 10\). The maximum profit is

  1. $40 000
  2. $128 000
  3. $88 000
  4. $0

\(P'(q) = -6q^2 + 60q - 96 = -6(q - 2)(q - 8)\). \(P(2) = -88\) (min), \(P(8) = 128\) (max). Endpoints: \(P(0) = 0\), \(P(10) = 40\).

To find the point on \(y = \sqrt{x}\) closest to \((2, 0)\), a student minimises \(D(x) = (x - 2)^2 + x\). This approach is

  1. valid: minimising \(D\) (the distance squared) gives the same \(x\) as minimising the distance
  2. invalid: you must minimise \(\sqrt{D}\)
  3. invalid: you must use \(y^2\) instead of \(x\)
  4. valid only if \(D(x) > 1\)

Square root is increasing, so \(\sqrt{D}\) and \(D\) are smallest at the same \(x\). \(D' = 2(x - 2) + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 1.5\); distance \(\sqrt{0.25 + 1.5} = \frac{\sqrt7}{2}\).

An open rectangular box has a square base of side \(x\) cm and volume 32 cm³. Find the dimensions that minimise the surface area.

\(x^2h = 32 \Rightarrow h = \frac{32}{x^2}\). \(S = x^2 + 4xh = x^2 + \frac{128}{x}\).

\(S' = 2x - \frac{128}{x^2} = 0 \Rightarrow x^3 = 64 \Rightarrow x = 4\), \(h = 2\). Base 4 cm × 4 cm, height 2 cm (\(S = 48\) cm²).

A gutter is made from a 30 cm wide sheet by folding up 10 cm on each side at angle \(\theta\) to the horizontal (so the cross-section is a trapezium with base 10 cm and slanted sides 10 cm). Show that the cross-sectional area is \(A = 100\sin\theta(1 + \cos\theta)\) and find the maximum area.

Height \(= 10\sin\theta\); the top width \(= 10 + 2 \times 10\cos\theta\). Area \(= \frac12(10 + 10 + 20\cos\theta)(10\sin\theta) = 100\sin\theta(1 + \cos\theta)\). [1]

\(A' = 100(\cos\theta + \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta) = 100(2\cos^2\theta + \cos\theta - 1) = 100(2\cos\theta - 1)(\cos\theta + 1)\). [1]

\(\cos\theta = \frac12 \Rightarrow \theta = \frac{\pi}{3}\) (for \(0 < \theta < \pi\)). [1]

\(A_{\max} = 100\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot\frac32 = 75\sqrt3 \approx 129.9\text{ cm}^2\). [1]

In the swimmer example above, suppose she can only run at 2.5 km/h (soft sand). Where should she land now, and what is the minimum time? Does slowing her running speed move the landing point closer to or further from the café?

\(T(x) = \frac{\sqrt{9 + x^2}}{2} + \frac{8 - x}{2.5}\), \(T'(x) = \frac{x}{2\sqrt{9 + x^2}} - 0.4\). [1]

\(T' = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0.8\sqrt{9 + x^2} \Rightarrow 0.36x^2 = 5.76 \Rightarrow x = 4\). \(T(4) = 2.5 + 1.6 = 4.1\) h; endpoints \(T(0) = 4.7\), \(T(8) \approx 4.27\). [1]

Land 4 km from \(P\); minimum time 4.1 h. The landing point moves closer to the café (from 1.31 km to 4 km along), because running is now relatively less attractive than swimming. [1]

Summary

  • One-variable function + domain → derivative = 0 → check nature → check endpoints.
  • Distance problems: minimise the distance squared.
  • Give the quantity asked for, with units, to the precision asked for.
