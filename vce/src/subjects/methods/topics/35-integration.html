The fundamental theorem

Fundamental theorem of calculus
\[ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a) \quad\text{where } F' = f \qquad\qquad \frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt = f(x) \]

The definite integral gives the signed area between the graph and the \(x\)-axis: area above counts positive, area below counts negative.

Properties
\[ \int_a^a f = 0 \qquad \int_b^a f = -\int_a^b f \qquad \int_a^b f + \int_b^c f = \int_a^c f \qquad \int_a^b (kf + g) = k\!\int_a^b f + \int_a^b g \]

Constants integrate to (constant) × (interval width): \(\int_a^b k\,dx = k(b - a)\).

Given \(\int_1^4 f(x)\,dx = 5\), find \(\displaystyle\int_1^4\big(2f(x) + 1\big)\,dx\) and \(\displaystyle\int_4^1 f(x)\,dx\).

Definite integrals are linear: constants factor out and sums split. A constant \(k\) integrated over \([a, b]\) gives \(k(b - a)\), not \(k\). Swapping the limits changes the sign.

\(2\int_1^4 f + \int_1^4 1\,dx = 2(5) + (4 - 1) = 13\).

13 and \(-5\). The common error is \(2(5) + 1 = 11\): the 1 must be integrated over an interval of width 3.

Signed area vs actual area

For \(f(x) = x^2 - 1\) on \([0, 2]\), find (a) \(\int_0^2 f(x)\,dx\) and (b) the total area between the graph and the \(x\)-axis.

The definite integral gives signed area: area below the axis counts as negative. For total area, split at each \(x\)-intercept and take the absolute value of each piece.

(a) \(\left[\frac{x^3}{3} - x\right]_0^2 = \frac83 - 2 = \frac23\).

(b) The graph crosses at \(x = 1\). Area \(= -\int_0^1(x^2 - 1)\,dx + \int_1^2(x^2 - 1)\,dx = \frac23 + \frac43\).

(a) \(\frac23\) (signed); (b) 2 square units. Always sketch first and split at the \(x\)-intercepts.

Area between curves

Area between curves
\[ A = \int_a^b\big(\text{upper} - \text{lower}\big)\,dx \]

Find the intersection points first. If the curves swap over, split the integral where they cross. Upper − lower works whether the region is above or below the axis.

Find the area of the region enclosed by \(y = x\) and \(y = x^2\).

The area between two curves is the integral of (upper minus lower) between their intersection points. Find the intersections first, then check which curve is on top.

Intersections: \(x = x^2 \Rightarrow x = 0, 1\). On \((0, 1)\), \(x > x^2\).

\(A = \int_0^1(x - x^2)\,dx = \frac12 - \frac13 = \boldsymbol{\frac16}\) square units.

Find the area enclosed between \(y = \sin x\) and \(y = \cos x\) from \(x = \frac{\pi}{4}\) to \(x = \frac{5\pi}{4}\).

Same area-between-curves method. Here the limits are the intersection points, and the exact values of \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) at \(\frac\pi4\) and \(\frac{5\pi}4\) do the rest.

They meet at \(\frac{\pi}{4}\) and \(\frac{5\pi}{4}\) (where \(\tan x = 1\)); in between, \(\sin x \ge \cos x\).

\(\int_{\pi/4}^{5\pi/4}(\sin x - \cos x)\,dx = \big[-\cos x - \sin x\big]_{\pi/4}^{5\pi/4} = \left(\frac{\sqrt2}{2} + \frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right) - \left(-\frac{\sqrt2}{2} - \frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)\).

\(2\sqrt2\) square units.

Find the total area enclosed between \(y = x^3 - x\) and \(y = 3x\).

When curves cross more than once, the upper curve switches. Split the integral at each intersection, or the positive and negative parts cancel. Symmetry can halve the work.

Intersections: \(x^3 - x = 3x \Rightarrow x(x^2 - 4) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -2, 0, 2\).

On \((0, 2)\), \(3x\) is on top; on \((-2, 0)\), the cubic is on top. By symmetry (both are odd functions), the two regions are equal.

\(A = 2\int_0^2\big(3x - (x^3 - x)\big)\,dx = 2\left[2x^2 - \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^2 = 2(8 - 4) = \boldsymbol{8}\). \(\int_{-2}^2(3x - x^3 + x)\,dx = 0\): don't integrate straight across a crossing.

Average value

Average value of \(f\) on \([a, b]\)
\[ f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \]

The height of the rectangle on \([a, b]\) with the same (signed) area as the region under \(f\).

Find the average value of \(f(x) = e^x\) on \([0, 2]\).

The average value of a function is the constant height that gives the same area over the interval: total area divided by the width.

\(\frac{1}{2}\int_0^2 e^x\,dx = \frac12\big[e^x\big]_0^2\).

\(\dfrac{e^2 - 1}{2} \approx 3.19\). This is not the same as the average of \(f(0)\) and \(f(2)\) (≈ 4.19).

From rates to totals

If \(R(t)\) is a rate (litres/min, cars/hour, m/s), then \(\int_a^b R(t)\,dt\) is the net change in the quantity from \(t = a\) to \(t = b\).

  • “Area” is never negative. Split at the \(x\)-intercepts and add magnitudes.
  • Constant terms integrate to (constant) × width, not the constant itself.
  • Upper − lower, not “curve − axis”, for regions between curves.
  • Average value divides by \(b - a\), not by 2.
  • Exact vs approximate: an Exam 1 area like \(\frac{e^2 - 1}{2}\) must stay exact.

Practice

\(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos(2x)\,dx\) equals

  1. 1
  2. \(\frac12\)
  3. 0
  4. \(-1\)

\(\left[\frac12\sin(2x)\right]_0^{\pi/2} = \frac12(\sin\pi - \sin 0) = 0\). (The areas above and below cancel.)

If \(\int_0^3 g(x)\,dx = 4\), then \(\int_0^3\big(g(x) - 2\big)\,dx\) equals

  1. \(-2\)
  2. 2
  3. \(-6\)
  4. 4

\(4 - 2 \times 3 = -2\). Option B forgets to multiply by the width.

\(\displaystyle\int_1^e\frac{2}{x}\,dx\) equals

  1. \(2e - 2\)
  2. 1
  3. \(e\)
  4. 2

\(2[\log_e x]_1^e = 2(1 - 0) = 2\).

The average value of \(f(x) = x^2\) on \([0, 3]\) is

  1. 9
  2. 3
  3. 4.5
  4. 1

\(\frac13\int_0^3 x^2\,dx = \frac13\cdot 9 = 3\).

The positive value of \(k\) for which \(\int_0^k(2x - 1)\,dx = 6\) is

  1. 2
  2. 6
  3. 3
  4. \(\sqrt6\)

\(k^2 - k = 6 \Rightarrow (k - 3)(k + 2) = 0 \Rightarrow k = 3\).

The area between \(y = x^3\) and the \(x\)-axis from \(x = -1\) to \(x = 1\) is

  1. \(\frac12\)
  2. 0
  3. \(\frac14\)
  4. 1

By symmetry each piece has area \(\int_0^1 x^3\,dx = \frac14\); total \(\frac12\). The integral from \(-1\) to 1 is 0, but that's not the area.

If \(F(x) = \displaystyle\int_1^x\log_e(t^2 + 1)\,dt\), then \(F'(2)\) equals

  1. \(\log_e 2\)
  2. \(\log_e 5\)
  3. \(\frac45\)
  4. \(\log_e 5 - \log_e 2\)

Fundamental theorem: \(F'(x) = \log_e(x^2 + 1)\), so \(F'(2) = \log_e 5\). No integration needed.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\big(e^{2x} + 3\big)\,dx\).

\(\left[\frac12e^{2x} + 3x\right]_0^1 = \frac12e^2 + 3 - \frac12 = \frac{e^2 + 5}{2}\).

Find the area enclosed between \(y = x^2 - 2x\) and \(y = x\).

Intersections: \(x^2 - 2x = x \Rightarrow x(x - 3) = 0\), so \(x = 0, 3\). The line is on top. [1]

\(A = \int_0^3\big(x - x^2 + 2x\big)\,dx = \int_0^3(3x - x^2)\,dx = \left[\frac{3x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^3\). [1]

\(= \frac{27}{2} - 9 = \frac92\) square units. [1]

Water flows out of a tank at \(R(t) = 5 + 4\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{12}\right)\) litres per hour, \(0 \le t \le 24\). (a) Find the total volume that flows out in 24 hours. (b) Find the average flow rate over the first 6 hours, exactly.

(a) \(\int_0^{24}R\,dt = \left[5t - \frac{48}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi t}{12}\right]_0^{24} = 120 - \frac{48}{\pi}(1 - 1) = 120\) L. [2]

(b) \(\frac16\int_0^6 R\,dt = \frac16\left(30 - \frac{48}{\pi}(0 - 1)\right) = \frac16\left(30 + \frac{48}{\pi}\right) = 5 + \frac{8}{\pi}\) L/h. [2]

A student finds the area between \(y = \sin x\) and the \(x\)-axis for \(0 \le x \le 2\pi\) by evaluating \(\int_0^{2\pi}\sin x\,dx\). What do they get, and what is the correct area?

They get \([-\cos x]_0^{2\pi} = -1 + 1 = 0\). The correct area is \(2\int_0^{\pi}\sin x\,dx = 2 \times 2 = 4\).

Summary

  • \(\int_a^b f = F(b) - F(a)\) gives signed area; split at the \(x\)-intercepts for actual area.
  • Between curves: \(\int(\text{upper} - \text{lower})\), split where they cross.
  • Average value: \(\frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f\).
  • \(\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt = f(x)\).
