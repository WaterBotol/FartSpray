Find the value of \(k > 0\) for which the graphs of \(y = k\ln(x + 1)\) and \(y = x\) have exactly one point of intersection.

Assessor’s report

General comments

This paper was pitched above a typical Exam 2. CAS could do most of the arithmetic, but each question needed an insight CAS can’t supply: that intersections of an increasing function and its inverse lie on \(y = x\), that \(x = \pi\) is a hidden stationary point, that a single intersection means tangency, that the two stationary points of \(A(a)\) must be checked against \(a > -1\). A median of about 45/80 is expected. 60+ is outstanding.

What separated the top scripts

  • Algebra before CAS. Factorising \(f'(x)\) in 2b revealed all three stationary points. CAS’s solve command (depending on settings) missed one.
  • Precision. Storing intersection values instead of retyping rounded ones kept the area in 1c accurate.
  • Reasoning with limiting cases. In 1d, the strongest answers explained why \(k > 1\) and \(k < 1\) both give two intersections.
  • Exact vs approximate. The top students gave exact answers wherever asked (\(\frac2e\), \(1 - \frac{5}{2e}\), \(\frac{2(e^2 + 1)}{e^2 - 1}\)).

Common errors

  • \(\sqrt{(x + 1)^2}\) simplified to \(x + 1\) (MC 8).
  • Reading transformations from \(f(3 - x)\) in the wrong order (MC 3).
  • Using a normal approximation when the exact binomial was asked for (3h), or vice versa (MC 19).
  • Counting \(k = 0\) as giving two solutions in 2f.
  • Tracing the pseudocode loop only once.

Advice

Below 40: redo the medium paper and practise writing algebraic setups before using CAS. Above 55: your technique is exam-ready. Keep your timing sharp with full papers under exam conditions.
