The normal curve

\(X \sim \text{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) has a symmetric bell-shaped pdf centred at \(\mu\) (mean = median = mode), with spread set by the standard deviation \(\sigma\). Note that the second parameter is the variance: \(\text{N}(170, 64)\) means \(\sigma = 8\).

Standardising
\[ Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma} \sim \text{N}(0, 1) \]

A \(z\)-score counts how many standard deviations a value is from the mean.

Heights of adult men are \(X \sim \text{N}(170, 8^2)\) cm. Find (a) \(P(X > 180)\), (b) the height exceeded by only 10% of men.

(a) \(z = \frac{180 - 170}{8} = 1.25\); \(P(Z > 1.25) \approx 0.1056\).

(b) Need \(P(X > h) = 0.1\), i.e. \(P(X < h) = 0.9\). Inverse normal: \(z = 1.2816\), so \(h = 170 + 1.2816 \times 8\).

(a) 0.1056, (b) \(h \approx 180.25\) cm. CAS inverse normal uses the area to the left by default: convert “top 10%” into 0.9.

\(X \sim \text{N}(50, 16)\). Without technology, estimate \(P(42 < X < 54)\).

\(\sigma = 4\). \(42 = \mu - 2\sigma\), \(54 = \mu + \sigma\). By symmetry, \(P(42 < X < 50) \approx \frac{95\%}{2} = 47.5\%\) and \(P(50 < X < 54) \approx \frac{68\%}{2} = 34\%\).

\(\approx 0.815\) (81.5%). Draw the bell and split at the mean.

The mass of a packet is normally distributed with mean 50 g, and 10% of packets weigh less than 40 g. Find the standard deviation.

\(P(X < 40) = 0.1 \Rightarrow \frac{40 - 50}{\sigma} = -1.2816\).

\(\sigma = \frac{10}{1.2816} \approx 7.80\) g.

For a normal variable, \(P(X < 30) = 0.2\) and \(P(X > 60) = 0.1\). Find \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\).

\(\frac{30 - \mu}{\sigma} = z_{0.2} = -0.8416\) and \(\frac{60 - \mu}{\sigma} = z_{0.9} = 1.2816\).

So \(30 = \mu - 0.8416\sigma\) and \(60 = \mu + 1.2816\sigma\). Subtract: \(30 = 2.1232\sigma\).

\(\sigma \approx 14.13\), \(\mu \approx 30 + 0.8416 \times 14.13 \approx 41.89\).

Using \(X \sim \text{N}(170, 8^2)\): (a) find \(P(X > 180 \mid X > 170)\); (b) ten men are chosen at random. Find the probability that at least two are taller than 180 cm.

(a) \(\frac{P(X > 180)}{P(X > 170)} = \frac{0.1056}{0.5} \approx 0.2113\).

(b) \(Y \sim \text{Bi}(10, 0.1056)\): \(P(Y \ge 2) = 1 - P(Y = 0) - P(Y = 1)\).

(a) 0.2113; (b) \(\approx 0.2858\). Use the unrounded probability from the normal part in the binomial part.

  • \(\text{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\): the second number is the variance. \(\text{N}(10, 9)\) has \(\sigma = 3\).
  • Inverse normal = area to the left. “Top 5%” means use 0.95.
  • Symmetry shortcuts: \(P(Z < -a) = P(Z > a)\), and \(P(X > \mu) = 0.5\).
  • The 68–95–99.7 rule is approximate, so use it only when told to (or tech-free).

Practice

\(Z \sim \text{N}(0, 1)\) and \(P(Z < a) = 0.3\). Then \(P(Z > -a)\) is

  1. 0.7
  2. 0.3
  3. 0.4
  4. 0.6

By symmetry, \(P(Z > -a) = P(Z < a) = 0.3\).

\(X \sim \text{N}(20, 9)\). \(P(X > 26)\) is approximately

  1. 0.025
  2. 0.16
  3. 0.0015
  4. 0.05

\(\sigma = 3\), so 26 is \(\mu + 2\sigma\). The tail beyond \(2\sigma\) is \(\frac{1 - 0.95}{2} = 0.025\). Using \(\sigma = 9\) gives \(z = 0.67\) and a very different answer.

The scores on a test are normal with mean 65 and standard deviation 10. A student's score has a \(z\)-score of \(-1.5\). Their score is

  1. 80
  2. 63.5
  3. 50
  4. 55

\(x = 65 - 1.5 \times 10 = 50\).

For any normal distribution, \(P(\mu - \sigma < X < \mu + 2\sigma)\) is approximately

  1. 0.95
  2. 0.68
  3. 0.975
  4. 0.815

\(0.34 + 0.475 = 0.815\).

\(X\) is normal with \(\sigma = 5\) and \(P(X > 62) = 0.2\). The mean is closest to

  1. 66.2
  2. 57.8
  3. 61.2
  4. 53.6

\(P(X < 62) = 0.8 \Rightarrow z = 0.8416\). \(\mu = 62 - 0.8416 \times 5 \approx 57.8\).

Which of the following is not true for \(X \sim \text{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\)?

  1. \(P(X = \mu) = 0\)
  2. mean = median
  3. \(P(X > \mu + \sigma) = 0.32\)
  4. \(P(X < \mu) = 0.5\)

About 32% lies outside \(\mu \pm \sigma\), split between both tails, so \(P(X > \mu + \sigma) \approx 0.16\).

Battery life is normal with mean 400 hours and standard deviation 25 hours. Find the proportion of batteries lasting between 350 and 420 hours.

\(z\)-scores \(-2\) and \(0.8\). \(P(-2 < Z < 0.8) \approx 0.7881 - 0.0228 = 0.7654\).

The time to complete a puzzle is normal with mean 12 minutes. 5% of people take more than 18 minutes. Find the standard deviation and the proportion taking less than 10 minutes.

\(\frac{18 - 12}{\sigma} = 1.6449 \Rightarrow \sigma \approx 3.648\) min. [2]

\(P(X < 10) = P\left(Z < \frac{-2}{3.648}\right) = P(Z < -0.548) \approx 0.2917\). [1]

Apples have masses \(M \sim \text{N}(150, 20^2)\) g. Apples under 120 g are rejected. (a) Find the proportion rejected. (b) Is the mean mass of the accepted apples greater or less than 150 g? Give a reason. (c) A box holds 12 apples chosen at random from the accepted apples. Find the probability that at least 11 weigh more than 140 g.

(a) \(P(M < 120) = P(Z < -1.5) \approx 0.0668\). [1]

(b) Greater: the lightest apples have been removed from the left tail. [1]

(c) \(P(M > 140 \mid M \ge 120) = \frac{P(M > 140)}{P(M \ge 120)} = \frac{0.6915}{0.9332} \approx 0.7410\). [1] Then \(Y \sim \text{Bi}(12, 0.7410)\): \(P(Y \ge 11) \approx 0.1423\). [1]

A student uses invNorm(0.05, 60, 4) to find “the value exceeded by 5% of the population”. What's wrong?

invNorm uses the area to the left, so 0.05 gives the bottom 5% (≈ 53.4). The value exceeded by 5% needs invNorm(0.95, 60, 4) ≈ 66.6.

Summary

  • \(Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}\); remember \(\text{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) uses the variance.
  • 68–95–99.7% for quick, tech-free estimates.
  • Unknown \(\mu\) or \(\sigma\): turn each probability into a \(z\)-equation; two unknowns need two equations.
  • Conditional: divide probabilities. Then feed an unrounded \(p\) into a binomial if needed.
