What formal language does

FunctionHowExample
Establish authority and credibilityTechnical lexis, impersonal voice, standard grammar, institutional conventionsA medical report; legislation; university policies
Clarify and create precisionDefined terms, numbered clauses, explicit cohesion, unambiguous syntaxContracts, instructions, terms and conditions
PersuadeRhetorical devices in planned speech: repetition, tricolon, parallelism, rhetorical questions, high modality (must, will), alliteration, inclusive pronouns, emotive lexisPolitical speeches
Promote social harmony and show respectPoliteness formulas, titles, ceremonial language, euphemism for sensitive topicsCondolence messages; Acknowledgement of Country; the National Apology
Manipulate or obfuscateDoublespeak, euphemism, nominalisation, agentless passive, jargon that hides meaning“Online Compliance Intervention” (Robodebt); “on-water matters”

Case study: the National Apology (2008)

On 13 February 2008, Prime Minister Kevin Rudd delivered the Apology to Australia's Indigenous Peoples, particularly the Stolen Generations, in Parliament. Its formal features served respect, reconciliation and persuasion:

  • Performative speech act: the formal verb “apologise” and the plain “we say sorry” perform the apology.
  • Anaphora and tricolon: “For the pain, suffering and hurt of these Stolen Generations…”, repeated structures that build emotional and rhetorical weight.
  • Inclusive first-person plural (“we”, “our fellow Australians”): positions Parliament as speaking for the nation, promoting social harmony.
  • Formal, Latinate lexis with emotive nouns (“laws and policies of successive Parliaments… inflicted profound grief, suffering and loss”): combines institutional authority with an admission of responsibility (active voice: Parliaments inflicted).

Case study: obfuscation

  • Robodebt: the automated welfare debt-recovery scheme (2016–2019) was officially the “Online Compliance Intervention”, a string of neutral, technical-sounding nouns that obscured its effect. The 2023 Royal Commission found it “a crude and cruel mechanism, neither fair nor legal”. The contrast between bureaucratic label and plain-English verdict is a powerful essay example.
  • “On-water matters”: the formula used in 2013–14 by the then Immigration Minister to decline to discuss asylum-seeker boat operations. Vague nominal phrases deflect scrutiny.
  • Corporate euphemism: rightsizing, restructure, let go for job losses; negative growth for decline.

Euphemism softens something unpleasant, often kindly (“passed away”). Doublespeak deliberately disguises or distorts meaning to avoid responsibility or manipulate (“collateral damage” for civilian deaths). Intent and context decide which label fits.

  • Formal language can be exclusionary: legal jargon can shut out non-experts. Don't present formality as purely positive.
  • Quote accurately and briefly in essays. Paraphrase rather than misquote.
  • Link features to function: “agentless passive → avoids accountability”, not just “passive voice is formal”.

Explain how formal language can both clarify and obscure meaning, with an example of each.

Clarify: precise terminology and numbered clauses in instructions or legislation reduce ambiguity (e.g. defined terms in a lease). Obscure: euphemism, nominalisation and passive voice can hide who acted or what happened (e.g. “Online Compliance Intervention”; “mistakes were made”). The same features can do either, depending on intent and audience.

“Formal language is used to exercise power.” Outline two points and examples for an essay on this topic.

Authority and exclusion: legal and bureaucratic jargon positions institutions as experts and can make it hard for ordinary people to challenge them (the Robodebt letters' technical language; the Royal Commission's plain condemnation). [2]

Counterpoint, power used for reconciliation: the 2008 Apology used formal parliamentary language to acknowledge wrongdoing and promote social harmony, showing formal language can also redistribute respect. [2]

Summary

  • Formal language establishes authority, clarifies, persuades and shows respect.
  • It can also manipulate: euphemism, doublespeak, nominalisation, agentless passive.
  • Australian examples: the National Apology (2008), Robodebt (Royal Commission 2023), “on-water matters”.
