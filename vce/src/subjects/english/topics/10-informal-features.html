Context first

Language is informal when the situational context allows it: familiar participants (close tenor), everyday topics, relaxed settings and spontaneous or quick modes (conversation, texting, social media). The cultural context (shared values, attitudes and knowledge) shapes what counts as appropriate, e.g. Australians' tolerance of informality, mateship and mild swearing in many settings.

Informal language is rule-governed and purposeful. It's economical, builds rapport and signals identity. In analysis, avoid judging it as “lazy” or “incorrect”. Describe what it achieves.

Summary

  • Informality is licensed by context: close tenor, everyday field, spontaneous mode.
  • Features appear in every subsystem: reductions, colloquial lexis, ellipsis, particles and hedges.
  • Always explain function: economy, rapport, identity, face.
