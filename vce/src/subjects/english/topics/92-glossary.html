Definitions you can use in exam answers. Click the link after each for the full explanation and examples.

A–C

Accent
Pronunciation features only (vs dialect, which also includes lexis and grammar). Australian English
Adjacency pair
Two linked turns such as question–answer or offer–refusal. Spoken discourse
Agentless passive
A passive clause with no “by…” agent, hiding or backgrounding who acted. Formal features
Amelioration / pejoration
A word's meaning becomes more positive / more negative over time. Metalanguage
Anaphoric / cataphoric / exophoric reference
Refers back / forward / to something outside the text. Cohesion
Anaphora
Rhetorical repetition at the start of successive clauses (not the same as anaphoric reference). Formal functions
Assimilation
A sound becomes more like a neighbouring sound (handbag /hæmbæg/). Metalanguage
Backchannel / minimal response
Short signals (mm, yeah) showing attention while another speaks. Spoken discourse
Blend
A word formed from parts of two words (strollout). Metalanguage
Clipping
Shortening a word (uni, sus). Metalanguage
Code-switching / code-mixing
Moving between languages or varieties / inserting words from one into another. Ethnolects
Coherence
The overall sense and logical flow of a text. Cohesion
Cohesion
Linguistic devices that link parts of a text (reference, connectives, lexical cohesion). Cohesion
Communication Accommodation Theory
Giles's theory that speakers converge towards or diverge from others' speech. Identity
Complaint tradition
The long-standing habit of lamenting that language is declining. Standard English
Conversion
Changing a word's class without changing its form (to google). Metalanguage
Covert / overt prestige
Status within a group, often for non-standard forms / status from society and institutions. Standard English
Cultural cringe
The belief that local culture (and Australian English) is inferior to British. Australian English

D–H

Descriptivism / prescriptivism
Describing how language is used / prescribing how it should be used. Standard English
Dialect
A variety with distinctive lexis, grammar and pronunciation linked to region or social group. Australian English
Discourse particle / marker
Words like like, well, anyway that manage talk rather than add content. Informal features
Doublespeak
Language that deliberately disguises or distorts meaning. Formal functions
Dysphemism
A harsh or offensive term for something (carked it). Power & inclusion
Elision
Omitting sounds in speech (fish 'n' chips). Metalanguage
Ellipsis
Omitting words that can be recovered from context. Cohesion
Ethnolect
A variety associated with an ethnic or cultural group. Ethnolects
Euphemism
A softer term that avoids offence or unpleasantness (passed away). Power & inclusion
Euphemism treadmill
Euphemisms taking on negative connotations and being replaced. Power & inclusion
Face (positive / negative)
The wish to be liked and approved / the wish not to be imposed upon. Informal functions
Field, tenor, mode
Subject matter, relationship between participants, and channel of communication. Register
Gratuitous concurrence
Agreeing with questions regardless of truth, to avoid conflict; documented in Aboriginal English speakers in legal settings. Aboriginal English
Hedge
A word or phrase that softens or qualifies (kind of, maybe). Informal features
High rising terminal (HRT)
Rising intonation at the end of a statement. Metalanguage
Hypercorrection
Overapplying a prestige rule (between you and I). Standard English
Hypocorism
An Australian-style diminutive, often with -ie or -o (arvo, servo). Australian English

I–O

Idiolect / sociolect
An individual's unique way of speaking / a variety associated with a social group. Identity
In-group / out-group
Those who share a group's language and belong / those who don't. Identity
Inflectional / derivational morpheme
Marks grammar without changing word class (-s, -ed) / creates new words or changes class (-ness, un-). Metalanguage
Jargon
Specialist vocabulary of a field or occupation. Formal features
Latching
A turn that follows the previous one with no gap (=). Spoken discourse
Lexical density
The proportion of content words; higher in formal written texts. Formal features
Multiethnolect
A variety shared by young people of many backgrounds in multicultural areas. Ethnolects
Nominalisation
Turning a verb or adjective into a noun (decide → decision). Formal features
Non-rhotic
Not pronouncing /r/ after a vowel unless a vowel follows; Australian English is non-rhotic. Metalanguage
Orthophemism
A neutral, direct term (died). Power & inclusion
Overlap
Two speakers talking at once; cooperative or competitive. Spoken discourse

P–Z

Performative speech act
An utterance that performs an action by being said (I declare this library open). Formal functions
Prosody
Stress, intonation, pitch, tempo, volume and pauses. Metalanguage
Register
A variety of language suited to a context, shaped by field, tenor and mode. Register
Repair
Fixing a problem in talk, by the speaker (self-repair) or another person. Spoken discourse
Semantic shift
A change in a word's meaning (broadening, narrowing, amelioration, pejoration). Youth language
Speech act (direct / indirect)
What an utterance does; indirect acts use one form for another function (a question as a request). Metalanguage
Standard Australian English
The codified variety used in formal contexts, defined by grammar, lexis and spelling rather than accent. Standard English
Style shifting
Moving along the formality scale within one variety. Identity
Taboo language
Words avoided in some contexts because they relate to sensitive topics. Power & inclusion
Topic management
How speakers introduce, shift and change topics. Spoken discourse
Tricolon
A rhetorical list of three parallel items. Formal functions
Turn-taking
How speakers get, hold and give up the floor. Spoken discourse
