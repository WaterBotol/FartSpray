Section A of the exam is a set of short-answer questions on one text (15 marks). Allow about 25 minutes. Every answer needs the term, the quote with line number, and (for “explain”) the function in context. Attempt the whole task before opening the answers.

Text 1: share-house conversation

Three university students who share a house in Melbourne talk in their kitchen on a Sunday evening. The conversation was recorded with their permission. Transcript key: (.) micro pause; (1.0) timed pause in seconds; [ ] overlapping speech; = latching; ↑ rising intonation; CAPS emphatic stress; (laughs) non-verbal.

  1. ZOE: okay so (.) the power bill came in↑
  2. AMIR: oh no (.) how bad
  3. ZOE: four hundred and twenty (.) for three months=
  4. TOM: =FOUR hundred↑ we were barely even [home]

  6. TOM: (laughs) that's (.) okay that's fair
  7. ZOE: so it's (1.0) um (.) a hundred and forty each (.) can youse send it by Friday↑

Questions (15 marks)

1. Identify two features of spontaneous spoken discourse in lines 3–5. Give line references.

Any two: latching between Zoe and Tom (“months=” / “=FOUR”, lines 3–4); overlapping speech (“[home]” / “[bro]”, lines 4–5); emphatic stress (“FOUR”, line 4); filler/discourse particle like (line 5); micro pauses (line 3, line 5). [1 each]

2. Identify the word-formation process in arvo (line 9) and in PayID as it is used in line 8.

arvo: hypocorism (clipping of afternoon with the -o suffix). [1] PayID: conversion (functional shift) of a noun/brand name into a verb. [1]

3. Explain the function of youse in line 7.

Youse is a non-standard plural second-person pronoun, so Zoe clearly addresses both housemates at once. [1] As an Australian colloquialism it keeps the request casual and friendly, supporting the close, equal tenor. [1]

4. Explain the meaning and function of “yeah nah” in line 10.

Yeah acknowledges Tom's request and nah dismisses any problem, so the phrase means “no problem, that's fine”. [1] It's a distinctively Australian discourse marker that softens the reply and maintains rapport, preserving Tom's face after asking for extra time. [1]

5. Analyse how Amir manages a potentially face-threatening criticism of Tom in lines 5 and 11.

In line 5 Amir blames Tom's heater use, but the address term bro signals solidarity and frames the criticism as friendly teasing. [1] In line 11 the hedges maybe and like, micro pauses and rising intonation on “a jumper↑” turn a direct instruction into a jokey suggestion, reducing the imposition on Tom's negative face. [1] Tom's laughter and concession (“okay that's fair”, “alright alright”) and Zoe's laughter show the teasing is accepted, so the humour maintains positive face and the housemates' rapport. [1]

6. Discuss topic management in lines 12–15.

Tom uses the discourse marker anyway (line 14) to shift the topic away from the teasing about his heater use, regaining some control of the conversation. [1] Amir's self-repair “don't (.) don't even start” (line 15) accepts the new topic playfully while showing reluctance (implying his team also lost or that the topic is sore), keeping the tone light. [1]

7. Identify two lexical features that reflect the close tenor between the speakers and explain one.

Any two: colloquial bro (line 5); hypocorism arvo (line 9); non-standard youse (line 7); slang cooked with semantic shift to “thrashed” (line 14); brand-name verb PayID (line 8). [1] Explanation: e.g. cooked draws on shared youth slang and footy talk, marking the speakers as an in-group of young friends. [1]

Metalanguage speed round

1. How many morphemes are in unbelievably?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 5

un + believ(e) + -abl(e) + -y (-ly): four.

2. Which suffix is inflectional?

  1. -s in runs
  2. -ness in kindness
  3. -er in teacher
  4. -ify in simplify

Inflectional morphemes mark grammar (tense, number, person) without changing word class. The others are derivational.

3. “Fish 'n' chips” shows

  1. assimilation
  2. elision
  3. insertion
  4. vowel lengthening

Sounds in and are omitted.

4. Handbag pronounced /hæmbæg/ shows

  1. elision only
  2. vowel reduction
  3. insertion
  4. elision of /d/ and assimilation of /n/ to /m/ before /b/

/d/ drops out, then /n/ takes on the bilabial place of the following /b/.

5. In “She ran fast”, fast is

  1. an adjective
  2. a noun
  3. an adverb
  4. a verb

It modifies the verb ran. In “a fast car” it would be an adjective.

6. “Although the bill was high, Zoe paid it and the others sent their share.” This sentence is

  1. compound-complex
  2. complex
  3. compound
  4. simple

Two independent clauses joined by and, plus a subordinate clause (Although…).

7. “Could you close the window?” said by someone who is cold is best described as

  1. a direct speech act
  2. an indirect speech act: an interrogative functioning as a request
  3. an exclamative
  4. an imperative

The form is a question; the function is a request, which softens the imposition.

8. Car, bus and tram are ______ of vehicle.

  1. synonyms
  2. antonyms
  3. hypernyms
  4. hyponyms

Vehicle is the hypernym (superordinate); the specific terms are hyponyms.

9. “The window was broken.” Which is true?

  1. It is active voice.
  2. It must be an agentless passive, because it is formal.
  3. It is ambiguous: it could be a passive (an event) or a description of a state with an adjective.
  4. It contains nominalisation.

Great trap: “was broken” can describe the result (state) or the action. Check context before claiming “agentless passive hides responsibility”.

10. In “Running late, Tom grabbed his keys”, Running late is

  1. a non-finite subordinate clause
  2. an independent clause
  3. a noun phrase
  4. a compound verb

The -ing verb has no tense or subject of its own, so it's non-finite and depends on the main clause.

11. Which word underwent amelioration?

  1. silly (once meaning “blessed”)
  2. nice (once meaning “foolish”)
  3. villain (once meaning “farm worker”)
  4. hussy (once meaning “housewife”)

Amelioration: a word's meaning becomes more positive. The others show pejoration.

12. “Nice weather for ducks,” says someone as it pours. The main semantic and pragmatic feature is

  1. hyperbole
  2. euphemism
  3. an adjacency pair
  4. irony that relies on shared context (flouting a conversational maxim)

What's said differs from what's meant; listeners infer the meaning from the situation.

13. Which is a free morpheme?

  1. -ing
  2. pre-
  3. book
  4. -ed

Free morphemes can stand alone as words.

14. “Me and Jas are gonna head off” contains all of the following except

  1. nominalisation
  2. a non-standard pronoun form in a coordinated subject
  3. a contraction (gonna)
  4. a phrasal verb (head off)

No verb or adjective has been turned into a noun.

Summary

  • Term + quote + line number every time; add function for “explain” and “analyse”.
  • Match the marks: 2 marks means two distinct points.
  • Watch the traps: state vs passive, derivational vs inflectional, form vs function in speech acts.
