Worked analysis

A manager (KATE) and a new employee (LIAM) in a staff kitchen.

  1. KATE: Liam (.) got a sec↑
  2. LIAM: yep (.) yeah course
  3. KATE: so the report (.) um (.) it's great (.) just a couple of tiny things=
  4. LIAM: =oh no what'd I [do]
  5. KATE: [nothing] bad (laughs) just the (.) the tables need (.) sources
  6. LIAM: right (.) mm (.) I'll fix that this arvo
  7. KATE: perfect (.) anyway how's your first week been↑

How do Kate and Liam manage the face-threatening act of criticism?

Kate opens with an informal pre-sequence (“got a sec↑”, line 1) with ellipsis and HRT, lowering the status gap and seeking permission (negative politeness).

She cushions the criticism with praise (“it's great”), minimisers (“just a couple of tiny things”, line 3), pauses and fillers (“um”, line 3) that signal reluctance, and a false start/repetition (“the (.) the tables”, line 5).

Liam's latched, anxious response (“=oh no what'd I do”, line 4) is overlapped by Kate's reassuring “nothing bad” and laughter (line 5): cooperative overlap to protect his positive face.

Kate controls the topic (she opens and closes it, shifting to “how's your first week”, line 7) but uses informality, hedging and humour to maintain rapport; Liam's minimal responses (“right (.) mm”) and promise (“this arvo”) complete the adjacency pair cooperatively.

  • Overlap ≠ rudeness. Decide from context whether it's cooperative or competitive.
  • Fillers aren't “errors”; they hold the floor and signal planning or hesitation.
  • Topic control is evidence of power: who introduces and closes topics?
  • Use the transcript key; symbols differ between texts.

Topic control is a strong indicator of power and status.

Explain two functions of minimal responses in conversation.

They show the listener is paying attention and encourage the speaker to continue (supportive feedback), and they can signal agreement or acceptance without taking the floor (e.g. Liam's “right (.) mm”, line 6).

Analyse how turn-taking features in the transcript reflect the relationship between Kate and Liam.

Kate initiates with a summons (“Liam (.) got a sec↑”) and Liam immediately complies (“yep (.) yeah course”), reflecting her higher status. [1] Liam's latched turn (line 4) shows anxiety about his new job; Kate's overlapping “nothing bad” (line 5) reassures him, a cooperative overlap that protects his positive face. [2] Kate closes the topic (“perfect”) and shifts to his first week (line 7), showing control but also care, which builds rapport. [1]

Summary

  • Spoken interaction is organised by adjacency pairs, turn-taking and topic management.
  • Overlaps, latching, pauses and fillers reveal attitudes, power and rapport.
  • Repairs and hedges manage accuracy and face.
