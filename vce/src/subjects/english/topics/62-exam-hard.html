English Language · real exam sections, harder

Every section here is a real VCAA exam section. English papers can’t be mixed question by question, because each section is built around one text, so the sections were ranked instead: for each of Section A, B and C, the three most recent exams (2023, 2024, 2025) were ordered by the state average. This paper uses Section A from 2023, Section B from 2023 and Section C from 2023.

Be warned: English results barely move from year to year (section averages range from about 44% to 58%), so the three English papers are much closer in difficulty than the science and maths ones. The biggest difference is in Section A.

VCAA publishes Sections B and C on a 0–15 scale. The exam paper weights each at 30 marks, so the state averages are doubled here. The texts and exact questions are in the official PDFs (VCAA copyright), so open them with the links.

How the state went on these exact sections: an average of 39 / 75 (53%).

Discuss the function of two different syntactic features used between lines 43 and 70 (examples, metalanguage). (p. 3 ↗)

  • Its function explained

Analyse two features that contribute to the coherence of the text (metalanguage, line numbers). (p. 3 ↗)

  • First coherence feature identified (e.g. formatting: bold, centred headings)
  • Explains how it creates coherence (e.g. lets readers navigate to relevant sections)
  • Second feature identified (e.g. logical ordering, conventions, consistent semantic field, inference)
  • Explains how it creates coherence

“Analyse” means explaining how each feature helps the reader make sense of the text, not just labelling it. Cohesive ties count only if linked to coherence.

Assessor’s report

How Victoria went on this paper

These are the real published results for each section in the year it was set, from the VCAA examiners’ reports. A student at the state average in every section would score 39 / 75 (53%): Section A 44%, Section B 55%, Section C 55%.

Short answers: where marks were lost

  • Q2 (Discuss the function of two different syntactic features used between lines 43 and 70 (examples, metalanguage).): 38% of available marks
  • Q4 (How does the register support two social purposes of the text? (examples, line numbers, metalanguage)): 42% of available marks
  • Q3 (Analyse two features that contribute to the coherence of the text (metalanguage, line numbers).): 48% of available marks
  • Q1 (Identify one semantic field/domain of the discourse and give two supporting examples with line numbers.): 55% of available marks

What the examiners kept saying

  • Short answers: quote precisely, give line numbers, and do every part the question asks. Five-mark questions usually need several features and their links to context or identity.
  • Commentary: choose the most salient features, and link each to function, context or register. Summary and subsystem-by-subsystem listing were marked down.
  • Essay: unpack every key word of the topic in the introduction, use contemporary Australian examples, and don’t pad with misattributed quotations.
