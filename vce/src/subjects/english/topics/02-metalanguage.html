Practice

“Wanna grab lunch?” contains which two features?

  1. Blending and nominalisation
  2. Contraction (wanna) and ellipsis (Do you omitted)
  3. Clipping and passive voice
  4. Hedging and dysphemism

Wanna contracts “want to”; the auxiliary and subject are ellipted.

Strollout (a slow vaccine rollout; Macquarie and ANDC word of the year 2021) is an example of

  1. clipping
  2. an initialism
  3. conversion
  4. blending

Stroll + rollout merged.

“Mistakes were made in the processing of claims.” The main effect of the syntax is to

  1. obscure who was responsible through an agentless passive and nominalisation
  2. create solidarity
  3. add emphasis through fronting an interjection
  4. signal informality

No agent (who made the mistakes?), and processing is a nominalisation. Common in political apology-avoidance.

The pronunciation of sandwich as /sæmwɪdʒ/ shows

  1. vowel reduction only
  2. flapping
  3. elision of /d/ and assimilation of /n/ to /m/
  4. insertion

/d/ is dropped, and /n/ becomes bilabial /m/ before /w/.

“The implementation of the recommendations will be undertaken in consultation with stakeholders.” Which features create its formal, impersonal register?

  1. Colloquialisms and contractions
  2. Nominalisations (implementation, consultation), passive voice and Latinate lexis
  3. Discourse particles and ellipsis
  4. Phonological reduction

Abstract nouns replace verbs, and there's no human agent.

A friend says “I mean, it's kind of a big deal, I guess.” The phrases kind of and I guess are best described as

  1. hedges that soften the claim, protecting face
  2. dysphemisms
  3. jargon
  4. initialisms

They reduce the force of the assertion.

Identify the word-formation process in each and give its likely function in an informal conversation: (a) servo; (b) to Uber somewhere; (c) hangry.

(a) Hypocorism (clipping + -o): casual, marks Australian identity and solidarity. (b) Conversion (noun → verb): economical, contemporary. (c) Blend (hungry + angry): humorous, creative, builds rapport.

Explain two ways syntax can make a text more formal, with examples.

Nominalisation turns processes into abstract nouns (The decision to relocate was made vs We decided to move), creating a dense, impersonal, authoritative register. [2]

Complex sentences with subordinate clauses and passive constructions (Applications that are received after the closing date will not be considered) foreground rules over people and create precision and distance. [2]

Summary

  • Five subsystems: phonetics/phonology, morphology/lexicology, syntax, semantics, discourse/pragmatics.
  • Always pair the feature with its function in context.
  • Learn the HCE vowel symbols for Australian English.
