Usage manual

This guide is one web page that works like an app. Everything runs on your device: there is no login, and your progress is saved in your own browser. Two minutes reading this will save you a lot of clicking.

The study loop (use it for every topic)

STEP 1

Choose a subject and topic

Use the subject buttons at the top of the course map (the ☰ button on phones), the subject cards above, or search. Topics follow the study design order.

STEP 2

Read the explanation

Each topic lists the study design dot points it covers. Read the theory, the boxed key ideas and every orange Trick alert.

STEP 3

Attempt the worked examples

Try each one yourself first, then press Show step to reveal one step at a time and see exactly where you went wrong.

STEP 4

Do the practice questions

Multiple choice marks itself instantly. For written questions, reveal the solution and mark yourself Got it or Review later.

STEP 5

Use the interactives

Predict what will happen, then move the sliders. If the result surprises you, re-read that section.

STEP 6

Tick it off, then revise

Mark the topic complete. Before SACs and exams, clear your Review list and run the Quiz gauntlet for that subject.

What the coloured boxes mean

Key idea: the core concept you must be able to explain. (Each subject has its own accent colour.)
Trick alert: a trap that costs students marks every year, the kind examiners' reports complain about.
Exam tip: how to set out an answer so you get every mark.
Definition: a precise meaning worth learning word for word.
Going deeper: extension material that helps understanding.
Interactive: a simulation or tool. Everything updates live.

Search

Press / (or Ctrl + K, ⌘ + K on a Mac) anywhere. Search covers every subject: topic titles, section headings, worked examples, glossaries and formula sheets. Results from the subject you're in are ranked first, and each result shows which subject it belongs to. Common shorthand works: chain rule, rollercoaster, Le Chatelier, trp operon, ethnolect, confidence interval.

Saving, sharing and privacy

  • Progress is saved in this browser only. Completed topics, self-marks and quiz answers stay on this device. A private window, or a different device, starts fresh.
  • Share a topic or a section. Hover over any heading and press # to copy a link straight to it.
  • Works offline. The whole guide is a single HTML file. Save it and it opens without internet (maths still renders; only the custom fonts fall back).
  • Dark mode. The moon button switches themes; by default the guide follows your device.

How this guide was written (read this)

Each subject follows the key knowledge in its current VCAA study design: Mathematical Methods 2023–2027, Physics 2024–2027, Chemistry 2024–2027, Biology 2022–2026 and English Language 2024 onwards. The explanations, diagrams, worked examples and questions were written for this guide. They are modelled on the question types that appear again and again in VCAA exams (and on the traps examiners' reports keep mentioning), but they are not copies of VCAA, Edrolo, Jacaranda, Cambridge or tutoring-company questions. That keeps the guide free to share.

Nothing beats sitting actual VCAA exams under timed conditions and then reading the examiners' report. After you finish a subject here, work through the past papers for your current study design on the VCAA past examinations page. Older papers are still useful for topics that haven't changed.
