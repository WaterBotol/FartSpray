Usage manual

This guide is one web page that works like an app. Everything is on your device: there is no login, and your progress is saved in your own browser. The manual below takes about two minutes to read and will save you a lot of clicking later.

The study loop (use this order for every topic)

STEP 1

Pick a topic

Use the course map on the left (the ☰ button on phones) or the search bar at the top. Topics follow the study design order: Unit 3 AoS 1 → Unit 4 AoS 2.

STEP 2

Read the explanation

Each topic opens with the study design dot points it covers. Read the theory, the boxed equations and every orange Trick alert.

STEP 3

Attempt the worked examples

Cover the solution and try the question yourself first. Then press Show step to reveal one step at a time and see exactly where you went wrong.

STEP 4

Do the practice questions

Multiple choice marks itself instantly. For written questions, reveal the solution and mark yourself Got it or Review later.

STEP 5

Play with the interactives

Drag the sliders and predict what happens before you look. If the sim surprises you, re-read that section.

STEP 6

Mark it complete, then revise

Tick the topic off at the bottom of the page. Before SACs and the exam, clear your Review list and run the Quiz gauntlet.

What the coloured boxes mean

Key idea: the core concept you must be able to explain in words.
Trick alert: a trap that costs students marks every year (the kind of thing examiners' reports complain about).
Exam tip: how to set out an answer so you get every mark.
Definition: a precise meaning worth memorising word for word.
Going deeper: extension material. It helps understanding but is not a separate dot point.
Interactive: a simulation with sliders. Everything updates live.

Search

Press / (or Ctrl + K, ⌘ + K on a Mac) anywhere to jump to the search bar. Search looks through topic titles, every section heading, worked examples, the glossary and the formula sheet, then takes you straight to the matching section. It understands common shorthand: try rollercoaster, tension, banked, emf, rms, gamma, work function or apparent weight.

Saving, sharing and privacy

  • Progress is saved in this browser only. Completed topics, self-marks and quiz answers stay on this device. A private or incognito window will not remember them, and neither will a different phone or laptop.
  • Share a whole topic or one section. The address bar always points at the page you are on. Hover over any heading and press the # button to copy a link straight to that section.
  • Works offline. The whole guide is a single HTML file. Save it and it opens without the internet (maths still renders, only the custom fonts fall back to your system font).
  • Dark mode. The moon button in the top bar switches themes. By default the guide follows your device setting.

About the exam

The end-of-year written exam has 15 minutes of reading time and 2 hours 30 minutes of writing time. Section A is multiple choice. Section B is short and extended answer, including questions on practical work and data analysis. You get a formula sheet and data sheet, and you may bring an approved scientific calculator. Check the VCAA Physics page for the current exam specifications, weightings, past papers and examiners' reports. The examiners' reports are the single best source of the mistakes that lose marks.

The Exam technique page covers command words, working, significant figures and how to write explanations that earn full marks.

Sources and how this guide was written

The topics follow the key knowledge in the VCAA VCE Physics Study Design 2024–2027, in the same order most Units 3 & 4 textbooks (including Edrolo) use. The explanations, diagrams, worked examples and practice questions were written for this guide. They are not copied from Edrolo, VCAA exams or any other publisher, so it is safe to share. Numerical answers use the values from the VCAA data sheet (for example \(g = 9.8\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) and \(h = 6.63\times10^{-34}\ \text{J s}\)).
