The force on a moving charge

Magnetic force on a moving charge
\[ F = qvB \quad (v \perp B) \qquad\qquad F = 0 \quad (v \parallel B) \]

\(q\) = charge (C), \(v\) = speed (m s⁻¹), \(B\) = magnetic flux density (T). A charge at rest feels no magnetic force at all.

The direction comes from the same right-hand rule as for currents, with the thumb along the velocity of a positive charge:

Fingers along \(B\), thumb along \(v\), palm gives \(F\) on a positive charge. For an electron (negative), the force is in the opposite direction: use the back of your hand, or point your thumb opposite to the electron's velocity.

Why the path is a circle

The magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity. So:

  • It changes the particle's direction, never its speed.
  • It does no work (force ⊥ displacement), so kinetic energy stays constant.
  • A constant-size force always perpendicular to the velocity is exactly the recipe for uniform circular motion. The magnetic force is the centripetal force.
Radius of the path (\(v \ll c\))
\[ qvB = \frac{mv^2}{r} \quad\Rightarrow\quad r = \frac{mv}{qB} \]

Faster or heavier particles make wider circles; stronger fields or bigger charges make tighter circles.

An electron moves at \(2.0\times10^7\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) to the right, perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of \(5.0\ \text{mT}\) directed into the page (as in the diagram). Find (a) the force on the electron, (b) the initial direction of the force and (c) the radius of its path.

(a) Size of the force:

\[ F = qvB = (1.60\times10^{-19})(2.0\times10^7)(5.0\times10^{-3}) = 1.6\times10^{-14}\ \text{N} \]

(b) Direction: for a positive charge, fingers into the page, thumb right: palm faces up (towards the top of the page). The electron is negative, so the force is down the page.

(c) Radius:

\[ r = \frac{mv}{qB} = \frac{(9.1\times10^{-31})(2.0\times10^7)}{(1.60\times10^{-19})(5.0\times10^{-3})} = 2.3\times10^{-2}\ \text{m} \]

(a) \(1.6\times10^{-14}\ \text{N}\) (b) towards the bottom of the page (c) 2.3 cm. Remember mT = \(10^{-3}\) T.

Because \(F \perp v\), a magnetic field does no work: it cannot change a particle's speed or kinetic energy. To accelerate a charged particle (increase its speed) you need an electric field. Magnetic fields only steer. Questions that ask “how much kinetic energy does the electron gain in the magnetic field?” have the answer zero.

Electrons are accelerated from rest through 500 V, then enter a uniform 2.0 mT magnetic field at right angles. Find the radius of their circular path.

Speed from the electric field (\(eV = \tfrac12 mv^2\)):

\[ v = \sqrt{\frac{2eV}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2(1.60\times10^{-19})(500)}{9.1\times10^{-31}}} = 1.33\times10^7\ \text{m s}^{-1} \]

Radius in the magnetic field:

\[ r = \frac{mv}{eB} = \frac{(9.1\times10^{-31})(1.33\times10^7)}{(1.60\times10^{-19})(2.0\times10^{-3})} = 3.8\times10^{-2}\ \text{m} \]

3.8 cm. This two-field combination (E to accelerate, B to bend) is exactly what a particle accelerator does.

Comparing particles

Using \(r = mv/(qB)\) in the same field:

  • Same speed: a proton's radius is \(m_p/m_e = 1835\) times an electron's, and it curves the other way.
  • Double the speed: double the radius.
  • Same kinetic energy: \(mv = \sqrt{2mE_k}\), so \(r \propto \sqrt{m}\): the proton's radius is \(\sqrt{1835} \approx 43\) times larger.

Particle accelerators and synchrotrons

VCE models a particle accelerator with two simple pieces of physics:

JobField usedPhysics
Speed particles up (increase \(E_k\))Uniform electric field across a gap\(\Delta E_k = qV\) for each gap crossed; \(F = qE\) along the direction of motion
Bend the path into a circleUniform magnetic field in bending magnets\(qvB = mv^2/r\), so \(r = mv/(qB)\); no change in speed

How a synchrotron works

  1. Electron gun: a heated cathode releases electrons, which are accelerated by a potential difference (\(E_k = eV\)).
  2. Linear accelerator (linac): a series of electric fields boosts the electrons to high energy in a straight line.
  3. Booster ring: the electrons travel around a ring, gaining energy from an electric field every lap. Bending magnets keep them on a circle. Because \(r = mv/(qB)\), the magnetic field must be increased in step with (synchronised to) the increasing speed to keep the radius fixed. That's where the name comes from.
  4. Storage ring: electrons circulate at constant energy. Bending magnets steer them; each time they are deflected they emit intense electromagnetic radiation (synchrotron radiation, from infrared to X-rays), which is directed down beamlines to experiments. Electric fields in radio-frequency cavities top up the energy they lose.

The Australian Synchrotron in Clayton, Melbourne, has a 216 m circumference storage ring holding 3 GeV electrons. Its X-ray beamlines are used for medical imaging, protein crystallography and materials science. At 3 GeV the electrons travel at 99.999999% of \(c\), so real synchrotron design uses relativistic momentum; the VCE \(v \ll c\) model is a simplification that still explains why \(B\) must rise with energy.

Crossed electric and magnetic fields push a charge in opposite directions. Only particles with \(qE = qvB\), i.e. \(v = E/B\), pass straight through undeflected. With \(E = 2.0\times10^4\ \text{V m}^{-1}\) and \(B = 0.010\ \text{T}\), only particles moving at \(2.0\times10^6\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) get through. Mass spectrometers use this before bending the beam to measure \(m\) from \(r\).

  2. left
  3. top of the page
  4. bottom of the page

In a synchrotron, the magnetic field in the booster ring is increased as the electrons speed up. This is because:

  1. a stronger magnetic field increases the electrons' speed
  2. the electrons' charge increases as they speed up
  3. a larger field is needed to keep faster electrons on the same radius
  4. the magnetic field provides the energy to produce synchrotron radiation

\(r = mv/(qB)\): for constant \(r\), \(B\) must increase in proportion to \(v\). Magnetic fields never add energy; electric fields do that.

A proton moving at \(1.0\times10^6\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) enters a 0.10 T field at right angles. Find the radius of its path.

\(r = \dfrac{mv}{qB} = \dfrac{(1.67\times10^{-27})(1.0\times10^6)}{(1.60\times10^{-19})(0.10)} = 0.10\ \text{m}\).

Protons with a kinetic energy of 5.0 MeV must be bent around a curve of radius 0.50 m. (a) Find the protons' speed (the non-relativistic formula is acceptable here). (b) Find the magnetic field needed.

(a) \(E_k = 5.0\times10^6 \times 1.60\times10^{-19} = 8.0\times10^{-13}\ \text{J}\). \(v = \sqrt{2E_k/m} = \sqrt{2(8.0\times10^{-13})/1.67\times10^{-27}} = 3.1\times10^7\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) (about 0.1c).

(b) \(B = \dfrac{mv}{qr} = \dfrac{(1.67\times10^{-27})(3.1\times10^7)}{(1.60\times10^{-19})(0.50)} = 0.65\ \text{T}\).

Describe how electric and magnetic fields are used in a synchrotron to (a) increase the energy of the electrons and (b) keep them moving in a circular path. Explain why a magnetic field alone could not be used to increase the electrons' energy.

(a) Electric fields (in the electron gun, linac and RF cavities) exert a force \(F = qE\) along the electrons' direction of motion, doing work \(W = qV\) and increasing their kinetic energy. [1]

(b) Bending magnets provide a magnetic force \(F = qvB\) perpendicular to the velocity, which acts as the centripetal force: \(qvB = mv^2/r\). [1]

As the electrons gain speed, \(B\) is increased so that \(r = mv/(qB)\) stays constant. [1]

A magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work and cannot change the electrons' speed or kinetic energy. [1]

Summary

  • \(F = qvB\) (\(v \perp B\)), zero when parallel; reverse the rule for electrons.
  • Magnetic force is centripetal: \(r = mv/(qB)\); speed and kinetic energy stay constant.
  • Accelerators: electric fields add energy (\(qV\)); magnetic fields bend the path. In a synchrotron, \(B\) rises with speed to keep \(r\) fixed.
