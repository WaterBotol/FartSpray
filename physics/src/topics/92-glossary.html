Definitions are written the way examiners like to see them. Click the topic link after each one for the full explanation.

A–D

Absorption spectrum
A continuous spectrum crossed by dark lines at wavelengths absorbed by atoms of a cool gas; the lines match the gas's emission lines. Spectra
Accuracy
How close a measurement is to the true value. Key science skills
Alternator
An AC generator: a coil rotating in a magnetic field connected to the external circuit through slip rings. Generators
Apparent weight
The normal force (or support force) on an object; what scales read. \(N = m(g + a)\) in a lift. Newton's laws
Apparent weightlessness
The sensation of zero weight when the normal force is zero because the person and their surroundings are in free fall. Gravity still acts. Satellites
Banked track
A curved road tilted towards the centre so the normal force provides some or all of the centripetal force. Circular motion
Centripetal acceleration
The acceleration towards the centre of a circle for an object in circular motion: \(a = v^2/r\). Circular motion
Coherent sources
Wave sources with the same frequency and a constant phase difference, needed for a stable interference pattern. Double slit
Conservation of momentum
In an isolated system (no net external force) the total momentum before an interaction equals the total momentum after. Momentum
Constructive interference
Superposition of waves arriving in phase (path difference \(n\lambda\)), giving maximum amplitude. Double slit
de Broglie wavelength
The wavelength associated with a moving particle: \(\lambda = h/p\). Matter waves
Design speed
The speed at which a vehicle can round a banked curve with no sideways friction: \(\tan\theta = v^2/(rg)\). Circular motion
Destructive interference
Superposition of waves arriving exactly out of phase (path difference \((n - \tfrac12)\lambda\)), giving minimum amplitude. Double slit
Diffraction
The spreading of waves through a gap or around an obstacle; significant when \(\lambda/w \gtrsim 1\). Diffraction
Dipole
A pair of equal and opposite sources: + and − charges, or N and S magnetic poles. Fields

E–K

Elastic collision
A collision in which total kinetic energy is conserved (as well as momentum). Momentum
Electric field strength
The force per unit positive charge at a point: \(E = F/q\) (N C⁻¹ or V m⁻¹). Electric fields
Electromagnetic induction
The production of an emf in a conductor by a changing magnetic flux. Flux
Electron-volt
The energy gained by an electron accelerated through 1 V: \(1.60\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}\). Electric fields
Emf (electromotive force)
The energy per unit charge supplied by a source such as a generator or cell, measured in volts. Flux
Emission spectrum
Bright lines at specific wavelengths emitted when excited atoms drop to lower energy levels. Spectra
Energy level
One of the discrete (quantised) energies an electron in an atom can have. Spectra
Field
A region of space in which an object experiences a non-contact force because of a property such as mass, charge or magnetism. Fields
Fringe spacing
The distance between adjacent bright (or dark) bands in an interference pattern: \(\Delta x = \lambda L/d\). Double slit
Geostationary satellite
A satellite orbiting above the equator, in the direction of Earth's rotation, with a 24-hour period, so it stays above one point on the surface. Satellites
Gravitational field strength
The gravitational force per kilogram at a point: \(g = F/m = GM/r^2\). Gravity
Ground state
The lowest energy level of an atom. Spectra
Impulse
The product of force and the time it acts, equal to the change in momentum: \(F\Delta t = \Delta p\). Momentum
Inelastic collision
A collision in which some kinetic energy is transformed into other forms (heat, sound, deformation). Momentum is still conserved. Momentum
Inertia
The tendency of an object to resist changes in its motion; depends on mass. Newton's laws
Inertial frame of reference
A frame of reference that is not accelerating (at rest or at constant velocity). Relativity
Ionisation energy
The energy needed to completely remove an electron from an atom in its ground state (13.6 eV for hydrogen). Spectra
Kepler's third law
For all satellites of the same central body, \(r^3/T^2 = GM/4\pi^2\) is constant. Satellites

L–P

Length contraction
The shortening of a moving object along its direction of motion, as measured by an observer who sees it moving: \(L = L_0/\gamma\). Time dilation
Lenz's law
The induced current flows in a direction such that its magnetic field opposes the change in flux that produced it. Lenz's law
Lorentz factor
\(\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}\), the factor by which time dilates and length contracts. Time dilation
Magnetic flux
The amount of magnetic field passing through an area: \(\Phi = B_\perp A\), in webers. Flux
Magnetic flux density
The strength of a magnetic field, \(B\), in tesla (T). Magnetic fields
Monopole
A single isolated source (a mass or a single charge). Magnetic monopoles have never been observed. Fields
Normal force
The contact force a surface exerts perpendicular to itself on an object pressing on it. Newton's laws
Path difference
The difference in distance travelled by waves from two sources to a point. Double slit
Peak-to-peak voltage
The difference between the maximum and minimum values of an AC voltage: \(V_{p\text{-}p} = 2V_p\). AC
Period
The time for one complete cycle or revolution: \(T = 1/f\). Circular motion
Photoelectric effect
The emission of electrons from a metal surface when light above a threshold frequency shines on it. Photoelectric
Photon
A discrete packet (quantum) of electromagnetic energy: \(E = hf\). Photoelectric
Polarisation
Restricting the oscillations of a transverse wave to one plane; evidence that light is a transverse wave. EM waves
Precision
How closely repeated measurements agree with each other. Key science skills
Proper length
The length of an object measured in the frame in which it is at rest. Time dilation
Proper time
The time between two events measured in the frame in which they occur at the same place. Time dilation

Q–Z

Quantised
Able to take only certain discrete values, not a continuous range. Spectra
Random error
Unpredictable variation that scatters measurements about the true value; reduced by repeating and averaging. Key science skills
Repeatability
Agreement between results obtained by the same experimenter, method and equipment over a short time. Key science skills
Reproducibility
Agreement between results obtained under changed conditions (different experimenter, method, equipment or location). Key science skills
Resolution
The smallest change an instrument can detect. Key science skills
Rest energy
The energy an object has because of its mass: \(E = mc^2\). Mass–energy
RMS (root-mean-square)
The DC value that would deliver the same average power to a resistor: \(V_{\text{rms}} = V_p/\sqrt2\). AC
Slip rings
Two complete rings connecting a rotating coil to the external circuit without reversing the connections; they give AC output. Generators
Solenoid
A long coil of wire; when carrying current it has a uniform field inside and a bar-magnet field outside. Magnetic fields
Split-ring commutator
A ring split into two halves that reverses the connections between a rotating coil and the external circuit every half turn. Motors
Stopping voltage
The reverse voltage that just stops the most energetic photoelectrons: \(eV_0 = E_{k\max}\). Photoelectric
Strain potential energy
Energy stored in a deformed elastic object: area under the F–x graph, \(\tfrac12 kx^2\) for an ideal spring. Springs
Synchrotron
A circular particle accelerator using electric fields to accelerate charged particles and magnetic fields (increased in step with their speed) to steer them. Particles
Systematic error
An error that shifts every measurement in the same direction by a consistent amount; not reduced by repeating. Key science skills
Tension
The pulling force exerted by a string, rope, cable or rod on the objects attached to it. Tension
Threshold frequency
The minimum frequency of light that can eject photoelectrons from a metal: \(f_0 = \phi/h\). Photoelectric
Time dilation
A moving clock is measured to run slow: \(t = \gamma t_0\). Time dilation
Torque
The turning effect of a force about an axis; in a motor it's maximum when the coil plane is parallel to the field. Motors
Transformer
A device that changes the size of an AC voltage by electromagnetic induction between two coils on a shared core. Transformers
Transverse wave
A wave whose oscillations are perpendicular to its direction of travel, e.g. electromagnetic waves. EM waves
Uncertainty
The range within which the true value of a measurement is expected to lie. Key science skills
Uniform field
A field with the same strength and direction at every point (e.g. between parallel plates). Fields
Validity
Whether an experiment actually tests what it claims to (a fair test measuring the intended quantity). Key science skills
Wave–particle duality
Light and matter both show wave-like and particle-like properties, depending on the experiment. Duality
Work
Energy transferred when a force moves an object in the direction of the force: \(W = Fs\). Work & energy
Work function
The minimum energy needed to remove an electron from the surface of a metal. Photoelectric
