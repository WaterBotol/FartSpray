Gravity provides the centripetal force

A satellite in a circular orbit is in uniform circular motion. The only force on it is gravity, so gravity is the net (centripetal) force:

The orbit equation
\[ \frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{4\pi^2 mr}{T^2} \]

\(M\) = mass of the central body (planet or star), \(m\) = mass of the satellite, \(r\) = orbital radius from the centre of the central body.

The satellite's mass \(m\) cancels from every part. That gives three results worth knowing:

\[ v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} \qquad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}} \qquad a = g = \frac{GM}{r^2} \]

A 1 kg CubeSat and the 420 000 kg ISS at the same altitude move at the same speed with the same period. Only the central mass and the orbital radius matter.

  • Higher orbit → slower speed and longer period. The Moon takes 27.3 days; the ISS takes 92 minutes.
  • The satellite's centripetal acceleration equals the gravitational field strength at its orbit.

Kepler's third law

Rearranging the orbit equation shows that \(r^3/T^2\) is the same for every satellite of the same central body:

Kepler's third law
\[ \frac{r^3}{T^2} = \frac{GM}{4\pi^2} = \text{constant} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \frac{r_1^3}{T_1^2} = \frac{r_2^3}{T_2^2} \]

A graph of \(T^2\) against \(r^3\) is a straight line through the origin with gradient \(4\pi^2/GM\). This is how the mass of a planet is found from its moons.

Satellite B orbits a planet at four times the orbital radius of satellite A. If A's period is 2.0 hours, what is B's period?

\[ \frac{T_B^2}{T_A^2} = \frac{r_B^3}{r_A^3} = 4^3 = 64 \Rightarrow \frac{T_B}{T_A} = 8 \]

16 hours. Four times the radius, \(4^{3/2} = 8\) times the period.

Low Earth orbit: the ISS

The ISS orbits 400 km above Earth's surface. Find its orbital speed and period.

Orbital radius: \(r = 6.37\times10^6 + 4.00\times10^5 = 6.77\times10^6\ \text{m}\).

Speed:

\[ v = \sqrt{\frac{GM_E}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{(6.67\times10^{-11})(5.98\times10^{24})}{6.77\times10^6}} = 7.68\times10^3\ \text{m s}^{-1} \]

Period:

\[ T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi(6.77\times10^6)}{7676} = 5.54\times10^3\ \text{s} = 92\ \text{min} \]

About 7.7 km s⁻¹ (27 600 km h⁻¹) and 92 minutes. Astronauts see about 16 sunrises a day.

Geostationary satellites

A geostationary satellite stays above the same point on Earth's surface, so ground antennas can point at it permanently (satellite TV, weather images, communications). It must:

  • have a period of 24 hours (one Earth rotation);
  • orbit directly above the equator;
  • travel in the same direction as Earth rotates (west to east).

Calculate the orbital radius and altitude of a geostationary satellite, and its orbital speed.

Period in seconds: \(T = 24 \times 3600 = 86\,400\ \text{s}\).

Rearrange Kepler's third law for \(r\):

\[ r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{GM_ET^2}{4\pi^2}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{(6.67\times10^{-11})(5.98\times10^{24})(86\,400)^2}{4\pi^2}} = 4.23\times10^7\ \text{m} \]

Altitude: \(4.23\times10^7 - 6.37\times10^6 = 3.59\times10^7\ \text{m}\) (about 35 900 km).

Speed: \(v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T} = \dfrac{2\pi(4.23\times10^7)}{86\,400} = 3.07\times10^3\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).

\(r = 4.23\times10^7\ \text{m}\); altitude \(3.59\times10^7\ \text{m}\); \(v = 3.1\ \text{km s}^{-1}\). Every geostationary satellite, whatever its mass, sits at this one radius.

A geostationary satellite moves at over 3 km s⁻¹. It only appears stationary from the ground because it keeps pace with Earth's rotation. It is accelerating towards Earth's centre the whole time. A satellite with a 24 h period that is not above the equator is geosynchronous but not geostationary.

GPS satellites

The Global Positioning System uses about 30 satellites at an altitude of about 20 200 km with periods of about 12 hours, in several tilted orbital planes so that at least four are visible from anywhere on Earth. A receiver works out its position from the timing of signals from several satellites. They are not geostationary.

Show that a satellite with a 12.0 hour period orbits at an altitude of about \(2.0\times10^7\ \text{m}\).

\(T = 12.0 \times 3600 = 43\,200\ \text{s}\).

\[ r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{GM_ET^2}{4\pi^2}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{(3.99\times10^{14})(43\,200)^2}{4\pi^2}} = 2.66\times10^7\ \text{m} \]

Altitude \(= 2.66\times10^7 - 6.37\times10^6 = 2.02\times10^7\ \text{m}\) ✓. Shortcut: halving \(T\) divides \(r\) by \(2^{2/3} = 1.59\): \(4.23\times10^7/1.59 = 2.66\times10^7\ \text{m}\).

Finding the mass of a planet or star

The Moon orbits Earth with a radius of \(3.84\times10^8\ \text{m}\) and a period of 27.3 days. Calculate the mass of Earth.

Period in seconds: \(T = 27.3 \times 86\,400 = 2.36\times10^6\ \text{s}\).

Rearrange for \(M\):

\[ M = \frac{4\pi^2 r^3}{GT^2} = \frac{4\pi^2(3.84\times10^8)^3}{(6.67\times10^{-11})(2.36\times10^6)^2} = 6.0\times10^{24}\ \text{kg} \]

\(6.0\times10^{24}\ \text{kg}\), close to the accepted \(5.98\times10^{24}\ \text{kg}\). The Moon's own mass cancels, so you can't find the Moon's mass this way.

Apparent weightlessness in orbit

An astronaut in the ISS has a true weight of \(F_g = mg\) with \(g = 8.7\ \text{N kg}^{-1}\). Yet they float. Why?

  • The astronaut and the station are both in free fall: both accelerate towards Earth at exactly \(g\).
  • For the astronaut: \(F_{\text{net}} = mg - N = ma = mg\), so \(N = 0\).
  • With no normal force, they experience apparent weightlessness. Bathroom scales would read zero.

Astronauts are not weightless because they are “outside Earth's gravity”, “in a vacuum”, or because “gravity is balanced by centrifugal force”. Gravity is the unbalanced, net force on them. Write: “the normal force is zero because the astronaut and spacecraft are both in free fall with acceleration \(g\)”.

Changing orbits (qualitative)

Moving a satellite to a higher orbit needs energy from its rockets. In the higher orbit it moves more slowly (less kinetic energy), but its gravitational potential energy has increased by more than the kinetic energy decreased, so its total energy is higher. A satellite in low orbit that loses energy to atmospheric drag drops to a lower orbit and actually speeds up.

Practice

Two satellites orbit Earth at the same altitude. Satellite P has twice the mass of satellite Q. Compared with Q, P has:

  1. twice the orbital speed
  2. half the orbital period
  3. the same orbital speed and period
  4. half the orbital speed

\(v = \sqrt{GM/r}\) and \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{r^3/GM}\) depend only on Earth's mass and the orbit radius. P does experience twice the gravitational force, but it also needs twice the centripetal force.

Which statement about a geostationary satellite is correct?

  1. It orbits above the equator with a period of 24 hours.
  2. It can orbit above any point on Earth as long as its period is 24 hours.
  3. It has zero velocity relative to the centre of Earth.
  4. It has zero acceleration.

To stay above one spot it must orbit the equator in the same direction as Earth's rotation with a 24 h period. It is moving at about 3.1 km s⁻¹ and accelerating centripetally.

An astronaut floats inside a space station in low Earth orbit. The best explanation is that:

  1. there is no gravity at that altitude
  2. the gravitational force is balanced by a centrifugal force
  3. the station shields the astronaut from gravity
  4. the astronaut and station are both in free fall, so the normal force is zero

At 400 km, \(g\) is still 8.7 N kg⁻¹. Everything falls together, so nothing pushes on the astronaut: apparent weightlessness.

A satellite moves from a circular orbit of radius \(r\) to one of radius \(2r\). Its orbital speed changes by a factor of:

  1. 2
  2. \(1/\sqrt{2}\)
  3. \(1/2\)
  4. \(\sqrt{2}\)

\(v \propto 1/\sqrt{r}\), so doubling \(r\) multiplies \(v\) by \(1/\sqrt2 \approx 0.71\).

A student plots \(T^2\) (vertical axis) against \(r^3\) (horizontal axis) for the moons of Jupiter. The gradient of the line equals:

  1. \(GM/4\pi^2\)
  2. \(4\pi^2 GM\)
  3. \(4\pi^2/GM\)
  4. \(\sqrt{GM}\)

\(T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}r^3\): the gradient of \(T^2\) vs \(r^3\) is \(4\pi^2/GM\). If the axes were swapped the gradient would be \(GM/4\pi^2\), so read the axes carefully.

A satellite orbits 2000 km above Earth's surface. Calculate (a) its orbital speed and (b) its period in minutes.

\(r = 6.37\times10^6 + 2.00\times10^6 = 8.37\times10^6\ \text{m}\).

(a) \(v = \sqrt{(3.99\times10^{14})/(8.37\times10^6)} = 6.90\times10^3\ \text{m s}^{-1}\).

(b) \(T = 2\pi r/v = 2\pi(8.37\times10^6)/6903 = 7.62\times10^3\ \text{s} = 127\ \text{min}\).

Phobos orbits Mars with a radius of \(9.38\times10^6\ \text{m}\) and a period of 7.65 hours. Calculate the mass of Mars.

\(T = 7.65 \times 3600 = 2.754\times10^4\ \text{s}\).

\(M = \dfrac{4\pi^2 r^3}{GT^2} = \dfrac{4\pi^2(9.38\times10^6)^3}{(6.67\times10^{-11})(2.754\times10^4)^2} = 6.4\times10^{23}\ \text{kg}\).

Io orbits Jupiter at \(4.22\times10^8\ \text{m}\) with a period of 1.77 days. Callisto orbits at \(1.88\times10^9\ \text{m}\). (a) Use Kepler's third law to predict Callisto's period. (b) Calculate the mass of Jupiter.

(a) \(T_C = T_I\left(\dfrac{r_C}{r_I}\right)^{3/2} = 1.77 \times \left(\dfrac{1.88\times10^9}{4.22\times10^8}\right)^{1.5} = 1.77 \times 9.40 = 16.6\ \text{days}\) (actual: 16.7 days).

(b) \(T_I = 1.77 \times 86\,400 = 1.529\times10^5\ \text{s}\); \(M = \dfrac{4\pi^2(4.22\times10^8)^3}{(6.67\times10^{-11})(1.529\times10^5)^2} = 1.9\times10^{27}\ \text{kg}\).

Explain why all geostationary satellites must be at the same altitude, regardless of their mass.

For a circular orbit, gravity provides the centripetal force: \(\dfrac{GMm}{r^2} = \dfrac{4\pi^2 mr}{T^2}\). [1]

The satellite mass \(m\) cancels, giving \(r^3 = \dfrac{GMT^2}{4\pi^2}\): radius depends only on Earth's mass and the period. [1]

All geostationary satellites have the same period (24 h), so they must all have the same orbital radius, \(4.23\times10^7\ \text{m}\) (altitude \(3.59\times10^7\ \text{m}\)). [1]

Summary

  • \(GMm/r^2 = mv^2/r = 4\pi^2mr/T^2\); satellite mass cancels.
  • \(v = \sqrt{GM/r}\), \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{r^3/GM}\), \(r^3/T^2 = GM/4\pi^2\).
  • Geostationary: 24 h, equatorial, eastward, \(r = 4.23\times10^7\ \text{m}\). GPS: about 12 h, 20 200 km.
  • Floating astronauts: free fall, \(N = 0\), gravity still acts.
